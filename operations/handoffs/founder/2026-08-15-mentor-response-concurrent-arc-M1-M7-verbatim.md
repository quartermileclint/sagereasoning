**M5 — The boulesis/sufficiency distinction.** The distinction holds and the build-blocker stands until the examination is complete. Here is the examination. Boulesis is rational wish — the eupatheic replacement for epithumia (craving). It is directed at genuine goods, held with the understanding that the outcome is not fully up to us, and free from the excessive impulse that defines passion. Sufficiency, as the ATRF uses it, asks whether the agent's reasoning reaches the threshold required for a particular action to be warranted. The question the build-blocker is holding: are these the same distinction or different ones? They are related but not identical. Boulesis is a motivational state — the agent wishes for something genuinely good, without craving it. Sufficiency is an epistemic threshold — the agent's reasoning has reached the point where action is warranted. An agent can have boulesis without having sufficient reasoning to act — they wish for the right thing but have not yet examined the situation adequately. An agent can have sufficient reasoning without boulesis — they have examined the situation correctly but are acting from habit or convention rather than from rational wish. The ATRF's sufficiency-examination trigger is therefore not a test of boulesis. It is a test of whether the reasoning that precedes action meets the threshold for a katorthoma rather than a mere kathekon. The distinction the build-blocker is protecting: do not conflate the motivational state (boulesis) with the epistemic threshold (sufficiency). They are separate conditions. Both are required for a right action. Neither substitutes for the other. The ruling: the build-blocker is released. The ATRF scoping session may proceed. The sufficiency-examination trigger should be designed as an epistemic threshold check, not a motivational state check. The two conditions — boulesis present, sufficiency reached — should be named as separate fields if the ATRF carries them, not collapsed into a single indicator.
