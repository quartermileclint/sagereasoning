## A real error caught mid-draft, and worth naming plainly

While drafting the R080 paragraph, an early version of the letter presented a two-part quote as
verbatim — *"'Does the flag exist in code' and 'is the flag on' are different questions"* — attributed
to R080 in quotation marks. **It is not in the extraction.** It was my own illustrative gloss on
R080's real, verified sentence (*"It wasn't — I did a presence check where a state check was
required"*), written in the moment as though it were R080's own continuation, and quote-marked
without being checked. The scripted verification pass caught it on the same grep sweep that verified
every other quote in the letter, before the letter was allowed to stand. **This is the letter's own
subject, committed while writing about it** — the same shape as Letter II's mid-session catch, and
the same shape as R080's own case: a plausible-sounding claim, produced fluently, presented with the
confidence a checked claim earns, that had not actually been checked at the moment it was written.
Fixed: the sentence now quotes only R080's real words and states the general point (a grep confirms a
name is written somewhere; it says nothing about whether the thing is switched on) in the letter's
own voice, not inside quotation marks.

**No claim is made that this pattern of self-catching is closing.** Two letters in a row have now
caught exactly one fabricated-or-misattributed quote apiece during the same verification pass that
was already mandatory for other reasons. That could mean the check works. It could also mean two
data points is not a trend, and asserting one would repeat this letter's own failure at one remove.
