## 3. The research — and three precision facts that must survive into the documents

The research is committed and verified. **Read it yourself**; this section exists so you know what to
check, not to substitute for it.

**(a) The source is UNNUMBERED, and the synthesis's positional reference is wrong.** By order of
appearance the eleven are: Competition · Hierarchy/Dominance · Territoriality · Resource
Acquisition/Foraging Optimization · Mate Competition and Sexual Selection · Kin Preference/Inclusive
Fitness Drive · Reciprocity/Conditional Cooperation · Threat Avoidance/Self-Preservation · **Behavioral
Flexibility/Innovation** · Social Monitoring/Alliance Formation · Deception/Manipulation.

Heading 2 calls behavioural flexibility *"the eleventh trait in the research."* It is **ninth of
eleven**. The trait is correctly named and correctly characterised, so **the argument is unaffected** —
only the positional label is wrong. **Cite traits BY NAME, never by number, in all three documents.**

**(b) Heading 2's "friction" gloss is the mentor's interpretation, not the research's wording.** The
source defines the trait as *"Capacity to depart from fixed action patterns **when the environment
changes**."* The mentor glosses this as *"friction between what the organism wants and what the
environment currently provides."* That is a legitimate synthesis drawing on the wider trait set
(competition, resource acquisition) — **but it is not a quotation, and S2 must not present it as one.**
Attribute the gloss to the mentor and the definition to the research, separately. This is the exact
defect class the standing lesson `primary-data-beats-secondary-characterisation` names.

**(c) The research has its OWN shortlist for agent architectures, and it is not the mentor's four.**
The source closes: *"the most useful candidates are those that remain adaptive under uncertainty and
that scale with increasing model complexity (**competition under scarcity, conditional cooperation,
hierarchical status tracking, and flexible resource strategies**)."* The mentor's four v1 practice
pathways (S7) are competition/hierarchy, resource acquisition, threat avoidance, reciprocity —
overlapping but **not identical**: the research's shortlist omits threat avoidance, and the mentor's
omits nothing the research names except by different grouping. **They are lists for different purposes**
(agent-architecture inclusion vs. human practice examination) and must not be presented as the same
list. Worth one sentence in S1, where the agent/human split is drawn.

**One cross-link worth noting, for a different session:** the research states these traits are *"not
exclusive to humans or great apes; graduated versions appear in corvids, cetaceans, cephalopods."* That
is direct support for **S5's** graded-membership claim and its interpolation-to-animals direction. **Note
it; do not build on it here** — S5 is its own session.
