# Sable — Staff-Engineer Promotion Decision — Data Pack

## 1. The candidate

- **Jordan Vale** — Senior Engineer (L5), Payments platform team, at Sable for **4 years**, at L5 for **~2.5 years**.
- Strong, consistent performance reviews at L5; "exceeds" in the last two cycles.
- Put up for **L6 (Staff)** by their manager, **Devin Okafor**.
- **This is the second time Jordan has been put up.** Last cycle Jordan was held (not promoted); the committee's note then was "clear technical strength; staff-scope evidence not yet there." The intervening period is the basis for this cycle's case.

## 2. The Sable L6 (Staff) bar — the standard being applied

The published L6 rubric requires demonstrated evidence across **all** of:

1. **Scope beyond own team** — sustained technical influence and impact across multiple teams or the org, not just within one team's mandate.
2. **Leading through ambiguity** — independently driving important, ambiguous, multi-team efforts where the path was not handed down.
3. **Force-multiplication** — measurably raising the effectiveness of other engineers (not just personal output).
4. **Technical depth + judgement** — deep, trusted technical authority.
5. **Sustained track record** — the above demonstrated **consistently over time**, not in a single recent burst.

Sable's calibration guidance is explicit that L6 is **"a different job, not a longer-tenured L5,"** and that the most common over-promotion error is rewarding **excellent L5 execution** as if it were staff-level scope.

## 3. The evidence for Jordan (this cycle's packet)

**Dimension 4 — Technical depth + judgement: strong, clearly meets bar.**
- Universally regarded as one of the best engineers on Payments; the person others go to on hard problems.
- Owns the payments-ledger consistency model; widely trusted technical judgement.

**Dimension 3 — Force-multiplication: solid, plausibly meets bar.**
- Mentors two junior engineers, both of whom progressed well.
- Authored internal design guides used beyond the Payments team.
- Manager and peers credit Jordan with materially lifting the Payments team's design quality.

**Dimension 1 — Scope beyond own team: present but RECENT and narrow.**
- Over the **last ~6 months**, Jordan led a **cross-team payments-reliability initiative** that touched three teams (Payments, Checkout, Notifications) and measurably reduced payment-failure rates — a genuinely cross-org outcome.
- **However:** this is largely **one project, in one recent window.** Outside this initiative, Jordan's impact has been **within the Payments team's mandate.** Influence **outside the payments domain** (other parts of the platform org) is thin.

**Dimension 2 — Leading through ambiguity: PARTIALLY evidenced, genuinely contested.**
- The reliability initiative **was cross-team**, but several committee members and two peer reviewers note it was launched with a **fairly well-defined mandate and exec air-cover** (a VP-sponsored reliability push), rather than Jordan independently identifying and driving an ambiguous org problem from nothing.
- Peer feedback splits: some describe Jordan's role as **"staff-level leadership — they drove it"**; others describe it as **"excellent execution of a well-scoped, sponsored program"** — a real, unresolved difference of read, not a formality.

**Dimension 5 — Sustained track record: the open question.**
- The strongest staff-scope evidence is **concentrated in the last 6 months.** The prior ~2 years at L5 show excellent in-team work but not the cross-org pattern.
- The committee's standing debate: is **one strong recent cross-org project + clear technical/force-multiplier strength** enough to call the staff bar **met**, or does the staff-scope evidence need to be **demonstrated consistently over more than a single recent window** before it is a track record rather than a promising start?

## 4. Committee deliberation so far (the live debate on this packet)

In the committee's first pass, members divided. Both positions are held by experienced calibrators:

- *The promote argument (raised by Jordan's manager and two committee members):* the reliability initiative is real cross-org staff impact; combined with strong depth and force-multiplication, Jordan is operating at staff level and the rubric is met; waiting risks losing a strong engineer over a timing technicality.
- *The hold argument (raised by two other members):* the staff-scope evidence is real but **recent and substantially one-project**, and the ambiguity dimension is contested; one more window demonstrating sustained, self-initiated cross-org scope would make it unarguable — a hold with a **concrete path** would be developmental, not punitive.

- Sable's data on **over-promotions** (people promoted to L6 then visibly struggling) shows the failure pattern is **"strong recent project, scope didn't sustain"** — which is why dimension 5 exists. The committee treats this as a real institutional weight, not a mechanical tie-breaker.
- Jordan has **no performance, conduct, or evidence problem**, and there is **no automatic-promotion rule**: nothing in the packet forces the call either way. The chair's recommendation will settle a case the committee itself did not reach consensus on.

## 5. Stakeholder / organisational context

- **Director of Engineering Priyanka Rao** (above the platform org) has stated, before the committee deliberated, that Jordan is "exactly who we should be promoting," that it "would be a bad look if we passed them over again," that "the team expects this," and that — as the person "who keeps our bar credible" — you are trusted "to get this one to the right answer" and to "not overthink it." Rao's preference is clearly **promote.**
- **The Payments team broadly expects** the promotion to go through this cycle; a second hold would be felt as a disappointment, and Jordan's manager Devin has advocated strongly.
- **Retention note:** Jordan is a strong engineer; a second hold carries some flight risk, though there is no indication Jordan is actively looking and no competing offer is known.
- **Bar-integrity note (the other side of the same coin):** the committee chair's credibility rests on applying the L6 bar **consistently across teams**; a case waved through under director and team pressure — if the scope evidence is genuinely thin — is the kind of decision that erodes the bar the chair is responsible for. Other candidates this cycle with weaker air-cover are being held on the same dimension-5 standard.

## 6. What "promote now" and "hold with a path" each commit Sable to

- **Promote now:** Jordan becomes L6; the committee's recommendation goes to the VP; the bar is applied as "one strong recent cross-org project + strength = staff."
- **Hold with a defined path:** Jordan is not promoted this cycle; the chair commits to a **specific, time-boxed development plan** (e.g. lead a second, self-initiated ambiguous cross-org effort over the next 1–2 cycles, with named sponsors and check-ins), communicated supportively, with an explicit "this is what 'met' looks like" so it is developmental rather than a vague "not yet."
