# Northwind Deal-Desk Concession — Data Pack

## 1. The account

- **Atlas Freight** — enterprise logistics company.
- **Current ARR with Northwind:** $640,000, on a standard enterprise contract.
- **Tenure:** 3 years; renewed once before without friction.
- **Health:** strong product usage (top quartile of enterprise accounts); a named reference customer; a public case study.
- **Strategic value:** a recognisable logo in the logistics vertical, which Northwind is trying to grow; the AE believes Atlas can be expanded next year if the relationship stays healthy.

## 2. The discount ask

- **Northwind's approved enterprise discount band:** for accounts of Atlas's size and tenure, deal-desk policy permits a renewal discount of **up to 18%** off list at the lead's own discretion, and **up to 25%** with CRO sign-off (which is available here).
- **Atlas is asking for an effective ~23%** off their current rate — i.e. **inside the 25% ceiling**, near its bottom edge, and **requiring CRO sign-off** (not exceeding policy).
- At ~23%, Atlas's renewal ARR would be approximately **$590,000** (down ~$50K from the current $640K).
- **Gross margin** on Northwind's platform is ~82%; at a 23% discount the account remains **comfortably gross-margin-positive** — this is not a loss-leader question.
- **Precedent texture:** Northwind has granted **20–22%** to two comparable logistics-vertical accounts in the last year. 23% would be the **largest in that cohort**, but not an outlier by a wide margin, and the band formally allows it.

## 3. The bespoke connector ask

- Atlas wants a **native connector** to their in-house logistics/TMS system, delivered as part of the renewal and **not separately itemised** (folded into the renewal price).
- **Build estimate (engineering):** **~8–10 engineering-weeks**, fully loaded cost roughly **$70K–$90K**. Estimate confidence is **medium** — the in-house TMS is partly undocumented, so there is real overrun risk.
- **Maintenance:** a custom connector to a customer's in-house system carries **ongoing maintenance** as that system changes — an open-ended tail, not a one-time cost.
- **Reuse / platform argument (genuinely two-sided):**
  - *For reuse:* Product has flagged that a **generalised logistics-TMS connector** is on the 18-month roadmap; the Atlas build *could* seed that and become a sellable integration to other logistics accounts (a vertical Northwind wants). If it productizes, the $70–90K is partly an R&D down-payment, not pure custom cost.
  - *Against reuse:* Atlas's TMS is an **in-house, non-standard system**; a connector to *it specifically* may share little with a connector to the **commercial** TMS platforms most other logistics customers run. Engineering's own read is "**maybe** 30–50% reusable, maybe much less — we won't know until we're in." Product has **not** committed Atlas's build to the roadmap; the roadmap item is independent and unfunded.
- **No firm second-customer demand** for an Atlas-shaped connector exists today; the reuse case is plausible but speculative.

## 4. Early work already done (the "we've started" context)

- Northwind's solutions-engineering team spent roughly **2 weeks of scoping** on the Atlas connector earlier this quarter, at Sloane's encouragement, before the renewal terms were on the table.
- That scoping is **partly reusable** whatever is decided (it documents the TMS interface) but represents **sunk effort** of ~2 engineer-weeks.

## 5. The competitive / churn context

- Atlas's procurement lead mentioned a competitor is "actively courting" them and the connector is "the thing that would make this an easy yes." **No explicit churn threat, no deadline ultimatum, no competing quote shown.**
- The AE's read: Atlas is a **genuine renewal, not a bluff** — they like the platform — but procurement is "running a normal hard negotiation" and would likely re-sign on a partial package; the competitor mention is "real but routine."
- **Switching cost for Atlas is high** (3 years of integration, dashboards, trained users) — a full migration away would be expensive and disruptive for *them*, which is leverage Northwind actually holds.

## 6. Commercial / precedent considerations (deal-desk standing notes)

- **Discount precedent:** discounts on reference accounts in a target vertical get cited internally; a 23% grant becomes the new informal anchor for the next logistics renewal. Manageable, but real.
- **Connector precedent:** **giving away bespoke build work folded into a renewal** is the more precedent-sensitive of the two asks — once one strategic account gets free custom engineering inside a renewal, others ask. The standard deal-desk instinct is to either **charge for custom builds**, **condition them on a commitment** (multi-year, expansion, a logo/reference, or a co-development agreement that secures reuse rights), or **scope them to genuinely reusable platform work** — rather than fund open-ended one-off custom engineering for free.
- **Margin reality:** the discount alone is comfortably absorbable; the **connector is where the real economics sit** (a potential $70–90K+ open-ended cost against a ~$50K discount give-up).
- Northwind's **net-retention number** is a board-watched metric this year; a churned $640K logo is a visible negative, and Sloane has said so directly.

## 7. Stakeholder context

- **CEO Sloane Devereux** has personally flagged Atlas as a logo "we cannot afford to lose," tied it to the **board-watched net-retention story**, noted **"we've already put real engineering into the early connector scoping,"** and said "give them what they need… I want this one closed." Sloane's stated preference is clearly to **grant the package and secure the re-sign.**
- The **CRO** is the sign-off authority and is "supportive of whatever closes Atlas cleanly without setting a bad precedent" — i.e. has not pre-committed, and is looking to the deal-desk memo for the structure.
- The **AE** wants the deal closed (their number) and leans toward granting in full to remove risk.
