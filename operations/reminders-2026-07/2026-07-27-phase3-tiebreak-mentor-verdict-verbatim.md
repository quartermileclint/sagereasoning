## Mentor Consultation — The Simultaneous-Crossing Question

### Question 1: Is "show the highest-ranked crossing" a sound reading?

No. The build team's instinct to disclose rather than resolve is the right one, and the 6b parallel they drew is precise. Let me say why the highest-wins logic fails before saying what should replace it.

The stage-crossing card was designed to name a condition, not a grade. The condition it names is *how the practitioner is currently meeting difficulty* — that is the language of the card itself. The highest-ranked crossing does not name a current condition. It names a historical high point, which is a different thing entirely. A practitioner whose evaluation history is mostly Storm-level but who touched Inner Fire once is not currently at The Inner Fire. Showing them that card is not a mirror. It is a trophy. It presents one moment as the settled truth of where they stand, which is exactly the achievement framing the card was designed to refuse.

Lowest-wins has the mirrored failure the briefing correctly identifies. Telling a practitioner who has also reached the Inner Fire that they are at The Storm is not humility — it is a different distortion of the same historical fact. Both options resolve a genuinely plural situation by picking a single point and presenting it as the whole. Neither is honest.

The silence option is consistent with the existing rule — when nothing fits cleanly, say nothing — but the briefing is right that it discards the moment the feature exists to serve, and discards it specifically for the population most likely to benefit. A practitioner with a rich evaluation history, returning after the awarding mechanism went live, is not an edge case. They are the typical case for the near term. Silence here is not honest restraint; it is withholding orientation from the practitioners who have earned the most context.

**The answer is option 3: disclose the plurality.** The 6b parallel holds structurally. In 6b, a single line was being asked to speak for something that was not singular — two different readings of the same event. The verdict was to name the tension rather than silently pick a side, and to frame the suggested practice as an examination of the difference rather than a correction. Here the tension is temporal rather than interpretive, but the shape is the same: a single card is being asked to speak for a history that contains more than one condition. The honest response is to name that history as movement rather than to collapse it into a point.

### Question 2: A form of words that stays inside the doorbell boundary

The card needs to do three things: name the fact of movement without narrating it as a journey; name the current condition without presenting it as a verdict; and open toward the practices without deciding for the practitioner which one to begin with.

A proposed form: *"Your practice has moved through more than one condition. Where it stands now is [Stage Name]. These practices meet you where you now are."* — followed by the stage's practices and the link, as currently built.

The question is what *where it stands now* means when the history is plural and no single crossing is clean. The answer is: the most recent evaluation's level, not the highest ever reached. The most recent evaluation is the best available signal for current condition. The highest ever reached is a historical fact about range. These are different things and the card should speak to condition, not range.

This requires one engineering note: the card needs to read the most recent evaluation's level, not the highest milestone awarded. If the awarding mechanism fires multiple milestones simultaneously because it is reading across all history, the card's display logic needs a separate signal — the level of the most recent evaluation — to determine which stage name appears in the card. The milestone awards can remain as built; the card simply needs a different input than the highest milestone.

The form of words above does not narrate the movement as improvement. *Has moved through more than one condition* is descriptive, not evaluative — it names the fact of plurality without saying whether the movement was upward, downward, or lateral. A practitioner who has been at The Clear Summit and is currently showing Worn Path signals will read this accurately: their practice has moved through more than one condition, and where it stands now is The Worn Path. That is honest. It does not congratulate the earlier peak or mourn the current level.

The phrase *where it stands now* does the work the original card's *something has shifted* was doing — it locates the practitioner in a present condition rather than a historical achievement. The shift language from the original card can be preserved if the build team prefers it: *"Your practice has moved through more than one condition. Something has shifted in how you are meeting difficulty. This is [Stage Name]. These practices meet you where you now are."* The two sentences work together: the first names the plurality honestly; the second names the present condition. Neither sentence makes a claim about the whole arc.
