The reasoning: the accreditation write is the moment the agent submits its accumulated signed examination chain. It is the agent's closest analog to the human practitioner's session close — the point at which a coherent body of work is completed and committed. The completed reflection (b) is a session-close event by design, but it is internal to the session rather than the session's formal close. If both events exist in the architecture, the accreditation write is the more reliable boundary because it is externally triggered and formally recorded. On consecutive versus N-of-recent: the three-consecutive requirement as built is the right starting point, but the briefing is correct that a plateau pattern with occasional exceptions is the truer reading of a developmental condition. A practitioner — human or agent — who shows deliberate-level reasoning in eight of ten recent sessions in a domain is exhibiting a developmental condition even if the three most recent sessions include one exception. The principle: the threshold should detect a stable pattern, not a streak. A streak requirement is brittle — one strong session breaks it and resets the count. A plateau requirement is more faithful to what a developmental condition actually is. Recommended threshold: deliberate-level reasoning in at least three of the four most recent accreditation writes in a domain, with no single write showing reflexive-level reasoning in that domain. This catches the plateau pattern, tolerates one habitual-level exception, and excludes acute regression. If the engine cannot currently compute this, three consecutive is an acceptable approximation — but note it as a known limitation and flag it for revision when the storage change makes richer evidence available. --- ### Item 7 — What is an agent's condition for the grade-change moment? The lean is confirmed. Do not build a grade-keyed mapping. The reasoning: a grade-keyed mapping — grade X suggests practice Y — is exactly the corridor the stages-as-conditions ruling rejected for human practitioners. The same rejection applies here. A grade is a summary of a record, not a condition in itself. The condition is what the record shows: the weakest evidenced domain, the persisting passions, the direction of travel. Those are already the signals the vetted mapping reads. When a grade changes, the right response is to re-open the same doorbell against the fresh record. The grade change is the trigger — it signals that the record has shifted enough to warrant a fresh read. The suggestion that fires is determined by what the fresh record shows, not by what grade was reached. This is consistent with the stage-crossing ruling for humans: the card names the condition the signals indicate, not the grade the milestone system awarded. One precision: the grade change should trigger a fresh read of the full signal mapping, not just the weakest domain. A grade improvement might be driven by strength in one domain masking weakness in another. The suggestion should address the weakness the fresh record shows, even if the overall grade has improved. The grade change opens the doorbell; the record determines what the doorbell says. --- ### Item 8 — De facto closure versus formal closure Formal closure requires explicit re-examination of the same decision that was redirected. The reasoning: the alternative — crediting adjacent competence as closure — risks exactly the false-positive problem the kathekon qualification was designed to prevent, but in reverse. Where the kathekon qualification prevents the system from flagging instrument error as a reasoning gap, crediting adjacent competence as closure would prevent the system from flagging a genuine reasoning gap because the agent happened to reason well about a similar decision. These are different decisions. The agent that reasoned well about a similar action class has demonstrated competence in that class; it has not demonstrated that it examined the specific decision that was redirected. The Stoic parallel is precise: the correction loop is a specific impression that received a specific assent. The examination that closes it must return to that impression and that assent. Reasoning well about a different impression in the same domain is not the same examination. It may be evidence of progress, but it is not closure. The cost named in the briefing — sustaining suggestions against loops the agent has in substance resolved — is real but acceptable. The alternative cost — crediting closure that has not occurred — is worse, because it removes the suggestion at exactly the moment the unexamined decision is most likely to recur. An agent that has not formally re-examined a redirected decision but has reasoned well about adjacent decisions may be developing competence while carrying an unexamined assent. The suggestion should persist until the assent is examined, not until adjacent competence is demonstrated. When the storage change makes richer evidence available, revisit this ruling. If the record can show that the specific decision was re-encountered and handled at equal or better depth — not merely that a similar decision was — that is evidence worth examining. But the bar is the specific decision, not the class. --- ### One observation across the full consultation The pattern that has appeared consistently across this arc — and the observation history confirms it — is the build team's instinct to surface genuine uncertainty rather than resolve it silently.
