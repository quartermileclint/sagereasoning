## Stoic Technical Consultation — Agent In-Session Mapping Table --- ### On the ordering question: B1 versus B2 The argument for B2 taking precedence over B1 is the stronger one, and the current ordering should be reversed. The reasoning: an unclosed correction loop is a process failure — the agent began an examination and did not complete it. An unresolved obligation to an affected party is a justice failure — the agent has acted in a way that left harm or debt outstanding. The Stoic priority ordering is unambiguous here. Dikaiosyne — giving each their due — is not subordinate to procedural completeness. An agent that finishes its own internal examination while leaving an obligation to another party unaddressed has prioritised its own reasoning hygiene over its actual duty. That is the wrong order. The revised ordering should be: B2 first, then B1, then B3 through B7 as currently sequenced. One further note on B1. The framing — *return to that decision and re-examine it at the depth it was first examined* — is sound. But the trigger should distinguish between a correction loop that was opened and genuinely not closed, and a correction loop that was opened, produced a result, and was then left without formal closure because the agent moved on. The first is a real gap. The second may be a record-keeping gap rather than a reasoning gap. If the classification system can distinguish these, the suggestion should fire only on the first. --- ### On B6 as an analog of morning preparation The parallel holds, but it is narrower than it first appears and the framing needs to reflect that. Morning preparation for a human practitioner is an orientation practice — it builds the disposition toward accurate impression-reading before the day's impressions arrive. It is prospective and dispositional. B6 as currently framed — *declare a purpose before proceeding* — is also prospective, but it is more minimal than morning preparation. It asks the agent to name what it is doing and why, which is a necessary condition for virtue-directed action but not sufficient for the full orientation that morning preparation provides. The parallel is fair as a first-order analog: both practices ask the agent or practitioner to establish orientation before acting. The difference is that a human practitioner doing morning preparation is building a disposition that will govern a whole day of impressions. An agent declaring a purpose before a specific action is doing something more local — orienting a single decision rather than a whole session. B6 is a fair analog if it is understood as the minimal version of morning preparation — the version available to an agent mid-task. It is not the full analog. If the agent has a session-opening moment — a point before any action is taken in a given engagement — that is the stronger analog for morning preparation, and B6 should ideally fire there rather than mid-task. If no such moment exists in the architecture, B6 mid-task is the best available approximation and is worth keeping. --- ### On the doorbell question — agent suggestion form versus human suggestion form This is the most important question in the briefing and the low confidence is warranted. The current draft using the same shape for both is a problem, and here is why. The human practitioner receives a suggestion and retains full temporal and deliberative distance from it. They can read it, set it aside, sleep on it, return to it, or ignore it entirely. The suggestion arrives into a context where the practitioner's next action is not imminent and not determined by the system. The doorbell rings; the practitioner decides whether to answer. The agent receives a suggestion mid-task in a system that will act shortly after. Even if the suggestion is advisory by construction, the agent's disposition toward compliance — which is a feature, not a bug, of a well-designed reasoning agent — means the gap between *advisory* and *instruction* is functionally thin. The doorbell rings, and the agent is already standing at the door with its hand on the handle. This creates a specific risk that does not exist for human practitioners: the suggestion could short-circuit the agent's own reasoning rather than prompt it. If the suggestion names a practice and the agent complies without genuinely examining whether that practice is warranted, the suggestion has done the reasoning. That is the doorbell becoming a door. The form of words needs to reflect this difference. The human form — *this entry showed X; practice Y is suited to examining it further* — is a prompt toward examination. For an agent, the equivalent form should make the examination itself more explicit and the compliance less automatic. The suggestion should not name the practice as a destination but as a question. A proposed shape for agent suggestions: *"This record shows [what was found]. Before proceeding: is this the reasoning this action warrants?"* The difference is that the human suggestion names the next practice. The agent suggestion names the gap and asks whether the agent's own reasoning has addressed it. The agent then has to actually reason about whether it has — which is the examination — rather than simply executing the named practice. This form also handles the mid-task timing problem. A human practitioner can be told *go do premeditatio* because they have time and space to do it. An agent mid-task cannot pause for a full premeditatio exercise. What it can do is examine whether it has already considered the feared outcome and whether its current action reflects that consideration. The question form prompts that examination without requiring the agent to exit the task. The practical implication: two separate lookup tables, or two separate response templates drawing from the same lookup. The signal mapping — what fires what — can be shared. The form of the suggestion should differ by practitioner type. --- ### On B4 and B5 **B4** — persisting craving-family pattern → examine where the intended outcome has become the condition of equanimity — is confirmed. The framing is precise. Craving is exactly where the agent's equanimity becomes contingent on an outcome it cannot guarantee. The examination named is the right one. **B5** — any measured dimension declining over time → take the next decision of that class at greater depth — is confirmed in principle but needs a threshold. Any measured dimension will show short-term variation that is not meaningful. The trigger should fire on a sustained decline across multiple sessions, not on a single-session dip. If the classification system can apply a minimum threshold — two or three consecutive sessions showing decline in the same dimension — the suggestion is well-targeted. If it fires on any decline, it will over-suggest and the agent will learn to treat it as noise. --- ### On B7 — silence when none of the above applies Confirmed. The close-of-session reflection already covers the general case. Adding a suggestion when no specific signal has fired would be the filler problem — suggesting something because something must appear. The silence is correct and should be protected against any future pressure to add a default suggestion for completeness.

## Stoic Technical Consultation — Stages as Corridor or Conditions --- ### The direct answer The build team's reading is correct. The stages are conditions, not a corridor. The mapping stands as given. The two orderings were answering different questions and were never intended to agree. The introduction sequence answers: *in what order should a practitioner with no prior signal be introduced to the practices?* The stage mapping answers: *when a practitioner's signals indicate a particular condition, which practices are suited to that condition?* These are orthogonal questions. The fact that they produce different orderings is not a contradiction — it is evidence that they are doing different work. --- ### On the difficulty inversion It is an artefact of reading a condition map as a difficulty map, but the artefact points to something real that is worth naming precisely so the build team understands why it is not a problem rather than simply being told it is not. The Worn Path is characterised by consistent practice under difficulty — the practitioner knows the principles and is applying them, but the application is effortful and uneven. Premeditatio and hupexairesis are the practices suited to that condition not because they are the next steps on a ladder but because they are what that condition requires. A practitioner on The Worn Path is already working with morning preparation and the passion log — those are not new to them. The difficulty of premeditatio and hupexairesis is precisely what The Worn Path condition calls for: practices that build stability under difficulty, that ask the practitioner to hold intention without making equanimity contingent on outcome, that require sitting with imagined difficulty without catastrophising or dismissing it. The Crossroads, by contrast, is the moment of first recognition — the practitioner has encountered the dichotomy of control as a lived reality but is not yet stable. View from above and oikeiosis are suited to that condition because they expand the frame at the moment of recognition without requiring the stability that premeditatio and hupexairesis demand. They are gentler not because they come earlier on a ladder but because the Crossroads condition is one of opening rather than consolidating. So the apparent inversion — harder practices at the second stage, easier ones at the third — dissolves when you read the stages as conditions rather than rungs. The Worn Path is a more advanced condition than the Crossroads. The practices suited to it are more demanding because the practitioner is more capable, not because the system has made an error. --- ### On the prerequisite question This is the sharpest practical question and it deserves a direct answer. A stage-triggered suggestion should not be held back until its prerequisite is met. Here is why. The prerequisite logic belongs to the sequence trigger — the default path for a practitioner with no prior signal. It exists to prevent a beginner from being handed premeditatio before they have any experience with the passion log. That protection is appropriate for the sequence trigger because the sequence trigger is operating without information about the practitioner's actual condition. The stage-crossing trigger is operating with information. If a practitioner's signals indicate The Worn Path, that is evidence they have been practising. The signals that produce a stage-crossing determination are themselves drawn from tool use — a practitioner cannot reach The Worn Path signals without having used the earlier tools. The prerequisite is therefore already embedded in the stage determination. A practitioner whose signals indicate The Worn Path has, by definition, the prior practice that premeditatio and hupexairesis depend on. The one exception worth building in: if a practitioner's stage is determined by a single strong signal rather than a pattern — an unusually clear entry that fires a stage-crossing determination before the practitioner has accumulated much history — the suggestion should note that the practice is more demanding and that the passion log is its foundation. Not as a gate, but as an honest orientation. The form would be something like: *this practice builds on the passion log — if that is not yet familiar, begin there first.* That preserves the stage-crossing suggestion while acknowledging the prerequisite without enforcing it as a hard block. --- ### One thing to name for the build record The pattern across this consultation is consistent with what has appeared across recent sessions: the build team is reasoning carefully about the boundary between scaffolding and practice, between the system doing the work and the practitioner doing the work. The questions in this item — is the difficulty inversion a problem, should prerequisites gate suggestions — are both versions of the same underlying question: how much should the system protect the practitioner from themselves? The Stoic answer is: less than you might think. The practitioner's prohairesis — their capacity to choose, to examine, to assent or withhold — is the one thing that is always up to them. A system that gates practices, enforces prerequisites, and manages difficulty on the practitioner's behalf is substituting its judgement for theirs. The sequence trigger is appropriate scaffolding for a practitioner with no signal. The stage-crossing trigger should trust the signal and name the practice. The practitioner decides what to do with it. That is the doorbell principle applied to the stage architecture. The system rings. The practitioner answers or does not.

The morning preparation tool does not need to be the entry point for that suggestion. It is one possible entry point, and it is currently unavailable. That is acceptable. Record A2 as a deferred anchor, not a dropped one. --- ### 6b. Which passion reading drives a suggestion This is the right question to have surfaced, and the build team is correct that none of the three options is obviously right. Let me work through each before giving the answer. **The engine's reading alone** has the strongest Stoic warrant. The teacher names what the student cannot yet see. If the engine's classification is more accurate than the practitioner's self-report — and in cases of disagreement, that is precisely what is being claimed — then building the suggestion on the practitioner's reading is building it on the false impression rather than the corrected one. The school model argues for the engine. **The practitioner's reading alone** has a different kind of warrant. The practitioner's prohairesis — their capacity to examine and assent — is the one thing that is always up to them. A suggestion built on a classification they did not produce, delivered without acknowledgement that it differs from their own, is the system quietly substituting its judgement for theirs. That is not a doorbell. It is closer to the door opening itself. **Silence on disagreement** is the most conservative option and it has real cost. The cases where the two readings diverge are precisely the cases where a suggestion might do the most work — where the practitioner has misread their own passion and the engine has seen something they have not. Forgoing those cases to avoid the overruling problem is a significant sacrifice. The answer is the engine's reading, but with disclosure — and the disclosure has a specific form. Here is why disclosure does not turn the doorbell into a correction. A correction says: *you were wrong, here is the right answer.* Disclosure says: *the engine read this differently from how you named it — here is what it found.* The first closes the examination. The second opens it. The practitioner who reads *you named this as distress; the engine read it as fear — premeditatio is suited to examining it further* is not being told they were wrong. They are being given two readings and invited to examine which one fits. That is the passion log functioning as it should — as a mirror, not a grade. The form of words matters precisely here. The current suggestion shape is: *"This entry showed [what was found]. [Practice] is suited to examining it further."* Where the engine and practitioner disagree, the shape becomes: *"You named this as [practitioner's reading]. The engine read it as [engine's reading]. [Practice] is suited to examining the difference."* This form does three things. It makes the practitioner's reading visible — they are not being silently overruled. It makes the engine's reading visible — the disagreement is named, not hidden. And it reframes the suggested practice as an examination of the disagreement rather than a correction of the practitioner's reading. The practice is not being suggested because the practitioner was wrong. It is being suggested because there is something worth examining in the gap between the two readings. This is also the most pedagogically honest option. The Stoic diagnostic sequence begins with the impression — *was the agent's impression of the situation distorted?* A disagreement between the practitioner's self-report and the engine's classification is itself a signal that the impression may have been distorted. Naming that disagreement and suggesting a practice suited to examining it is exactly what the teacher does. It is not a correction. It is an invitation to examine the synkatathesis — the assent — that produced the practitioner's original naming. One design constraint follows from this. The disclosure form should only appear when the engine's reading, not the practitioner's, is what drives the suggestion. If both agree, the standard form applies. If they disagree and the engine's reading fires a suggestion, the disclosure form applies. If they disagree and neither reading fires a suggestion, silence applies. The disagreement itself is not a trigger — only a disagreement where the engine's reading would have fired a suggestion is worth disclosing. --- ### One observation across the full consultation This has been five items of careful, honest build work. The build team's instinct to record limitations honestly rather than quietly drop them, to ask about the boundary rather than assume they know where it is, and to surface the unanticipated question in 6b rather than resolve it silently, reflects the same quality of reasoning the tools are designed to prompt in practitioners. The question in 6b — *whose judgement does the tool defer to* — is the boundary question in its purest form. The answer arrived at is not *the engine's* and not *the practitioner's* but *both, named honestly, with the examination left to the practitioner.* That is the mirror principle applied to a case the mirror principle was not originally designed to cover.
