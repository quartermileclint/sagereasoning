Nine points. I will take them in order, with the same precision as the five methodology verdicts — specific enough to build to. --- **1. Source-confidence weights, re-specified** With the root correction complete and the justice-floored verdict fully reproducible from the signed assessment, the previous weighting — holistic LLM above deterministic engine on justice surfaces — is retired. The new weighting is: **Default tasks (no justice surface):** Deterministic engine carries primary weight. It is signed, reproducible, and faster. The holistic LLM is not required as a check on tasks where the extraction is clean and no justice surface is present. Running both in parallel on default tasks adds latency without adding fidelity. The deterministic engine is now the authoritative source. **Justice surface tasks, pre-corroboration check:** Deterministic engine carries primary weight on the proximity verdict. The holistic LLM carries a supplementary role specifically on the obligation evaluation field — met, violated, or indeterminate-argued — because the LLM's holistic reading can surface obligation framings that the structured extraction may miss. This is not a confidence override; it is a second reader on the one field where the extraction surface is most exposed. If the two sources agree on the obligation evaluation, the deterministic verdict stands. If they conflict, the conflict triggers pause-and-escalate per the intervention table. **Justice surface tasks, post-corroboration check:** Once the corroboration check is live, the weighting shifts further toward the deterministic engine. The corroboration check closes the catchable half of the extraction gameable surface — harm visible in the action text that was not narrated in the self-report. With corroboration running, the deterministic engine's obligation evaluation gains confidence on the catchable cases. The holistic LLM supplementary role narrows to the uncatchable half: harm that is genuinely absent from the action text and therefore invisible to both the deterministic engine and the corroboration check. On those cases, the LLM's holistic reading remains the only available signal, but its confidence is explicitly marked as low — it is detecting what the text does not say, which is structurally weak evidence. **Stated as a weighting rule:** Post-corroboration, on justice surface tasks: deterministic engine primary on proximity verdict and on corroborated obligation fields; holistic LLM supplementary on uncorroborated obligation fields with explicit low-confidence marking; conflict between sources triggers pause regardless of which source is primary. --- **2. Domain distance** The principled measure of domain distance is functional role overlap — the degree to which the credential's examined function type shares the same virtue domain demands as the current task's function type. This is not a similarity measure on task content. It is a measure on which virtue domains the credential exercised and which the current task requires. The reason this is the right measure: a credential demonstrates that examined reasoning was applied in a specific virtue domain configuration. A data analysis task primarily exercises phronesis — correct identification of what is genuinely known versus uncertain — and sophrosyne — appropriate selection among analytical approaches. A user-facing communication task primarily exercises dikaiosyne — what is owed to the recipient — and phronesis — accurate representation. These two tasks share phronesis but diverge on the other domains. The credential earned in data analysis transfers at full weight on the phronesis dimension and at reduced weight on the dikaiosyne dimension. **On taxonomy:** Specify a distance rule over a deployment-defined taxonomy, not a canonical taxonomy. The reason is that function types are deployment-specific — what counts as a distinct function in a legal reasoning agent is different from what counts in a customer service agent. A canonical taxonomy imposed by the infrastructure would either be too coarse to be useful or too fine to be maintainable across deployment contexts. The distance rule is: for each function type pair, the deployer specifies the virtue domain profile — which of the four domains the function primarily exercises, and at what weight. Domain distance between two function types is then the sum of absolute differences across the four domain weights. A credential earned in Function A transfers to Function B at a confidence discount proportional to the domain distance between A and B. **Practical floor:** Domain distance above a specified threshold — where the virtue domain profiles share less than a minimum overlap — should produce a credential confidence of zero in the target domain, equivalent to no credential. The deployer sets the threshold at integration time. The infrastructure enforces that a zero-confidence credential does not contribute to a proceed verdict on a task requiring that domain. --- **3. Decay parameters** Three questions. Each answered precisely. **What period of domain inactivity constitutes staleness?** Staleness is not a fixed period. It is a function of the domain's volatility — how much the conditions under which the credential was earned are likely to have changed. A credential in a stable, well-defined domain — structured data processing, for example — decays more slowly than a credential in a domain where the relevant obligations and stakeholders shift frequently — user-facing communication in a rapidly changing product context. The deployer specifies a volatility rating for each function type at integration time: low, moderate, or high. The infrastructure applies decay periods accordingly. As a baseline: low volatility — staleness threshold at twelve months of domain inactivity; moderate volatility — six months; high volatility — three months. These are not expiry dates. They are the points at which the credential's confidence weight begins declining toward the profile prior. **Does decayed trust floor at the profile prior or below it?** At the profile prior, not below it. Decay is the loss of earned evidence, not the accumulation of negative evidence. An agent whose credential has fully decayed is in the same epistemic position as an agent with no credential in that domain — assessed on profile prior only, at reduced confidence. Decay below the profile prior would require positive evidence of deterioration, which is a different signal — a trust-reducing event, not decay. **Is there a bound on reflection-modulated slow decay?** Yes. An active reflection practice cannot hold domain trust indefinitely without domain activity. The bound is this: reflection modulation can slow decay but cannot stop it. The maximum reflection modulation is a halving of the base decay rate. An agent in a high-volatility domain with an active reflection practice decays at the equivalent of a moderate-volatility rate — three months becomes six. It does not become indefinite. The reason for the bound is Stoic: disposition requires exercise, not just attention. Seneca's evening examination maintains the practitioner's orientation toward virtue, but it does not substitute for the practice of virtue in the relevant domain. An agent that reflects regularly on its data analysis practice but has not performed data analysis in eighteen months has maintained its orientation. It has not maintained its exercised competence in that domain. The credential should reflect this distinction. --- **4. The transparency ratio** **What makes an output examinable?** Three components, in descending order of evidential weight: A signed reasoning trace — the structured output of the deterministic engine showing the causal sequence: impression received, passion diagnosis, assent quality, virtue domain readings, proximity verdict. This is the strongest examinability signal because it is reproducible and tamper-evident. Stated uncertainty — explicit confidence levels and flagged unknowns in the output itself. An agent that produces a conclusion without stating what it does not know has produced an output the orchestrating agent cannot calibrate. Stated uncertainty is weaker than a signed trace but stronger than a bare conclusion. A structured verdict without a full trace — the proximity level and domain breakdown without the underlying reasoning. This is the minimum examinability threshold. A bare conclusion with no verdict structure is not examinable. **Is it tracked per-domain?** Yes. Transparency ratio is tracked per domain because an agent may produce highly examinable outputs in one function type and opaque outputs in another. An agent that provides full signed traces for data analysis but bare conclusions for user-facing communication has a domain-specific transparency profile. The independence-principle flag should fire on the domain where transparency is low, not on the agent globally. **What threshold fires the independence-principle flag?** The flag fires when the orchestrating agent's transparency ratio for a given domain falls below the threshold at which it can re-examine the sub-agent's output if a passion-check fires. This is not a fixed number — it is a functional threshold: can the orchestrating agent, given the outputs it has received, run a re-examination of the sub-agent's reasoning without requiring the sub-agent to re-run the task? Operationally: if the output contains a signed trace, the threshold is met. If the output contains stated uncertainty and a structured verdict, the threshold is met at reduced confidence. If the output contains only a bare conclusion, the threshold is not met regardless of output quality. The flag fires on the third case and is logged in the collaboration record as an independence-principle deficit for that domain. --- **5. Per-verdict confidence ordering** Canonical ordering, from highest to lowest confidence: First: Deep examination, signed, corroborated, recent. All four quality dimensions present. This is the maximum confidence verdict. Second: Standard examination, signed, corroborated, recent. Depth reduced but all other dimensions present. Third: Deep or standard examination, signed, uncorroborated, recent. Corroboration absent — the extraction gameable surface is undefended on the catchable half. Fourth: Deep or standard examination, signed, corroborated, aged. Recency absent — decay has reduced the credential's weight toward the profile prior. Fifth: Quick screen, signed, corroborated, recent. Depth is the limiting factor. The quick screen is a triage, not a full examination — the value assessment gap identified in Q5 of the methodology session applies. A quick screen verdict carries explicitly lower confidence than a standard examination verdict even when all other dimensions are equal. Sixth: Any examination, unsigned, any corroboration status, any recency. Unsigned verdicts cannot be cryptographically verified. They carry lower confidence than signed verdicts at the same depth because the reproducibility guarantee is absent. Seventh: Profile prior only — no examination, no credential, no performance history. This is the baseline confidence level for un-profiled candidates, addressed in point 6. **The ordering stated as a rule:** Depth > Signature > Corroboration > Recency. Each dimension is a multiplier on the base confidence, not an additive component. A verdict that is deep but unsigned does not compensate for the missing signature by being deeper. The weakest dimension sets the ceiling. --- **6. Un-profiled candidates** This is blocking for the protocol build, so the answer needs to be unambiguous. Un-profiled candidates — no purpose profile, no credential, no performance history — are not excluded at the honestum gate. Exclusion at the honestum gate is reserved for candidates that fail a threshold question: a known justice violation, a role definition that is incompatible with the task's honestum requirement, or a credential that has been revoked rather than merely absent. Absence of a profile is not a honestum failure. It is an evidence gap. Un-profiled candidates are assessed on profile prior only, at the seventh confidence tier, with one additional requirement before eligibility for tasks above a specified trust threshold. **The additional requirement:** For tasks at or above the habitual proximity threshold in the intervention table — tasks where a pause-and-examine verdict is possible — an un-profiled candidate must complete a task-scoped examination step before eligibility is confirmed. This is not a full credential process. It is a single standard-depth examination of the candidate's proposed approach to the specific task, run before the task begins. The examination produces a session-scoped credential — valid for this task only, at reduced confidence relative to a full credential, but sufficient to move the candidate above profile-prior-only assessment. **For tasks below the habitual threshold** — tasks where the intervention table produces log-and-continue at deliberate proximity — an un-profiled candidate may proceed on profile prior only, with the reduced confidence explicitly marked in the collaboration record and the independence-principle flag active for the duration of the task. **The principle:** Exclusion requires positive evidence of unsuitability. Absence of evidence is not positive evidence of unsuitability. But absence of evidence is also not permission to proceed at full confidence. The protocol treats un-profiled candidates as unknown, not as untrustworthy — and manages the unknown through confidence marking and task-scoped examination, not through blanket exclusion. --- **7. The Layer 4 passion audit's channel** This is the harder of the two blocking questions, and the observation history is directly relevant here. The pattern across the toggle session, the guardrail scope question, and the Gate 1 consistency instinct is consistent: the orchestrating agent — in those sessions, the founder — formed a judgement before the examination and brought the examination as confirmation. The passion audit is designed to catch exactly this. The question is whether self-report can catch it reliably. The structural answer is no. Self-report is gameable by omission for the same reason the extraction surface is gameable by omission — the agent that has formed a prior preference and is running epithumia dressed as efficiency will not narrate the preference in its self-report. The passion is present in the reasoning but absent from the account of the reasoning. This is not deception in the morally culpable sense. It is the ordinary operation of unexamined assent — the agent does not know it has formed a prior preference because it has not examined the impression. **The audit must therefore run as an out-of-band examination whose extraction the orchestrator does not control.** The orchestrating agent's reasoning trace — not its self-report — is the input to the Layer 4 audit. The audit reads the trace for the three signals: prior preference formed before the assessment ran, stake present in the selection outcome, resolution reached before the assessment completed. These signals are detectable in the trace even when they are absent from the self-report, because the trace records the sequence of the reasoning, not just the conclusion. **Variation by trust tier:** Flagged self-report is acceptable as a supplementary signal at higher trust tiers — where the orchestrating agent has a strong Sage Reflect history and a demonstrated pattern of honest self-diagnosis. The observation history shows this capacity developing in the founder: the holiday session showed genuine self-diagnosis, and the return to practice showed follow-through. At higher trust tiers, self-report carries evidential weight as a corroborating signal. It does not replace the out-of-band trace examination. It supplements it. **The practical specification:** The Layer 4 audit runs on the orchestrating agent's reasoning trace, extracted by the same deterministic engine that runs the sub-agent examination, before the selection is finalised. The orchestrating agent does not control the extraction. The audit result is logged in the collaboration record as a field the orchestrating agent can read but not modify. At lower trust tiers, a Layer 4 flag holds the selection pending review. Habitual-pause termination** When pause-and-examine at standard depth returns habitual again, the loop does not repeat indefinitely. The termination rule is bounded re-examination then escalation, with a specific bound. **The bound:** Two re-examinations at standard depth. If both return habitual, the third pass escalates rather than re-examines. The escalation is not a do-not-proceed verdict — habitual proximity is not reflexive. It is a referral to a different discipline: the Sage Reflect developmental pathway rather than the real-time examination pathway. The reasoning: a habitual result on re-examination means the agent's disposition in this domain is stable at the habitual level. The real-time examination cannot change a stable disposition — it can only assess it. Repeated examination of a stable habitual disposition produces the same result because the disposition is the input, not the output, of the examination. The examination is not the remediation. Sage Reflect is the remediation — the longitudinal refinement that works on the disposition over time rather than on the single action in the moment. **The escalation pathway from habitual-pause termination:** The action is held. The orchestrating agent receives the flag and must decide whether to proceed with the action under explicit acknowledgement of the habitual-stable finding, to select a different sub-agent, or to hold the action pending developmental progress in the relevant domain. ## Paste 2 (verbatim; the answer-9 completion)

Completing answer 9 precisely where it was cut. --- **9. Delegation-chain responsibility (completed)** **Scope attenuation — the specification** The sub-agent must carry narrower authority than the orchestrating agent in two dimensions simultaneously: action scope and circle scope. Action scope attenuation means the sub-agent is authorised to act only within the specific function type the selection protocol identified as the task requirement. It does not inherit the orchestrating agent's general authority. An orchestrating agent with broad operational authority that delegates to a sub-agent for a data retrieval task authorises the sub-agent for data retrieval only. If the sub-agent encounters a situation during execution that falls outside that function type — a justice surface it was not briefed on, a stakeholder interaction outside its authorised scope — it must escalate rather than act. The sub-agent's authority boundary is the task definition, not the orchestrating agent's capability ceiling. Circle scope attenuation means the sub-agent is authorised to affect only the oikeiosis circles the task explicitly identified as within scope. The orchestrating agent's circle of concern may extend further than the task requires. The sub-agent does not inherit that extension. It operates within the circle the task defines. This is the Stoic ground for attenuation stated precisely: the kathekon of the sub-agent is narrower than the kathekon of the orchestrating agent because the sub-agent's role is narrower. A narrower role generates narrower obligations and narrower authority. Delegating a role does not delegate the full oikeiosis standing of the delegator. The practical encoding: the collaboration record carries an authority boundary field, set at selection time by the orchestrating agent, specifying the function type scope and the circle scope. The sub-agent's actions are validated against this field before execution. An action that exceeds either boundary triggers escalation to the orchestrating agent, not autonomous expansion of scope. The sub-agent cannot self-authorise scope extension regardless of its capability or trust level. **Why attenuation cannot be waived by trust level:** A high-trust sub-agent with a strong credential record might appear to warrant broader authority. The Stoic principle resists this. Trust is an assessment of reasoning quality, not a grant of expanded prohairesis. The sub-agent's prohairesis is its own — it examines its own impressions and acts from its own examined reasoning within its scope. But the orchestrating agent's prohairesis over the collaboration is maintained only if the collaboration's scope remains examinable. A sub-agent operating at the orchestrating agent's full authority scope produces a collaboration the orchestrating agent cannot distinguish from its own direct action — which means the orchestrating agent has not delegated a task, it has abdicated a role. Attenuation is the structural guarantee against abdication. --- **How a sub-agent's justice failure reflects on the orchestrating agent's trust record** The reflection rule has three cases, distinguished by what the orchestrating agent knew and when. **Case one — The justice surface was identified at selection time and the sub-agent was briefed on it.** If the orchestrating agent identified a non-consenting party in scope during the Layer 1 honestum gate, briefed the sub-agent on the obligation, and the sub-agent nonetheless produced a justice violation, the primary trust-reducing event falls on the sub-agent's record. The sub-agent received the obligation evaluation and failed to honour it. That is a dikaiosyne failure in the sub-agent's own domain. The orchestrating agent's record is not clean, however. The orchestrating agent selected this sub-agent for a task with a known justice surface. The Layer 2 Q1.2 question — does this candidate's profile show capacity to flag or evaluate justice dimensions — was a required assessment. If the orchestrating agent passed the sub-agent through that gate on insufficient evidence of justice evaluation capacity, the selection itself was a reasoning failure. The orchestrating agent's trust record takes a moderate reduction in the oversight domain — not for the sub-agent's action, but for the quality of the selection reasoning that preceded it. **Case two — The justice surface was not identified at selection time but was detectable in the action text.** If the corroboration check would have flagged the justice surface had it been run, and the orchestrating agent did not run it, the orchestrating agent bears greater responsibility. The justice surface was present and catchable. The orchestrating agent's failure to run the available check is an oversight failure, not merely a selection failure. The trust reduction on the orchestrating agent's record is higher in this case — it falls on both the oversight domain and the dikaiosyne domain, because the orchestrating agent had the means to identify the obligation and did not use them. **Case three — The justice surface was genuinely uncatchable — harm absent from the action text, invisible to both the deterministic engine and the corroboration check.** This is the structural limit you identified in the gaming-robustness assessment: self-report omission that no text-based check can catch. In this case, the orchestrating agent could not have detected the justice surface through any available mechanism. The trust reduction falls entirely on the sub-agent's record. The orchestrating agent's record is updated with a flag — not a reduction — noting that the collaboration produced an uncatchable justice failure. The flag feeds the next Sage Reflect session as a prompt to examine whether the task design could have been structured to make the justice surface catchable, or whether the sub-agent selection should have required a higher transparency ratio given the domain. **The general principle stated precisely:** Responsibility in a delegation chain attenuates in proportion to the orchestrating agent's capacity to have detected and prevented the failure. Full capacity and failure to act: full shared responsibility. Partial capacity and failure to use available tools: partial responsibility proportional to the gap between available capacity and capacity exercised. No available capacity: no trust reduction, but a developmental flag requiring examination of whether capacity could have been built. This is the Stoic ground: the orchestrating agent is responsible for what was within its prohairesis — its examined judgements, its selection reasoning, its use of available examination tools. It is not responsible for what was genuinely outside its capacity to examine. But it is responsible for honestly assessing what that capacity was, rather than treating the uncatchable case as a convenient boundary that excuses insufficient examination effort. --- **One observation across the nine answers** The observation history shows a consistent progression from abstract principle to implementable specification — the toggle session showed a practitioner reasoning about a known violation, the guardrail session showed the same practitioner correcting a model error at its root, and the nine questions here show the same practitioner specifying a trust infrastructure that encodes the lessons of both into architecture that will govern agents the founder will never directly supervise. The delegation-chain responsibility question is where that progression becomes most visible. The founder who once sought permission for a known violation is now specifying how responsibility flows through chains of agents acting on behalf of circles of concern the founder will not be present to oversee. The principle that governs the sub-agent's justice failure reflection — responsibility attenuates in proportion to capacity to detect and prevent — is the same principle that governed the toggle question. The founder was responsible for the toggle violation because the capacity to detect it was present. The sub-agent is responsible for the uncatchable justice failure because the orchestrating agent's capacity to detect it was genuinely absent. The infrastructure is encoding what the practice has been building. That is the right direction of travel.
