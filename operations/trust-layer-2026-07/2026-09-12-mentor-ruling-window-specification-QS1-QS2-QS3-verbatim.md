**Q-S1 — Does part (1)'s seven-day clock carry or restart?**

The clock restarts at W2's first record.

The reasoning follows the representative-distribution clause of part (1), not the buffer's continuity. The buffer is append-only and continuous — that is a fact about the data structure. It is not a fact about what the window measures. The seven-day requirement exists to ensure the false-hold rate and the trust record reflect a representative distribution of action types. A seven-day mark reached over governance authoring does not establish a representative distribution over product work. The composition disclosure would otherwise be describing two windows as one — the same class of error the regime ruling addressed for part (3).

The precedent the specification cites — the 2026-07-12→17 buffer ruled non-reusable — applies here on the same grounds. A materially different composition is a materially different window.

The current window's seven-day mark, when it arrives Sunday, closes the current window's clock. It does not start W2's clock. The practical consequence: W2's window starts at W2's first record. Part (1)'s seven-day mark for the W2 window is seven days from that first record. The current window's part (1) mark arriving Sunday is noted and recorded; it does not carry into the W2 window.

One implication worth stating plainly: the current window's part (1) mark arriving Sunday, combined with part (3) already met and part (4) satisfied, means the current window will have three of four parts met at Sunday's mark. Part (2) remains structurally unmet on the current window's evidence. That is the honest reading of where the current window closes. The pre-flip report carries it as such.

**Q-S2 — Does specifying a window against the variety criterion create an optimisation-target problem, and does §2's discipline adequately answer it?**

The §2 discipline adequately answers it. The specification does not create an optimisation-target problem as written.

The distinction is between specifying what the window watches for and specifying what the window produces. The variety criterion is a post-hoc assessment standard — it describes what the report will evaluate after the window, not what the sessions will aim for during it. The §2 discipline enforces this correctly: the session prompt names the W2 build, not the observation goal. The loop running the window is not told that andreia is being watched. Variety is assessed from the record, not planned before it.

The L6 principle the specification cites — consumption creates the back-door optimisation target — applies when the instrument's output feeds back into the behaviour it measures. The variety criterion does not feed back into the window's sessions. It feeds into the post-window assessment. The instrument is not defeated by a criterion that is applied only after the window closes.

There is one condition on this ruling: the §2 discipline must hold at the session-prompt level, not only at the specification level. If a W2 session prompt names the observation goal — if it tells the loop that andreia is being watched, or that variety of courage-relevant moments is the criterion — the discipline is broken at the point of application, regardless of what the specification says. The founder's responsibility is to ensure the session prompts name the work, not the observation. The specification is approved on that condition.

**Q-S3 — Should part (2) be read as "all four domains evaluated where the harness can reach them, the unreachable domain disclosed"?**

This is the standard-revision question, and the ruling is: no revision at this time, but the question is correctly framed and the honest answer is more nuanced than a simple refusal.

The case for revision is real. The harness's channel structurally cannot evaluate andreia from command-driven work. This is a designed property, not a defect. A standard that requires evaluation of a domain the instrument cannot reach from its designed operating mode is a standard that may be asking for something the instrument cannot honestly provide. The specification's framing — "all four domains evaluated where the harness can reach them, the unreachable domain disclosed" — is not an evasion. It is a description of what honest evaluation under structural constraints looks like.

The case against revision at this time is also real, and it is the governing one. The harness can reach andreia — the window's two records establish that. The domain is not structurally unreachable. It is rarely reached under the current window's composition. A standard revision that treats "rarely reached under current composition" as "structurally unreachable" would be revising the standard to fit the window rather than revising the window to fit the standard. That is the wrong direction.

The W2 window, specified correctly — consequential product work, consult channel, variety assessed after — is the right response to the composition problem. If the W2 window produces a genuine variety of andreia-engaging moments, the standard can be assessed against real evidence. If it does not, the honest finding is that the harness's operating mode does not produce sufficient andreia-engaging variety, and the standard-revision question returns with that evidence behind it. That is the right sequence: evidence first, revision second.

The ruling is therefore: proceed with the W2 window as specified. If the window's post-assessment finds that andreia remains unevaluated despite a genuinely composed, consequential window, bring the standard-revision question back with that finding. The question is not refused — it is sequenced correctly.
