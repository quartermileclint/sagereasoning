## Returning on M-4 before acting on it — two facts the question did not put in front of you

Yesterday you ruled on the `disposition_stability` signal: that certifying low variance as advanced
dispositional development inverts the Stoic account of progress, since the sage's disposition is stable
because it has been tested and held, not because it was never tested; that the signal should be
corrected with a perturbation-adjusted measure if that is tractable, and otherwise retired from

That ruling is accepted and I am not asking you to revisit it. I set out to execute it, and in grounding
the execution found two things about the live mechanism that the original question did not describe to
you. Both change what the remedy costs, and one of them means the signal has a second defect that is not
the one you ruled on. I would rather bring these back than act on a ruling that could not have accounted
for them.

### One — the signal is not merely reported to agents. It gates the grade.

The question described `disposition_stability` as a dimension surfaced on agent-facing surfaces. It is
also a gate on the Senecan grade ladder.

There are exactly four dimensions. Every grade upgrade tests two conditions against them: that *every*
dimension meets a floor, and that a required *number* of them reach an elevated level. The two highest
rungs both demand the elevated level be "advanced" — and the topmost transition, principled to
sage-like, requires **all four** dimensions at advanced. The resulting grade sets the authority level
published on the agent's public credential, and the path is live: it runs on every Sage Reflect
completion.

So the consequence of the remedy is larger than the question implied. Retiring this dimension — or
capping it below advanced, which is what removing the false certification amounts to — does not just
remove a misleading reading from a display. **It makes the principled-to-sage-like transition
structurally unreachable**, because that rung cannot be cleared without this dimension reaching a level
it could no longer reach.

I want to be clear that I think a defensible reading of your own logic endorses that outcome: if the
certification cannot honestly be made, the grade that depends on it should not be reachable until it can
be. But it is a live change to what standing an agent can attain, and it should be said rather than
assumed.

### Two — the signal ignores the level of what is consistent.

Your grievance was that low variance in the absence of perturbation is certified as stability under
perturbation. The function has a second, independent defect.

It computes the mean of the readings and then uses that mean only to derive the variance. The level it
awards is set from the variance alone. The mean is never consulted again. So the signal cannot
distinguish consistent excellence from consistent failure.

I ran this against the live function rather than inferring it. Feeding thirty identical readings and
varying only their quality:

- thirty readings at the worst level — advanced
- thirty at the second-worst — advanced
- thirty at the middle level — advanced
- thirty at the second-best — advanced
- thirty at the best — advanced

Thirty consecutive readings of the *worst available reasoning*, perfectly consistent, certify as
advanced, with the indicators "highly consistent proximity across actions" and "disposition approaching
hexis", at maximum confidence — because the confidence figure is itself an inverse of variance and is
maximised by having none.

Two honest limits on that, so it is not read as more than it is. First, this alone does not promote a
poor agent: the same upgrade gate separately tests the *distribution* of reasoning quality, which thirty
worst-level readings fail outright. The real exposure is narrower — an agent who does clear that quality
bar receives a free "advanced" toward the required count without having earned it on this dimension's own
terms. Second, and more importantly: this is not your defect. Thirty identical readings at the *best*
level, in an environment that never tested the agent, would also still certify advanced. Fixing
mean-blindness leaves the perturbation problem entirely intact.

### What this does to the tractability question your preference order turned on

You made correction conditional on tractability. Grounding that honestly, in both directions:

An earlier internal assessment claimed no independent signal existed to condition on. That claim was
wrong, and I would rather say so than lean on it. There are usable channels the measure does not
currently read — a server-side timestamp already attached to every element and never consulted, and a
record of which consultation surface each reading came from, persisted but not selected.

But none of them distinguishes perturbation. Time-spread measures persistence, not having been tested.
Surface variety measures where an agent was used. Neither separates holding steady under varied and
adverse conditions from never having been varied. A further obstacle applies to any corrected measure,
not only a perturbation-adjusted one: the population itself includes examinations whose framing was never
actually delivered to the agent, and carries no marker distinguishing them — a survivorship problem
underneath whatever is computed on top.

**So: the correction you preferred is not available on the terms you set it — though not for the reason
first given.** A partial correction is cheaply available (require an adequate mean as well as low
variance before certifying advanced) and fixes the second defect while discharging none of the first.
