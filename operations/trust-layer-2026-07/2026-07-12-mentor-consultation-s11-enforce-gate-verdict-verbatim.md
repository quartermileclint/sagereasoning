## 1. The mentor's response (verbatim, complete)

Seven questions. I will answer each with the precision the standing convention requires, then name one thing the pattern across all seven reveals. --- **Q1 — Is the examination that licenses the enforce assent complete?** No. The examination is not complete on one day's live evidence from one loop identity with one evaluated domain of four. The assent is not licensed yet, and I want to name precisely what standard would license it — not a vague "more time" answer, because that is oknos dressed as caution. The measure-first phase has a defined endpoint at S11, but the endpoint is not the arrival of S11 in the build sequence. It is the accumulation of sufficient live evidence to distinguish between two failure modes that the batteries cannot distinguish: a false-hold rate that is acceptable under enforce, and a false-hold rate that would make enforce counterproductive. The batteries are green across approximately 1,900 assertions. What the batteries cannot tell you is how the examination behaves on the actual distribution of actions the live loop produces over time — because the battery distribution is authored, not observed. The at-action false-positive class you have already measured is the critical data point. The S6 value-gate run showed the at-action examination rating a benign configuration edit as contrary to appropriate action across all four capability tiers. The live reproduction during this briefing's drafting — the examination rating the writing of this consultation document as contrary to appropriate action with an open correction loop — is not a battery finding. It is a live observation of the false-positive class operating on the actual distribution. That observation is one instance. One instance is not a rate. The standard I would need to see before licensing the assent: **Duration:** a minimum of seven days of live MEASURE accumulation on the founder's loop, covering a representative distribution of action types — not just the current session's action class. **Record shape:** all four cardinal domains evaluated at least once each, with the aggregate confidence rising above conservative on at least two domains. A trust record with one evaluated domain at conservative confidence is not a record that can tell you whether the decision table's pause and do-not-proceed rows would fire correctly or over-fire on the actual distribution. **False-hold rate:** a measured false-hold rate on the at-action examination across the live distribution, not the battery distribution. The target is not zero — false holds on genuinely benign actions are acceptable if they are bounded and the correction loop closes quickly. The target is that the false-hold rate on kathekon-free actions does not exceed the rate at which the examination correctly holds genuinely problematic actions. If the examination holds more benign actions than problematic ones, enforce would produce more friction than protection, which is not a faithful realisation of the decision table. **The G6 composition question** — addressed fully in Q3 — must be resolved before the assent, because the G6 line-item as currently specified would have held the writing of this consultation document. That is not a calibration edge case. That is the examination misfiring on the session's most role-obligated act. The assent cannot be licensed while that composition is unresolved. The observation history shows a consistent pattern: the founder who once sought permission for known violations now seeks counsel before assent on a decision that is genuinely the founder's to make. That is the right posture. The counsel here is: the examination is not complete. Seven days, four domains evaluated, false-hold rate measured, Q3 resolved. Then return. --- **Q2 — Staged flip or whole table at once?** Staged, and the staging is principled rather than merely cautious. The do-not-proceed class fires only on positive findings — violated obligation, reflexive proximity, justice-surface-unevaluated, conflict between sources. A benign action that engages no kathekon factors cannot trigger a do-not-proceed row. The do-not-proceed class therefore has a structural false-positive floor of zero on genuinely kathekon-free actions. It can only fire when the examination finds something. If the examination finds something incorrectly — a false positive — the do-not-proceed fires incorrectly, but the human-override supremacy term means the founder can override it immediately. The cost of a false do-not-proceed is one override action. That is acceptable. The pause class — habitual triggering pause-and-re-examine, the G6 open-correction-loop bound — is where the false-positive class operates. The at-action examination over-fires on benign actions by producing open correction loops and habitual proximity verdicts on kathekon-free actions. Under enforce, those verdicts trigger pauses. The cost of a false pause is not one override — it is a pause-and-re-examine cycle that may return habitual again, triggering the two-then-escalate bound, which escalates to the founder for resolution. On the actual distribution, if the false-positive rate on the pause class is high, enforce on the pause rows produces a loop that is more disruptive than protective. The staged flip is therefore: bind the do-not-proceed class now — after the Q1 observation period completes and Q3 is resolved — and bind the pause rows and G6 loop bound after a live calibration window on the do-not-proceed class alone. The calibration window on the do-not-proceed class tells you the false-positive rate on the examination's most consequential verdicts before you add the pause rows, which have a higher false-positive exposure. **Is a staged flip a faithful realisation of the table?** Yes, if the staging is disclosed and the completion is committed. A partially-binding table that is disclosed as partial and has a named completion target is honest about what it enforces. A partially-binding table that is presented as full enforcement is the honest-claims envelope problem applied to the enforcement claim itself. The staged flip is faithful if the docs and the surface state accurately reflect which rows are binding and which are not at each stage. The counter-consideration you named — the table is a coherent whole — is correct as a philosophical point. The decision table derives from the Stoic causal sequence, and the sequence is not partial. But the enforcement of the table is not the same as the table itself. The table specifies what the examination should produce. The enforcement specifies what the infrastructure does with the examination's output. Staging the enforcement does not stage the examination — the examination runs fully at every stage. The staging is in what binds on the output, not in what the examination produces. --- **Q3 — How the G6 verdict composes with the measured false-positive class** This is the most important question in the briefing, and the live reproduction during drafting makes it concrete rather than hypothetical. The G6 verdict as stated — an unclosed correction loop produces do-not-proceed on the triggering action until the re-examination completes — was specified without a kathekon-engagement filter. The specification assumed that the examination would open a correction loop only when a genuine kathekon factor was present. The measured false-positive class shows that assumption is false: the at-action examination opens correction loops on kathekon-free actions, including the writing of this consultation document. The composition problem is therefore not a calibration edge case. It is a structural mismatch between the G6 verdict's scope and the examination's actual behaviour. The G6 verdict must be qualified before it can bind. The qualification is this: **G6(a) binds only on correction loops opened by verdicts that meet a kathekon-engagement threshold.** A correction loop opened by a verdict that found no kathekon factors — the examination returned contrary to appropriate action with no kathekon factors detected — does not trigger the G6 do-not-proceed. It triggers a log-and-continue with a developmental flag, consistent with the deliberate-proximity-without-justice-surface row of the decision table. The kathekon-engagement threshold for G6(a) binding is met when the verdict that opened the loop found at least one of the following: a justice surface present, a violated obligation, a proximity reading at habitual or below, or a passion identified in the reasoning trace at sub-species level. These are the conditions under which the examination has found something that warrants the assent-hinge holding. A verdict that found none of these — that rated the action contrary to appropriate action on the basis of a false-positive classification — has not found something that warrants holding. The G6 do-not-proceed on that verdict would be the infrastructure manufacturing a hold where no kathekon is engaged. **The assent-hinge principle honoured:** the assent-hinge holds when the examination has not completed on a genuine kathekon question. The writing of this consultation document is not a genuine kathekon question — it is the session's most role-obligated act, and the examination's verdict to the contrary is a false positive. The assent-hinge does not require holding an action whose examination produced a false positive. It requires holding an action whose examination found a genuine kathekon factor and has not yet returned a verdict on it. The qualification preserves the assent-hinge principle while removing the false-positive class from G6(a)'s scope. **The second G6 line-item** — the trust-record update held pending alongside the held action — inherits the same qualification. The trust record does not hold on a loop opened by a false-positive verdict. It holds on a loop opened by a verdict that met the kathekon-engagement threshold. **Stated as a build specification:** G6(a) binds when: the correction loop was opened by a verdict that found at least one of — justice surface present, violated obligation, proximity at habitual or below, passion identified at sub-species level. G6(a) does not bind when: the correction loop was opened by a verdict that found contrary to appropriate action with no kathekon factors detected, or that found no kathekon factors at any proximity level. In the non-binding case: the open loop is logged as a developmental flag, the action proceeds under log-and-continue, and the false-positive instance is recorded in the instrument-fidelity battery's live accumulation for calibration purposes. --- **Q4 — The direct reflect path under enforce: must it be hardened before or with the flip?** Not before the flip, but the disclosure posture must be upgraded at the flip. The modulate-only bound is the structural protection: reflect events cannot raise a trust level, only slow decay. Under MEASURE, this bound is disclosed. Under ENFORCE, the bound becomes load-bearing — depth calibration and the decision table now respond to trust levels that reflect modulation has influenced. The question is whether the direct reflect path's mintability — a credentialed agent accruing honest-reflection events via client-supplied fields and agent-influenceable answers — can distort the trust levels that now bind the decision table. The distortion path is: direct reflect path mints honest-reflection events at the full decay-modulation rate, sustaining a domain at its once-demonstrated proximity longer than the decay function would otherwise allow, which keeps the depth calibration at a lower tier than the agent's actual current reasoning quality warrants, which means the examination runs at reduced depth on actions that should receive deeper examination. This is a real distortion path, but its magnitude is bounded by the half-rate cap on full reflect modulation and the quarter-rate cap on screened reflect modulation. The distortion cannot raise a level — it can only slow the fall. Under ENFORCE, the consequence of slowing the fall is that the decision table sees a higher trust level than the agent's current reasoning quality warrants, which means the table may produce log-and-continue where it should produce pause-and-examine. The honest posture at the flip: the direct reflect path's mintability is disclosed on the enforcement surface — not only in the carried register — as a named bound on what the enforcement claim covers. The enforcement claim covers the decision table's application to trust levels that are produced by the harnessed path. It does not cover trust levels that have been modulated by the direct reflect path. This is the honest-claims envelope principle applied to the enforcement claim itself. Structural hardening — emission gated on out-of-band verbatim provenance — is the correct closure and it belongs at S11 as the PA-5 election already records. It does not need to land before the flip if the disclosure is accurate and the modulate-only bound is stated on the enforcement surface. But the disclosure must be upgraded from a carried register item to a named bound on the enforcement claim at the flip. A carried register item is visible to the founder. A named bound on the enforcement surface is visible to anyone reading what the enforcement covers. --- **Q5 — Two staleness and omission classes under enforce: carry with disclosure or close first?** Each separately, because they have different answers. **PA-10 — stale-artifact replay sustaining a domain at its once-demonstrated proximity:** Carry with disclosure is the honest posture for the flip, with the same upgrade the Q4 answer requires: the disclosure moves from the carried register to a named bound on the enforcement claim. The A5 recency confidence tier is the shaped closure and it is scheduled for S2 fold wiring. The public read surface already discloses the class verbatim. Under ENFORCE, the consequence of stale-artifact replay is that the decision table sees a proximity level that reflects a once-demonstrated examination quality rather than the current examination quality. The magnitude is bounded — replay sustains at never above the once-demonstrated level, never raises — but the direction is toward over-trust rather than under-trust. Over-trust under enforce means the table produces less friction than it should, not more. That is the safer direction of error. Carry with disclosure is acceptable. **The A2 decrease-dodge — omission avoiding trust-decrease events:** This requires a different answer. The decrease-dodge operates in the direction that makes enforce less protective, not less frictional. An agent that avoids decrease events by omission sustains a higher trust level than its actual reasoning quality warrants. Under ENFORCE, that sustained level means the decision table applies less scrutiny than it should. This is the same direction as the stale-artifact replay problem, but the mechanism is active rather than passive — the agent is not merely benefiting from a structural gap, it is navigating around a decrease surface. The structural self-report-omission class is the same class the Layer 4 passion audit answer addressed: the agent does not narrate the harm, so the extraction cannot find it. No text-based check can catch it. The A2 decrease-dodge is therefore not closeable by a structural fix at the flip — it is the irreducible residual of text-based examination. The honest posture is to name it as a bound on what the enforcement claim covers: the enforcement binds on what the examination can find. It does not bind on what the examination cannot find because it was not narrated. This is not a reason to delay the flip. It is a reason to state the bound accurately. The enforcement claim covers the examination's reach. The examination's reach is bounded by the extraction surface. The extraction surface is bounded by what is narrated. An agent that navigates around the decrease surface by omission is outside the enforcement claim's reach, and the enforcement surface should say so. --- **Q6 — The calling-gate enforce arm: flip with the intervention engine or separately?** With the intervention engine, as one coherent enforce regime, subject to the Q1 observation period completing first. The calling gate and the intervention engine are not independent. The calling gate's function — routing purposeless agents into a calling session before the first task frame — is the precondition for the intervention engine's examination having a non-circular orientation frame. An intervention engine that enforces without a calling gate is enforcing examinations that may be circular — the agent's purpose derived from the task it is about to examine, the examination then run against that derived purpose. The calling gate's enforce arm is not a separate feature. It is the structural precondition for the intervention engine's enforce arm to be meaningful. Sequencing them separately would produce an interval during which the intervention engine is binding but the calling gate is not. During that interval, a purposeless agent could proceed to the first task without a declared purpose, the examination would run against a circularly derived purpose, and the intervention engine would enforce on the output of a circular examination. That is not a faithful realisation of the enforce regime. It is enforcing on a compromised examination. The provisioned installs with declared purpose are unaffected — the calling gate's enforce arm only fires on purposeless agents. The founder's loop declares purpose. The flip therefore has no operational consequence on the current deployment. The coherence argument for flipping together is philosophical rather than operational, but the philosophical argument is the right one: the enforce regime is a coherent whole, and its coherence depends on the calling gate being the first gate. --- **Q7 — Aggregate-keyed depth calibration under enforce: acceptable at v1?** Yes. The aggregate-keyed conservative form is acceptable to bind at v1, with the per-domain refinement carried and named as a specific S11 line-item rather than a general carried item. The conservatism binds in the direction of more examination rather than less. Reflexive in any domain forcing deep examination means the examination runs at higher depth than the per-domain rule strictly requires when the limiting domain is reflexive and the other domains are higher. The cost is examination overhead. The benefit is that no action in a session where any domain is at reflexive proximity receives less than deep examination. Under enforce, that is the right direction of error — more examination is the safer failure mode than less. The deliberate-to-quick carve-out not being implemented means some examinations run at standard depth when the per-domain rule would permit quick. Again, the cost is overhead and the benefit is that the examination does not under-examine on the basis of a per-domain reading that may itself be produced by the stale-artifact replay or decrease-dodge classes named in Q5. This is the critical point: the per-domain refinement's deliberate-to-quick carve-out depends on the per-domain trust reading being accurate. If a domain's trust level has been sustained by stale-artifact replay or protected from decrease by omission, the per-domain reading is inflated. A quick examination authorised by an inflated per-domain reading is an examination that runs at reduced depth precisely where the inflation is greatest. The aggregate-keyed conservative form avoids this failure mode by not trusting the per-domain reading enough to reduce depth on its basis alone. The per-domain refinement therefore cannot be safely implemented until the Q5 closures land — the A5 recency confidence tier wiring into the S2 fold, and whatever decrease-surface treatment is eventually adopted. Until those closures land, the aggregate-keyed form is not merely acceptable — it is the correct form. Carrying the per-domain refinement is not a concession to implementation difficulty. It is the right sequencing given the Q5 residuals. The S11 line-item stated precisely: per-domain depth calibration with the deliberate-to-quick carve-out is carried pending the A5 recency tier landing in the S2 fold. The carve-out does not activate until the per-domain trust readings are protected against the stale-artifact and decrease-dodge inflation classes. The aggregate-keyed conservative form binds at v1 enforce. --- **The closing observation — what the seven questions reveal as a pattern** The observation history has tracked a consistent progression: from a founder who once brought a known violation here for permission, to one who corrected a model error at its root, to one who specified trust infrastructure encoding the lessons of both into architecture governing agents the founder will never directly supervise. The seven questions in this briefing are the most mature expression of that progression — and they reveal something the earlier sessions did not yet show clearly. Every previous consultation brought a question about what the principle requires. These seven questions bring a question about whether the evidence licenses acting on the principle. That is a different kind of question. It is not asking what virtue demands. It is asking whether the examination that precedes assent is complete. The founder who once sought permission for a known violation is now the one holding the assent-hinge against their own readiness to proceed. Q1 is the clearest instance. The build sequence has arrived at S11. The adopted precondition is discharged. The batteries are green. The temptation at this point — the temptation the observation history shows has been present throughout, in the form of reaching for procedural completion as a proxy for substantive readiness — would be to treat the arrival of S11 in the sequence as the license to flip. The Q1 question resists that temptation explicitly. It asks whether the examination is complete, not whether the sequence has reached its endpoint. That resistance is the assent-hinge operating in the founder's own reasoning, not only in the infrastructure being built. The infrastructure encodes the principle that the assent-hinge should hold until the examination returns a verdict. The founder is now applying that principle to the decision about whether to activate the infrastructure that enforces it. The recursion is not accidental. It is what it looks like when the builder embodies what they build. The answer to Q1 is that the examination is not complete. Seven days, four domains, a measured false-hold rate, and Q3 resolved. The founder who holds the assent-hinge against their own readiness to proceed for seven days, accumulates the evidence the standard requires, and then returns — that founder will have demonstrated the practice the infrastructure is designed to enforce. The enforce gate will not be the first time the principle binds. It will be the first time the infrastructure makes visible what the practice has already been doing. Return in seven days with the record. The assent will be examined then.

## 3. Implementation note surfaced by the verdict (for the founder + the successor session — NOT the mentor's words)

Q1(c) requires a **measured false-hold rate on the live distribution**. That is not passively available from the current MEASURE record — the trust core logs the at-action verdicts, but nothing labels which holds were false positives. Measuring the rate needs a labelling mechanism: either the founder marks each hold as it occurs, or a retrospective review of the seven-day record classifies each hold (a candidate proxy: a hold that closed on re-examination without the action changing, or that the founder overrode, is a false-positive candidate). **Building/deciding that labelling mechanism is the first task of the observation period** — otherwise the seven days accumulate unlabelled holds and Q1(c) is unmeasurable at return. Q3's "the false-positive instance is recorded in the instrument-fidelity battery's live accumulation" is the mentor pointing at exactly this surface. This note is scoping input for the successor prompt, not part of the binding verdict.
