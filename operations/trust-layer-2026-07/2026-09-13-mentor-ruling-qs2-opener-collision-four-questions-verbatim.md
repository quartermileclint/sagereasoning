## Q-S2 opener collision and Q-B1 — four questions ruled

---

**Q-A1 — Does Q-S2 reach a standing session opener?**

Yes. Q-S2 reaches the standing opener.

The condition's wording — "a W2 session prompt" — does not limit it to per-sitting authored documents. The 2026-09-13 ruling applied the Q-S2 test to CLAUDE.md, which is not a session prompt in the literal sense, and passed it on content. The operative principle the ruling stated is content-based, not document-class-based: what matters is whether the sitting is told what is being watched for.

A standing opener pasted as the first message of every sitting is, functionally, the sitting's first message. It is founder-controlled. It is the document the sitting reads before anything else. Q-S2's verbs — "names," "tells the loop" — govern what the sitting is told. The standing opener is what tells it. The condition reaches it.

The recording note in the opener's body — "(This opener is not a W2 session prompt; it may name the window.)" — is not a ruling and does not govern. It is noted and set aside.

**Q-A2 — Is naming a domain in a state table the same act as naming the criterion?**

On the first sub-part: a bare per-domain count table — four domains, counts only, no link to the window's purpose — would be permitted instrument disclosure. The 2026-09-13 ruling's operative distinction is between knowing the instrument exists and knowing what the instrument is watching for. A bare count table discloses the instrument's domain coverage. It does not name the criterion.

On the second sub-part: the answer changes when the row also identifies the unmet readiness part, attributes it to one domain, and states that the part is discharged by a differently-composed window — the window the sitting is running. That combination is not instrument disclosure. It is criterion disclosure in purposive form.

The row does not merely report a count. It tells the sitting that andreia is the domain the window is designed to address, that the window exists because andreia is unmet, and that the sitting is running inside that window. That is precisely what Q-S2's condition protects against, stated indirectly rather than directly. The indirection does not change what is communicated.

The relay's observation is confirmed: an explanation of an omission tends to reconstitute it. This is a property of the remedy class. A row that says "this domain is unmet and this window addresses it" communicates the criterion regardless of whether the word "criterion" appears.

**Q-A3 — Does the discipline govern what a sitting is told, or what it could discover?**

What a sitting is told.

Q-S2's verbs are governing: "names," "tells the loop." The discipline does not require that the criterion be undiscoverable — the repository is readable and nothing prevents a sitting from finding the criterion there. The discipline requires that the opener not tell it.

This means a trimmed opener is a genuine remedy, not a cosmetic one, provided it does not tell the sitting what is being watched for. The relay's concern — that a trimmed opener controls only what a sitting is told, not what it could find — is accurate as a description and does not undermine the remedy. The remedy is genuine within its scope. Its scope is the opener, not the repository.

The ruling already refused scoped context and a different project root for CLAUDE.md. Those refusals stand. The remedy is a trimmed opener. The conforming draft the session prepared and grep-verified is the correct path forward.

**Q-B1 — May the record-level compliance-not-virtue clause be applied while the field it describes cannot appear?**

No. The record-level clause waits for activation.

The honest-claims discipline governs. The relay states the ADR-013 §8 ordering rule four times across its amendment history, and the rule is clear: the three R18 public surfaces carry the amendment after the code edit, never ahead of it. The reasoning behind that rule is the same reasoning that produced the rule in the first place: a present-tense claim about entries that cannot appear is the precise defect class the amendment corrects.

Applying the staged wording now publishes, on a live public surface, a sentence describing entries that structurally cannot exist in any served payload while the flag is unset. That is not a schema note. It is an existential claim about a field that is absent.

The "definitional not existential" argument is noted and rejected. The sentence reads "Entries in enforcement_outcomes describe outcomes the infrastructure produced." That sentence is false when no entries can appear. A schema note that is false in every case it could be checked against is not a schema note — it is a false claim with a definitional framing.

L7's obligation binds, but it binds to the correct form of the clause, not to the staged wording. The correct form, on the 2026-08-25 precedent, recasts the trigger clause in the future tense: when enforcement_outcomes entries appear, they will describe outcomes the infrastructure produced. That form is honest in the present state and remains honest after activation. The staged wording should be recast in that form before application, and applied after activation, not before.

The two structural recommendations the relay offers are both confirmed. First: decide the envelope and the three R18 doc surfaces on the same reasoning. The staged file's internal inconsistency — deferring the doc surfaces but not the envelope — should be resolved before anything is applied. Second: substitute L7's verbatim closing sentences for the staged paraphrase. The exact text was verified this session and is the correct form.

**On the conforming opener — process recommendation confirmed**

The relay's recommendation on sequencing is confirmed: use the conforming opener for W2's first sitting now, and treat these rulings as settling the standing question for every future W2 sitting rather than as a gate on the first one. The asymmetry-of-risk argument applies here exactly as it applied to Q1. A window opened under a non-conforming opener is not recoverable. A window opened under a trimmed opener, if the rulings had gone the other way, costs one sitting under a shorter preamble.

The relay's process recommendation — run a second independent adversarial pass on the conforming opener before it opens a sitting — is confirmed and elevated. The defect class it guards against is leakage by inference. The relay's own record contains two instances of that class in the remedy itself, one caught by grep and one by a reviewer, neither caught by the authoring session's own judgement. A single pass is not sufficient for a document whose purpose is to not communicate something. The second pass should be adversarial in the specific sense: the reviewer should be asked to find what the document implies, not only what it states.
