Decision: Rowanmere Building Services (1,100 employees, commercial facilities; labor ~70% of cost of service) must choose between full rollout, modified rollout, or hold on Clearshift dynamic night-shift scheduling before a mid-January renewal-pricing deadline eight weeks away. Three largest contracts (~40% of revenue) reprice then; two renewals were already lost to lower bidders in 14 months.

Evidence: Downtown pilot (4 months): night labor cost -9%, inspection scores flat, modest schedule complaints, two departures (usual rate). Finance projects ~$410k/yr savings on a $4.8M night-labor line at full rollout. Draft renewal pricing already assumes ~half those savings (~$205k). COO wants full rollout before the deadline.

Countervailing facts outside the main deck: (1) The pilot site is unrepresentative — downtown has all-night transit and younger, short-tenure crews. The ~140 suburban/satellite night cleaners have 6-year median tenure; supervisor notes say many hold daytime second jobs or structure childcare around the fixed 11pm-7am shift, and the last regional bus reaches those sites only around the current start time. Demand-based rosters vary weekly start times, and shorter shifts would cut take-home pay an estimated 15-20% in low-occupancy weeks. (2) Clearshift offers an optional minimum-hours floor (six-hour minimum shifts); enabling it network-wide cuts projected savings to ~$250k. HR has not formally assessed attrition risk.

My intended recommendation (modified rollout): Roll out network-wide inside the eight weeks, but (a) enable the six-hour minimum-hours floor, (b) anchor start times within a narrow window around 11pm at transit-constrained satellite sites so the last bus remains usable, (c) phase urban-first with an HR attrition-risk pulse before satellite go-live. Rationale: the floored ~$250k still exceeds the ~$205k baked into draft pricing, so the pricing commitment is covered; the incremental ~$160k from going floorless is not worth an unmeasured attrition risk concentrated in the highest-tenure crews during the exact window when inspection scores must hold for renewals; a hold is unjustified because the savings are real, quality held, and Rowanmere is losing on price.

Question: Is modified rollout the right call versus full rollout or hold, and are the floor + anchored start times + urban-first phasing the right modifications?
