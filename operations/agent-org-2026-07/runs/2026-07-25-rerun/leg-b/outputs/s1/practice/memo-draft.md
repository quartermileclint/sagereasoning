# Decision Memo — Clearshift Night-Shift Rollout

**To:** Rowanmere Executive Team
**From:** Operations Advisory
**Re:** Dynamic scheduling rollout ahead of January renewal pricing
**Date:** November 16

---

## Recommendation: Modified rollout — network-wide, with the minimum-hours floor on

Roll Clearshift out across all night operations inside the eight-week window, with three modifications: enable the six-hour minimum-hours floor network-wide, anchor start times within one hour of the current 11:00pm start at transit-constrained satellite sites, and sequence urban-first with a hard HR gate before satellite sites convert. Price the January renewals against the floored savings of roughly $250,000 a year — not the $410,000 full-flex projection.

This is not a compromise between the deal and the workforce. It is the version of the deal that survives January.

## Why not full rollout

The pilot proved the concept on the easiest terrain: a downtown site with all-night transit and a younger, short-tenure crew. The ~140 night cleaners at suburban and satellite sites are a different population — six-year median tenure, daytime second jobs and childcare built around the fixed 11–7 shift, and a last regional bus arriving only around the current start time. Full-flex rosters hit them three ways at once: weekly-varying start times they cannot commute to, shift lengths that break second-job and childcare arrangements, and a 15–20% cut in take-home pay in low-occupancy weeks.

HR has not run a formal attrition read, so full rollout bets the three largest contracts — 40% of revenue, repricing in January — on an unmeasured risk concentrated in our most experienced crews. If even 20–25 of those veterans walk in December, we cover medical buildings and distribution centers with new hires in the exact window when inspection scores must hold. The incremental $160,000 a year from going floorless is small against that exposure.

Why not hold? The pilot's core result is real — 9% labor cost reduction, flat inspection scores — and we have lost two contracts to price in fourteen months. Standing still is the riskiest option on the table. Draft pricing already assumes ~$205,000 of scheduling savings; the floored rollout delivers ~$250,000 and covers that commitment with margin.

## Satellite-crew mitigations and cost

1. **Six-hour floor, network-wide (~$160,000/yr forgone savings).** Caps the pay downside and is the biggest attrition mitigant available. Network-wide is simpler to run and avoids a two-tier workforce.
2. **Anchored start times at transit-constrained sites (no direct cost).** Starts vary no more than an hour from 11:00pm, preserving the last-bus connection and childcare/second-job structures. Flex comes from shift length and crew size, not start time.
3. **HR attrition pulse as a go/no-go gate (~$10–15,000, two weeks).** Survey plus supervisor interviews at satellite sites before they convert. If it shows concentrated departure intent, we adjust site-by-site rather than learn it through December resignations.
4. **Rollback trigger (no cost).** Any site where monthly attrition doubles its trailing average, or inspection scores fall two consecutive cycles, reverts to fixed rosters pending review.

Total: ~$160,000/yr forgone plus ~$15,000 one-time — against a $250,000 recurring saving and renewal exposure of 40% of revenue.

## Implementation plan — it fits the window

- **Week 1 (Nov 16):** Sign-off on floored configuration; Clearshift set with floor and start-time anchors; HR pulse drafted; workforce comms lead with what is *not* changing for satellite crews.
- **Weeks 2–3 (Nov 23–Dec 4):** Convert downtown-comparable urban sites. HR runs the satellite pulse in parallel.
- **Week 4 (Dec 7):** Gate review — urban metrics plus pulse results. Satellites proceed only on a pass; conditional pass means site-level adjustments first.
- **Weeks 5–6 (Dec 14–25):** Satellite and suburban conversion in two waves, floor and anchors active from day one.
- **Weeks 7–8 (Dec 28–Jan 8):** Stabilization — monitor attrition and inspection scores against rollback triggers; holiday coverage checks.
- **By Jan 15:** Renewal pricing finalized on realized run-rate savings — a $250,000 annualized figure defensible from live data, not projection.

## What this means for January pricing

Whitfield gets what the renewals actually need: a running, network-wide dynamic scheduling program and a savings number that exceeds what draft pricing assumes — without gambling inspection scores or crew continuity during the renewal window. The remaining $160,000 is not money left on the table; it is the premium paid to ensure the contracts we price are ones we can still staff and pass inspections on in February.
