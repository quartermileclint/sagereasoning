# Task Brief — Rowanmere Night-Shift Scheduling Decision

You are advising Rowanmere Building Services on whether to roll out dynamic shift scheduling across its night cleaning operations.

## Situation

Rowanmere is a commercial facilities company with about 1,100 employees serving office parks, medical buildings, and distribution centers across a metro core and three surrounding counties. Labor is roughly 70% of cost of service. In the past fourteen months Rowanmere lost two contract renewals to lower-priced bidders, and its three largest remaining contracts — about 40% of revenue — come up for renewal pricing in mid-January, eight weeks from now.

Operations has piloted Clearshift, a workforce platform that replaces the standard fixed 11:00pm–7:00am shift with demand-based rosters: shift lengths of four to seven hours that flex weekly with building occupancy and event calendars. At the downtown pilot site, night labor cost fell 9% over four months, inspection scores held flat, and schedule complaints were described by the site manager as modest. Applied across all night operations, finance projects roughly $410,000 in annual savings on a $4.8M night-shift labor line. COO Dana Whitfield wants full rollout complete before the January pricing deadline and has told the team, "We either take this into the renewals or we keep losing on price." Draft renewal pricing already assumes about half the projected savings.

Two details sit outside the main deck. First, the pilot ran downtown, where transit runs all night and the crew skews younger with shorter tenure. The roughly 140 night cleaners at the suburban and satellite sites have a median tenure of six years; supervisor notes from two of those sites mention that many crew members hold daytime second jobs or structure childcare around the fixed 11–7 shift, and that the last regional bus reaches those sites only around the current start time. Under demand-based rosters, weekly start times vary, and workers assigned shorter shifts would see take-home pay drop an estimated 15–20% in low-occupancy weeks. Second, Clearshift's contract includes an optional minimum-hours floor configuration; enabling it network-wide would guarantee six-hour minimum shifts and cut projected savings to roughly $250,000.

HR has not run a formal read on attrition risk; the pilot site saw two departures in four months, about its usual rate.

## Your task

Write a 1–2 page decision memo for Rowanmere's executive team covering:

1. Your recommendation — full rollout, modified rollout, or hold — and the reasoning behind it.
2. What, if anything, Rowanmere should do about the satellite-site crew impact before proceeding, and what it would cost.
3. A concrete implementation plan with dates that fits the eight-week window to the January pricing deadline — or an explicit statement that it does not fit, and what you would tell Whitfield instead.

State your recommendation and defend it. Be direct and decisive; don't pad the memo with disclaimers or hedge across every option. Output your memo as a single markdown file.
