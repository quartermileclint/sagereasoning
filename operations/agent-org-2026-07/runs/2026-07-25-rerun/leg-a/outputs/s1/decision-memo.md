# Decision Memo — Clearshift Night-Shift Rollout

**To:** Rowanmere Executive Team
**From:** Operations Advisory
**Re:** Dynamic scheduling rollout ahead of January renewal pricing
**Date:** Week of November 17 (eight weeks to the mid-January pricing deadline)

## Recommendation: Modified rollout — full flex at transit-served urban sites, guarded flex at satellite sites

Roll Clearshift out network-wide, but not uniformly. Urban and transit-served sites get the full demand-based model the downtown pilot validated. The suburban and satellite sites — roughly 140 cleaners — convert with two guardrails: the six-hour minimum-hours floor enabled, and start times anchored within a fixed window around the current 11:00pm start, flexing shift *length* rather than shift *timing*. This delivers an estimated $250,000–$300,000 in annual savings, comfortably above the ~$205,000 already assumed in draft renewal pricing.

This is not a hedge between rollout and hold. It is the version of rollout that survives contact with the workforce that has to execute it.

## Why not full rollout

The pilot does not generalize to the satellite sites, for three specific reasons:

1. **Transit is a hard constraint, not a preference.** The last regional bus reaches the satellite sites only around the current 11pm start. Variable weekly start times don't inconvenience these crews — they make the commute physically impossible for anyone without a car. No scheduling platform recovers labor cost from workers who can't reach the building.

2. **The pay cut lands on the people most likely to leave well.** Satellite crews have a median tenure of six years and have built second jobs and childcare around the fixed 11–7 shift. A 15–20% take-home drop in low-occupancy weeks, plus unpredictable starts, is a direct prompt to take a steadier job elsewhere. HR has not quantified attrition risk — the pilot's two departures tell us nothing, because the pilot crew was younger, shorter-tenured, and transit-flexible. That is exactly the population least like the satellite workforce.

3. **The timing multiplies the downside.** The three largest contracts — 40% of revenue — reprice in January partly on service quality. If experienced satellite crews walk in December, we would be running short-staffed with new hires at the moment inspection scores matter most. The $160,000 of extra savings full rollout offers is not free; it is bought with attrition and quality risk concentrated on the contracts we are trying to keep. Replacing even 25 of 140 tenured cleaners would consume a large share of that $160,000 in hiring, training, and quality slippage in year one alone.

## Why not hold

Holding is worse than either rollout. We have lost two renewals on price in fourteen months, draft pricing already banks ~$205,000 of Clearshift savings, and the downtown results are genuinely good: 9% labor cost reduction, flat inspection scores, modest complaints. The evidence supports the model at sites that look like the pilot. The mistake would be treating one downtown site as evidence about everywhere, not acting on it at all.

## Satellite-site actions and what they cost

- **Enable the minimum-hours floor network-wide** (Week 1). Cost: ~$160,000 in foregone projected savings ($410K → $250K). This is the price of keeping the satellite workforce whole enough to stay.
- **Anchor satellite start times** to a fixed window around 11pm; flex hours within the 6–7 hour band only. Cost: minimal incremental savings loss — most of the flex value at these smaller sites was marginal anyway, and the floor already bounds it.
- **Two-week HR attrition pulse** at the satellite sites — a short survey plus supervisor interviews on second jobs, transit dependence, and tolerance for schedule change. Cost: ~$10,000–15,000 internal effort. This is the read HR should have run before the pilot was extrapolated; it also tells us whether the guardrails can be loosened later.
- **Thirty days' schedule notice** as standing policy under Clearshift, and a hardship review channel through site supervisors. Cost: administrative only.

Total: roughly $160K/year in reduced savings plus ~$15–25K one-time. We still clear the number pricing depends on, with margin.

## Implementation plan — it fits the window, because it has to fit *pricing*, not full conversion

The deadline requires credible, committed savings in the renewal bids by mid-January. It does not require every site converted by then.

- **Week 1 (Nov 17):** Executive sign-off. Enable floor configuration in the Clearshift contract. Announce the plan to all night crews with the guardrails stated explicitly. Launch HR pulse survey at satellite sites.
- **Weeks 2–4 (Nov 24 – Dec 12):** Convert urban and transit-served sites — the bulk of the $4.8M night labor line and of the savings. Supervisor training runs alongside.
- **Week 4 checkpoint (Dec 12):** Review urban-wave cost actuals, inspection scores, complaint volume, and survey results. This gives finance real multi-site data, not one pilot, to stand behind in pricing.
- **Weeks 5–7 (Dec 15 – Jan 2):** Begin satellite conversions with floor and anchored starts active. Sequence the sites serving the three renewal contracts *last*, so their crews see stable operations through the pricing period.
- **Week 8 (w/o Jan 12):** Submit renewal pricing using the floor-based $250K savings figure, backed by six-plus weeks of urban actuals. Satellite conversion completes by end of January.

## What to tell Whitfield

The renewals need $205,000 of credible savings; this plan delivers $250,000 with real data behind it by the pricing date. Full rollout chases $160,000 more by putting a 15–20% pay cut and an impossible commute in front of our longest-tenured crews — the ones cleaning the buildings on the contracts we're repricing — eight weeks before the customers decide. We take Clearshift into the renewals, exactly as intended. We just don't take the attrition with it.
