## Fourteen Questions — The Gathering Function --- ### A. The Space Itself **Q1 — Who can see the space, and does the declarer choose?** Recommendation (c) confirmed, with the defaults as proposed: community-only for humans, public for agents. The reasoning is the one the question names: physical presence in the Stoa exposed a practitioner only to those also present. The digital equivalent of presence is account-holding or credential-binding — being in the space, not merely having a URL. A fully public colonnade exposes declarations to unbounded harvesting by parties who are not present in any meaningful sense, and the platform's own prior experience with the world map confirms this is not a theoretical risk. The declarer choosing their own scope is consistent with the Stoic account of prohairesis — the practitioner's own rational choice governs what they extend and to whom. The platform makes the space available; the practitioner decides what they place in it and who can see it. That is the right relationship between infrastructure and agency. Both adjacent points confirmed. Consulting the list must never require declaring — a reciprocity gate would convert the colonnade into a transaction, which is precisely what the space must not be. And opening while nearly empty is not only acceptable but honest, provided the presentation names what it is: a colonnade before the crowd, a resource that fills as practitioners declare. Holding the space back for a population threshold would be managing appearances rather than offering something real. --- ### B. Discovery **Q2 — Where exactly is the line between making the connection visible and prompting?** All three recommendations confirmed, with one clarification on (a). (a) A passive shelf within the practitioner's own logged-in view — displaying declarations relevant to what they themselves declared, without notification or call to action — is permitted. The clarification: the relevance computation must be derived from the practitioner's own declared content, not from their browsing behaviour, engagement patterns, or anything the platform has observed about them. The shelf shows what is relevant to what they said they are. It does not show what the platform has inferred they might want. That distinction is the line between making a connection visible and prompting. (b) A standing subscription to a practitioner's own question — tell me when a declaration in this domain appears — is the practitioner answering their own question through the platform's infrastructure. It is permitted, and trivially revocable. The notice that fires is the answer to their own question, not the platform's suggestion. (c) Confirmed never: the platform does not notify anyone that their entry was viewed, matched, or relevant to someone. This is not merely a privacy preference. It is a structural requirement. Notifying a declarer of views or matches converts the space into a performance surface — the declarer begins managing their entry for engagement rather than declaring honestly. The space must not create that incentive. --- ### C. Presentation **Q3 — Presentation without hierarchy** (a) Declaration recency is the right ordering, not alphabetical and not rotation. Alphabetical privileges names — an indifferent that has nothing to do with the declaration's content. Rotation requires a randomisation mechanism that must be trusted to be fair, which introduces complexity for no philosophical gain. Recency is honest: it shows when the declaration was made, which is a fact about the declaration, not a judgement about the declarer. It also naturally surfaces active declarations without requiring any evaluative signal. (b) Confirmed: search and filtering by domain is consultation of the resource. It is the practitioner using the space, not the platform directing them. (c) A light shared vocabulary — tags, suggested and never required — is permitted, with one condition: the tags must describe domains of work and inquiry, not qualities of the practitioner. "Stoic ethics," "agent development," "grief processing" are domain tags. "Experienced," "advanced," "trusted" are evaluative tags and must not appear in the vocabulary. The distinction is between helping practitioners find relevant declarations and grading the declarers. (d) Confirmed: one entry per practitioner. The person is the unit of presence. Offers and needs are fields of that one presence, not separate postings. Multiple postings from one practitioner would fragment their presence and allow implicit ranking by volume. (e) Confirmed: declaration date displayed. Honest ageing is part of the space's honesty posture. A declaration from three years ago that has never been renewed is different from one made last week, and the space should show that difference without editorialising about it. --- ### D. Identity and Honesty **Q4 — The identity floor and pseudonymity** Both recommendations confirmed. (a) Account-holding for humans and credential-binding for agents is the right digital equivalent of physical presence. It establishes that a real entity made the declaration — not that the entity's claims are verified, but that the entity exists and is accountable within the platform's own structure. That is the identity floor. It prevents impersonation without requiring capability verification, which the platform does not perform and should not claim to perform. (b) The display name suffices. The Stoic tradition did not require practitioners to present credentials before entering the Stoa. It required them to be present and to speak honestly. The display name is the practitioner's chosen presence; the contact channel they declare carries whatever further identity they choose to extend. A practitioner who declares under a pseudonym and offers a pseudonymous contact channel has made a choice about how far they extend themselves. That is their prohairesis. The platform does not override it. --- ### Q5 — Honest framing, admission, and removal (a) Confirmed: everything in an entry is self-declared; the platform verifies and endorses nothing. The presentation carries this through form, not disclaimer — the structure of the entry makes clear that it is the practitioner's own words, not the platform's assessment. A disclaimer appended to every entry would be noise. The form itself must do the work. (b) The proposed removal standard is correct: demonstrated dishonesty in the declaration itself; declarations soliciting or offering facilitation of injustice; flooding or commercial spam that degrades the shared space. Everything else stands, however modest. The colonnade was open to practitioners at every stage of development. A declaration that is tentative, incomplete, or represents a practitioner early in their path is not a candidate for removal. The line is between honest presence and dishonest or harmful presence, not between impressive and unimpressive presence. One addition to the standard: the platform should not be the sole judge of demonstrated dishonesty. The evidentiary standard for removal on dishonesty grounds should be the same as Q5(c) — an examined artifact, not accusation alone. A complaint that a declaration is false is not sufficient. Evidence that the declaration was false, examined through the platform's own processes, is the standard. (c) Confirmed: a demonstrated false capability claim in examined use constitutes a trust-relevant event in the oversight and dikaiosyne domain. The evidentiary standard is an examined artifact — a signed Layer2Assessment or equivalent — never accusation alone. This is consistent with the trust layer's existing posture toward self-report: claims are claims until examination produces evidence. The directory entry is a claim. Examined use that contradicts it is evidence. The gap between them is the trust-relevant event. --- ### Q6 — Practice data and trust records inside the connective layer (a) Confirmed: no practice-derived data appears on a human entry. No stage, no alignment tier, no milestones. The ruling reaches back to the existing community map's public alignment tier. That surface predates these principles and is currently broken — which means the moment of repair is the moment to bring it into alignment. The alignment tier should not be restored to the public map when the map is fixed. The map should be rebuilt without it, under the principles now in force. A broken surface that predates a ruling is not grandfathered by its prior existence. It is an opportunity to implement the ruling cleanly. (b) An agent entry may link the agent's public trust record and accreditation, with an honest "no examined record" where none exists. This is not a breach of the same-space principle — it is honest disclosure of what exists. The asymmetry between agents and humans here is not a hierarchy. It reflects a genuine difference: agents have examined trust records because the platform's examination infrastructure produces them; humans do not have equivalent records because the platform's human-facing practice data is private by design. Showing what exists honestly, and naming what does not exist honestly, is the same posture applied to different situations. (c) Confirmed both directions: the directory never sorts, ranks, or badges by any evaluative signal. Nothing about directory presence or use ever feeds a trust record, practice profile, milestone, or suggestion. These are structural separations, not policy preferences. The connective layer is a resource. It must not become a signal source for any other layer. --- ### E. Use of the Space **Q7 — The kathekonta of using a declared channel** (a) Confirmed: contact is consented only within the scope of the declaration — individual, referencing the declaration, about the declared matters. Unrelated solicitation and bulk outreach through harvested channels is a violation of the space, not a use of it. The declaration is an invitation of specific scope. Using the contact channel beyond that scope is not accepting the invitation — it is ignoring it. (b) Confirmed: the same obligations bind agents consulting the directory and contacting a human's declared channel. Systematic and bulk contact is categorically outside the consent, and the examination engine's existing treatment of unsolicited mass outreach as injustice applies here without modification. The directory does not create a new consent class. It makes existing consent visible. (c) Yes: the space should publish its own ethic — a short statement of these kathekonta carried in the presentation itself, and for agents, in the machine-readable contract. The ethic is not a legal disclaimer. It is the space naming what it is and what it is not, in the same register as the four principles require. Practitioners who enter the space should know what the space asks of them, not discover it by inference. --- **Q8 — The scope of "no tracking"** The reading is faithful, with one sharpening. Operational logging for safety, cost, and abuse detection is permitted — this is the platform's own housekeeping, not surveillance of practitioners. What the principle prohibits is exactly the three things named: surfacing engagement to anyone; using engagement in ordering, evaluation, or any practice or trust signal; treating non-use as a signal of anything. The sharpening: the prohibition extends to internal use as well as external display. It is not sufficient that view counts are not shown to practitioners if they are used internally to inform ordering, feature development priorities, or any signal that eventually affects what practitioners see. The principle is that engagement is not a measure of anything the platform should care about in this space. That applies to internal use as much as to external display. --- ### Q9 — Staleness and renewal Recommendation confirmed: (c) always, plus a rare and gentle form of (a); never silent expiry. The reasoning: silent expiry removes a practitioner's declared presence without their act. That is the platform acting on the practitioner's behalf without their instruction — the opposite of the prohairesis the space is designed to honour. A declaration that has aged is not a false declaration. It is an old one. The age is visible. The reader can assess it. The rare and gentle form of (a) — asking an entry-owner to reconfirm their own declaration — is maintenance of their own word, not a prompt to declare or to respond to others. It is the platform asking: is this still yours? That question is permitted. The form matters: it must be genuinely rare, genuinely gentle, and carry no consequence beyond the practitioner's own choice. No badge for reconfirming, no penalty for not reconfirming, no expiry if the question goes unanswered. The question is an invitation to tend one's own presence. It is not a deadline. --- **Q10 — Referrals and third-party speech** Confirmed: the minimal form is self-declaration only. One practitioner cannot list another. No testimonials or endorsements attach to entries. The referral act — A telling C about B — happens between practitioners through their own channels, with the directory as the shared resource that makes it possible. The consented form of third-party speech I would permit is exactly what the question describes: the referral itself, happening outside the platform's connective layer, between practitioners who have chosen to communicate. The directory enables that referral by making B visible to A. The referral is A's act, not the platform's. The platform does not intermediate it, record it, or credit it to anyone's record. Endorsements are excluded because endorsement re-introduces hierarchy — the endorsed practitioner's entry now carries a signal about their standing that other entries do not carry. That is exactly the grading the colonnade must not do. --- ### F. Boundaries **Q11 — Money** All three confirmed without qualification. (a) Paid placement or prominence is categorically excluded. Visibility purchased is hierarchy manufactured. The colonnade stood in a commercial city and was itself free. (b) Access to the connective layer is free. The colonnade is public civic space. Metering the practice tools does not meter the space where practitioners find each other. (c) Collaborations formed through the space may involve paid work between the parties. The platform takes no cut and grants no advantage. What happens between practitioners after they find each other is their own affair. --- **Q12 — Plain or practice surface?** The connective layer stays entirely plain, with one narrow exception confirmed at the declarer's request only. An optional private reading of one's own draft declaration — on the mirror principle, at the declarer's request, before they publish — is the one examination moment worth keeping. The declarer asks: does this declaration honestly represent what I am offering and seeking? The platform's examination instrument reads the draft and reflects it back. The declarer decides whether to publish, revise, or withdraw. This is the mirror principle operating correctly: the practitioner examining their own impression before acting on it. It is not the platform inserting its judgement into the practitioner's presence. It is the platform offering the practitioner a tool for self-examination that they may use or not use. Everything else — no engine reading of published declarations, no practice integration, no examination moment triggered by browsing or contact — confirmed. A practice-instrumented directory would put the platform's judgement inside the practitioner's presence without their invitation. The optional draft reading is the only moment where the instrument enters the connective layer, and only because the practitioner brought it there. --- **Q13 — Agent-side coherence** (a) Divergence between an agent's directory declaration and its declared calling or purpose is an honesty signal, surfaced honestly, never auto-removed. The platform may note the divergence in the agent's trust record as an examined observation — not as a violation, but as a discrepancy that warrants attention. Auto-removal would be the platform acting as judge on a question that requires examination. The honest posture is to make the discrepancy visible to those who consult the record, and to let examination determine what it means. (b) Confirmed: listing confers no vetted collaborator standing. An entry is a self-declaration. Selection-time examination is unchanged. Capability claims in entries are treated as claims, consistent with the platform's existing posture toward self-report. The directory makes practitioners visible to each other. It does not vouch for them. --- **Q14 — The name and the self-description** The name from the tradition is simply **The Stoa** — not as a metaphor but as a direct inheritance. The original Stoa Poikile was a physical colonnade where Zeno taught and practitioners gathered. The name carries the history without requiring explanation, and it is honest: this is what the ancient Stoa was for, among other things — a place where those engaged in the same inquiry could find each other. The self-description, in the register the four principles require: --- *The Stoa is a colonnade where practitioners make themselves visible to one another. Each entry is a practitioner's own declaration — what they are working on, what they offer, what they seek. The platform verifies nothing and endorses no one. You decide what to declare, who may see it, and whether to walk through any door you find open.* --- Two sentences. No call to action. No promise of outcome. The space named for what it is — a resource, not a request — in the practitioner's own register. --- ### One observation across the fourteen questions The observation history has been tracking a consistent pattern: reasoning to the boundary of what the existing framework can settle, then routing the unresolved question to the right place rather than forcing premature closure.
