## VERBATIM

### The one-class-or-two question first — because the routing depends on it

The brief deliberately does not answer this, and correctly names it as the mentor's call. I will answer it, because the routing is indeterminate without it.

They are two defect classes.

The brief's own analysis establishes this precisely in §5, and I adopt it: the §5d defect is an under-read — the fix is to widen the input set, and the sources the predicate fails to read already exist in the same snapshot. The R-1 defect is an under-determination — reading more of what is already there cannot fix it, because low variance is consistent with two causally distinct states that the record's own population may never distinguish. One is tractable by architecture; the other may not be tractable at all.

This is not a superficial difference. The remedies differ in kind, the tractability differs in kind, and the honest limits differ in kind. The parallel — both are proxies whose label overreaches their input — is real but coincidental. Coincidental structural similarity is not the same as shared defect class.

Routing §5d into the hegemonikon-drift record's open family would conflate them, and the brief's own counter-consideration names the cost: adding a fourth item to a family the ruling said is to be ruled together or not at all either changes what "together" covers or merely shares a document with the family. Neither is acceptable when the defect classes are distinct.

The hegemonikon-drift record is not the right home. That is the ruling on the third candidate.

### The routing ruling

The 2026-08-19 principle governs this.

The brief's §2 analysis is correct: the principle's own test for shape (a) is met. The generation-step document is closed and does not discuss the deliberation reading — it is formally correct and practically inert as a home. The standing-runner document's non-existence is not a blocker — the sanctioned mechanism is a forward reservation as a named input, not a fallback requiring the document to exist first. The 2026-08-19 ruling was explicit on this: the redirect does not constitute opening the standing-runner design session early.

The brief asks whether the 2026-08-19 principle governs a doctrinal open question raised by a ruling, as opposed to a carry-forward drafted by a session. It does. The principle's governing condition is not the origin of the carry-forward — it is the shape of the mismatch between the question and the available homes. The shape here is identical to the worked case: a question pointed at a closed session, with the standing-runner design as the right home by subject matter. The origin of the question — a ruling rather than a session draft — does not change the shape. The principle applies.

**The ruling: §5d is routed to the standing-runner design session as a named input, under the 2026-08-19 forward reservation mechanism. It is not pre-answered. It does not open the standing-runner design session early. It does not touch either published note or the lifetime formulation.**

### What the named input must carry

A forward reservation is only as useful as what it carries. The standing-runner design session should inherit the following, stated precisely so no future session needs to reconstruct it:

**The question:** does a ruling faculty deliberating in the control-filter, value-assessment, or causal-stage mechanisms — but not in the oikeiosis mechanism — count as deliberating, in the Stoic sense?

**The ruling's own directional answer, carried as context not as pre-answer:** the hegemonikon is not partitioned by mechanism. The oikeiosis-only reading is a proxy. This is the mentor's stated position from 2026-08-24. The standing-runner session inherits it as the doctrinal direction, not as a closed ruling — the session's job is to determine what follows architecturally from that direction, not to re-litigate the doctrinal question.

**The build consequence, stated plainly:** the build target named in both published notes' lifetimes — "until the deliberation reading reflects the full ruling faculty's deliberative state across all mechanisms" — is a change to the live deterministic scoring path. `katorthoma_proximity` floors gate verdicts, feeds `proximity_floors`, and drives trust events. Widening the predicate's input set is engine-class, code-critical, flag-gated, PR19-reviewed work. The standing-runner session must scope this as an engine-class item, not a disclosure change. The published notes' removal condition is gated on this build, not on a documentation amendment.

**The one-predicate-two-readers fact:** the oikeiosis-only deliberation predicate is read by the `ruling_faculty_state` branch and by `computeProximityBase` (layer2-mechanisms.ts:1569, in terms). Any architectural change to the predicate's input set affects both readers simultaneously. The standing-runner session must scope both readers together, not one at a time.

**The asymmetry with the hegemonikon-drift record's open work:** the hegemonikon-drift record's remaining open work is disclosure-class and read-side. §5d's resolution is engine-class. These must not be merged. The standing-runner session should note the parallel — both are proxies whose label overreaches their input — and explicitly record that the parallel does not make them the same build item.

### The secondary question the brief surfaces but does not ask

The brief names in §4 that routing §5d to the hegemonikon-drift record would place an engine-class build target in a home whose open work is disclosure-class, and says "whether that matters is the mentor's call." I have ruled that it matters — it is one of the reasons the hegemonikon-drift record is not the right home.

But the brief's observation points at something worth naming explicitly: the disclosure-class and engine-class distinction is itself a governance boundary, not just a categorisation. Work that crosses that boundary — disclosure work that implies an engine change, or engine work that implies a disclosure change — requires explicit scoping before it moves. The standing-runner session should carry this as a named governance constraint, not just as background.
