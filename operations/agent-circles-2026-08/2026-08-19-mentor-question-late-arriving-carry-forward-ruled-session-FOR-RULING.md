## The question

**When design thinking connects to a scoping session that has already been ruled and closed, what is
the correct path for that connection to actually land — and does "carry it forward" implicitly mean
something different once the target has already closed than it does when the target genuinely has not
opened yet?**

Three shapes seem possible, and we surface them rather than pick one:

**(a) The carry-forward needs its own dated-amendment ruling** against the closed generation-step
document, following the named S8/B1 precedent — a small, explicit ruling that adds the connection to
that document by name, the same way S8's completion-signal requirement was added.

**(b) The carry-forward should be redirected to whichever open or future session is actually
positioned to receive it** — here, plausibly the standing-runner design, since that is the next
unopened item in the Q11 sequence and is where a live runner's cycle behaviour (which is what a
conjectural-entry-illustrating-GS-ATRF-4 connection would actually inform) would be scoped.

**(c) A carry-forward pointed at an already-closed session should simply be treated as void** — not
redirected anywhere automatically, but named as a dropped connection whose original target no longer
exists, to be re-raised fresh (with its own mechanism facts, against whatever session is actually open
at that time) if and when it turns out to still matter.

---

## What we are NOT asking

We are not asking you to rule on the conjectural entry type's content, on GS-ATRF-4's vocabulary, or to
open the generation-step, standing-runner, or ATRF scoping sessions early. We are asking only about the
mechanics of a carry-forward whose target has closed underneath it — a process question, not a content
question, and one this session would rather have a settled answer to than improvise one under the same
pressure that produced the error being corrected.
