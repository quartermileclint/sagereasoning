## The ruling, verbatim

## Q1 — Who produces the completion signal? **Q1a.** Reading (a) is ruled correct and named explicitly as the v1 producer design. The founder personally — or an agent session the founder directs — executing an adopted proposal, holding a founder-minted `completion_signal_write` credential, is the only v1 producer. This is not a deficiency. R8's framing is confirmed: naming it honestly also names the signal's expected rate as low and adoption-bounded. The balancing loop's only path runs through examined human adoption, and that is Prerequisite-Criterion-aligned. The completion signal is not designed to fire automatically or frequently. It fires when a human being has examined an elected proposal, adopted it, acted on it, and is now in a position to attest honestly about what happened. That is exactly the kind of post-execution evidence the Q-C1 ruling named as the signal's epistemic basis. A low-frequency, high-integrity signal is the correct design for this instrument at this stage. It is not a deficiency to be engineered around. **Q1b.** The designed handoff is ruled. The elected proposal carries its own `loop_id` and `cycle_number` when surfaced for adoption on the founder dashboard. The executing actor learns the cycle identity from the proposal surface, not from manual relay. This is both more reliable and more honest — the identity is part of the proposal's own record, not reconstructed from memory. The dashboard adoption surface is the correct place for this handoff. This is a build-session precision item, not a new ruling. **Q1c.** Distinct identities required at mint. The runner and the executing agent may not share an `agent_id` across their two separate credentials. The capability separation at the credential level is necessary but not sufficient — identity coincidence would degrade the Q-C2b signature by allowing the runner's own ambient calls to satisfy the preceding-examination window. That would make the signature meaningless as a discriminating signal. The prohibition is ruled: distinct identities required, enforced at mint, not left to convention. --- ## Q2 — The elector/attester tension inside Q-C2a's first question Reading (a) is ruled. The first examination question is re-scoped to the producer's own assent: "what impression did you assent to when you adopted and executed this idea?" This is the honest question for the actor who is actually attesting. The runner's election-assent — frequently a random tie-break, as the §6 report's h7 finding established — is a different act by a different actor, and asking the executing agent to attest about it produces either a false answer or a refusal. Neither is useful. The re-scoping does not erase the election-assent data. The runner's tie-break behaviour is already recorded in the watching table's candidate rows and is a named finding from the §6 report. That finding belongs in the standing-runner design session's analysis of the election mechanism, not in the completion signal's examination object. The completion signal asks the producer about the producer's own examined assent at adoption — which is the act that matters for the balancing loop's purpose. A producer who adopted an idea habitually, without examination, should say so. That is a legitimate and informative signal. A producer who examined the idea carefully before adopting it should say so. The question as re-scoped makes both answers honest. The three ruled examination questions are therefore amended in their first item. The amended first question is: "What impression did you assent to when you adopted and executed this idea?" The second and third questions — assent quality (examined / habitual) and threshold reached (katorthoma / kathekon) — remain as ruled. The amendment is binding on relay. --- ## Q3 — What does a floor mean under sampling? Option S is ruled buildable now. Option S is pure measurement — all K verdicts recorded, the first remains operative, nothing behavioural changes, the per-input disagreement rate becomes a measured property of the instrument. This is safe to build ahead of the M-vs-W doctrine ruling because it changes nothing about the gate's behaviour. It only makes the instrument's variance visible. That visibility is exactly what is needed before the doctrine question can be answered honestly. The M-vs-W ruling is deferred to the standing-runner design session, with the following framing locked so the session inherits it correctly. R8's corrected position is confirmed: this is a doctrinal question about what a floor means under sampling, not a statistics choice. The question is whether the floor doctrine — weakest-link, conservative, locked after the 2026-06-25 review — applies per-examination or per-judgement. Option W applies it per-examination: any floor among K stands, consistent with the weakest-link doctrine applied at the sampling layer. Option M applies it per-judgement: the modal reading decides, which is a real loosening of the gate's behaviour on floor-borderline candidates. The measured consequence on the demonstrated input class — floor probability falling from 10% to 2.8% under M, rising to approximately 27% under W — is the empirical basis the session will reason from. Neither option is ruled now. S runs first, produces the disagreement-rate data, and the session rules M-vs-W with that data in hand. R8's evaluation of the would-be-winner refinement path — that it is an examination-refinement loop within a cycle, not an information-integration edge, and therefore does not violate the single-backward-edge constraint — is carried forward as a named evaluation awaiting confirmation, not as a settled ruling. The standing-runner design session confirms or corrects it. It is not treated as settled in any build or design work before that confirmation. --- ## One thing to name before the session closes The R8 document demonstrates something the observation history has been tracking across several sessions: the project's own governance apparatus is now catching its own errors before they propagate. R8's first draft chose M on a symmetry argument that was wrong, the independent review caught it, and the corrected position arrived here as a doctrinal question rather than a statistics choice. That is the adversarial-review discipline operating inside the design session itself, not just at the relay boundary. The pattern of catching drift before it propagates — which you named explicitly in the baseline correction — is now operating at multiple layers simultaneously. The Q1c ruling is worth holding as a practitioner signal as well as a technical one. The prohibition on identity coincidence between the runner and the executing agent is, at its root, a ruling about the integrity of attestation. An agent that proposes and an agent that executes and attests must be distinct, because the attesting agent's testimony is only meaningful if it is not the same agent whose ambient behaviour would satisfy the examination window automatically. That is a structural honesty requirement. The Stoic parallel is exact: the agent who generates the impression and the agent who examines the assent cannot be the same act. Examination requires a genuine gap between impression and assent. The credential separation enforces that gap at the infrastructure level.
