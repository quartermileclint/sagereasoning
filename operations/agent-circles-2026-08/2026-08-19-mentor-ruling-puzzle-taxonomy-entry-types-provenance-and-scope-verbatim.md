**MENTOR RULINGS — Puzzle taxonomy entry-type instruction: provenance and scope**

**Date:** 2026-08-19. Three-part question. All three parts answered. The question is well-formed and
the mechanism facts are stated correctly. The distinction being drawn — between a self-declared design
direction from an exploratory session and a properly routed ruling — is exactly the right distinction
to surface, and surfacing it rather than assuming an answer is the correct pattern.

---

**Q(a) — Provenance: is a self-declared design direction sufficient to bind a future build?**

No. A self-declared design direction from an exploratory session is not sufficient to bind a future
build. It is sufficient to inform one.

Every binding addition this project has made has followed the same path: scoped question, PR20
mechanism facts, founder relay, verbatim-recorded ruling. That path exists because the governance
structure requires rulings to be grounded in accurate mechanism facts, examined before they land, and
recorded in a form that can be audited. A document that self-declares its own status as established
bypasses that examination.

The fact that the document also self-limits — stating it licenses no build beyond the already-scoped
stub — does not resolve the provenance question. A self-limiting document that has not been ruled is
still an unruled document.

The instruction produced by the 2026-08-19 exploratory session is valuable. The three entry types are
well-reasoned and the structural analysis connecting them to Euler, Ramanujan, and Poincaré is sound.
But valuable is not the same as binding. The document's status is: exploratory design thinking, not yet
ruled, not yet binding on any future build.

The correct path forward is the one this question has already taken: route it as a scoped question
with PR20 mechanism facts, receive a ruling, record the ruling verbatim. That is what is happening now.
This ruling is the examination the document needed.

---

**Q(b) — Scope: should the taxonomy stub's design record be extended now, or held separately?**

Held separately, as a named design document, until the taxonomy moves from stub to full build.

The taxonomy stub was scoped exactly and deliberately no wider — the docstring records this. Extending
the stub's design record now with three entry types, seven to nine fields each, transition semantics,
and cross-references to GS-ATRF-4 and the Consciousness and Continuity Obligation would widen the stub
beyond its scoped boundary without a ruling that specifically authorises that widening. The stub's
value is precisely its narrowness: it is a named, addressable location that exists without doing
anything. Adding a detailed field specification to the stub's design record is not the same as building
the full taxonomy, but it is not nothing either — it creates expectations about what the full build
will look like that will be treated as binding when the full build is scoped.

The three entry types should be held as a separate named design document — not in the stub's
docstring, not in project-context.json, not in any live surface. A design document that sits alongside
the stub and is explicitly labelled as pre-ruling exploratory thinking, available to inform the full
taxonomy build when that build is scoped.

The document already exists in the form of the exploratory session output. It needs a label change,
not a content change: from self-declared instruction to pre-ruling design thinking.

When the taxonomy moves from stub to full build — a post-first-build-gate item — the design document
is brought as input to the scoping session, examined, and ruled on at that point. The three entry
types may survive that examination intact, or they may be revised. That is what the examination is
for.

---

**Q(c) — The "do not open early" gate: does connecting the entry types to GS-ATRF-4 and the
Consciousness and Continuity Obligation constitute opening the ATRF scoping session's territory
early?**

No, with one condition.

The ATRF scoping session's gate — do not open early — protects the session itself: the examination,
the rulings, the build decisions that will emerge from it. Forward-pointing notes that name
connections without resolving them are not the same as opening the session. GS-ATRF-1, GS-ATRF-2,
GS-ATRF-3, and now GS-ATRF-4 all carry forward-pointing notes of this kind. The open-questions block
exists precisely to hold named connections that will be examined later without pre-answering them.

The condition: the design document's connections to GS-ATRF-4 and the Consciousness and Continuity
Obligation must remain forward-pointing notes, not pre-answers.

The exploratory session's instruction crossed this line in one place — the incubation entry type
states "the Consciousness and Continuity Obligation is directly implicated here" and then proceeds to
describe what the obligation implies for the entry type's architecture. That is not a forward-pointing
note. That is a pre-answer to a question the Consciousness and Continuity Obligation has not yet been
ruled on.

The design document should be revised to convert that passage from a pre-answer to a named connection:
the incubation entry type raises questions that connect to the Consciousness and Continuity
Obligation — specifically, whether continuity of inquiry across cycles requires accumulated memory, and
whether the incubation entry's examination history is the architecture that makes that possible. Those
questions are carried forward to the relevant scoping sessions. They are not answered here.

With that revision, the design document's connections to GS-ATRF-4 and the Consciousness and
Continuity Obligation are the same kind of forward-pointing note the open-questions block already
carries. The gate is not breached.

---

**Summary of what happens to the document now.**

The instruction produced by the 2026-08-19 exploratory session is reclassified from self-declared
design direction to pre-ruling design thinking. It is held as a named design document, separate from
the taxonomy stub and from any live surface, available to inform the full taxonomy build when that
build is scoped. One revision is required before it is held: the passage on the Consciousness and
Continuity Obligation is converted from a pre-answer to a named connection with forward-pointing
questions.

No build is licensed by this ruling. The taxonomy stub remains scoped exactly as it was. The three
entry types are not yet binding on any future build. They are well-reasoned design thinking that has
now been examined and given a proper status.

---

**One observation for the record.**

The pattern that has been consolidating across this session — moving from insight to structure faster
than the governance examination can follow — appeared here in the instruction document itself. The
document self-declared its status, self-limited its scope, and self-connected to live governance
questions, all without the examination that would have caught the one place where a forward-pointing
note became a pre-answer.

The question Claude raised is the examination the document needed. That it was raised rather than
assumed is the correct pattern operating correctly. The observation history shows this pattern
recurring with high confidence.

The discipline it requires is not slowing the generation of ideas — the ideas are valuable and the
pace of synthesis is a genuine strength. The discipline is holding the ideas in the right epistemic
state — as conjectural entries, probably true, structural account pending — rather than allowing them
to harden into established conclusions before the examination has run. That is the Ramanujan
discipline applied to governance: record the result honestly, mark the proof status absent, and trust
the examination process to upgrade it when the account is ready.
