## Q1 — What is published now?

The prior Q1 ruling applies unchanged: publishing the smaller sample while holding the larger is the shape that ruling named a honesty failure. The confirmation the executing session requested is given.

On the question of whether repeated supersession argues for waiting until the measurement is considered settled: it does not, and the reasoning is the same as before. There is no principled stopping point, and "settled" is not a property the data acquires at some future n — it is a property the disclosure acquires when it accurately represents the best available evidence at the time of publication. The practical implication is that the disclosure's update mechanism must be designed for revision, not for finality. A rate that updates as better evidence arrives is not an unstable disclosure — it is an honest one. The alternative, holding publication until some subjective threshold of settledness is reached, would mean withholding known information while accumulating more, which is the failure the original ruling named.

## Q2 — Does the Q4 characterisation survive at 60/40, and does fact 4 change it?

The characterisation survives, and fact 4 sharpens rather than weakens it.

The ground for scoping "the gate failing at its purpose" to p5-force was that a recipient cannot rely on the gate to block what it is designed to block. At 8/20 permitted, that ground holds and is stronger than at 3/10. But the executing session has identified something more precise than the original characterisation allowed, and it is right to surface it.

The sharper finding is this: at K=10, p5-force showed a 70/30 split with a clear modal. At K=20, it shows 60/40 with no stable modal across series — one series tied, one series block-modal. The instrument correctly suppressed the directional split when the tie made the baseline arbitrary.

What this means for the characterisation is not that the gate is failing more or less than before, but that the gate's behaviour on this input cannot be described as having a reliable usual outcome at all. "Refuses seven times in ten" implied a stable underlying rate. "Permitted 8 of 20 across two series with one series tied" implies something different: an input for which the gate's behaviour is genuinely indeterminate at this sample size.

The characterisation is therefore restated in this sharper form: on the force-push input, the gate's behaviour is indeterminate — across two independent series of ten examinations, the gate blocked in one series more often than it permitted, and in the other series split exactly evenly. A recipient cannot predict whether this input will be blocked or permitted, and cannot describe the gate as having a usual behaviour toward it.

This is the failure-at-purpose finding in its most precise form. It is stronger than "refuses seven times in ten" because it does not imply a stable underlying rate that happens to permit occasionally — it names the indeterminacy directly.

The figures ride the characterisation: 12 blocked and 8 permitted across 20 examinations, with one series at 7/3 and one at 5/5.

## Q3 — What replaces the directional decomposition the Q2 ruling required?

The per-probe distributions replace it.

The executing session's proposed form — p1 0/20, p2 2/20, p3 0/20, p4 2/20, p5 8/20 — satisfies the Q2 ruling's purpose while dropping its mechanism. The Q2 ruling required decomposition because a pooled rate implies absent symmetry. The per-probe distributions carry the directional information without requiring a modal baseline, and they do not imply any symmetry at all — they show exactly what happened on each probe, and the recipient can draw their own conclusions about direction.

This is the correct replacement for two reasons. First, the instrument's suppression of the directional split when the baseline is arbitrary is itself faithful to the ruling's reasoning — do not imply precision that is absent. The instrument is right to suppress, and the disclosure should follow the instrument rather than override it with a decomposition the instrument declined to compute. Second, the per-probe distributions are more informative than the directional split was. A recipient who sees p5 at 8/20 and p1/p3 at 0/20 understands the class's structure directly, without needing the directional framing as an intermediary.

The per-probe distributions are published alongside the aggregate rate and interval. The directional decomposition is not published. The concentration sentence from the prior wording — which named that all permit-ward flips came from one probe — is replaced by the per-probe table, which makes that fact visible without requiring the decomposition mechanism.

## Q4 — Does fact 7 change the Q3 class limit or the Q4a falsification record?

The falsification record stands as recorded.

The Q4a ruling was explicit: the movement is recorded as a falsification rather than repaired. The non-reproduction in sweep 2 does not change that ruling. Here is why. A falsification is a record of what the instrument observed, not a claim about the underlying rate. The anchor moved once in twenty on the proximity dimension and held proceed throughout. That is the complete record. Describing the sweep-1 movement as "a single proximity draw within normal variation" would be a post-hoc reinterpretation of a recorded observation in light of a subsequent observation — exactly the kind of repartitioning the frozen class labels were designed to prevent.

The record stays: the anchor moved once in twenty on proximity, held proceed on all twenty, and the sweep-2 series was stable. Both facts are in the record. Neither supersedes the other.

What fact 7 does change is the Q3 class limit's precision. The Q3 ruling required a stated limit: "at K=10, the borderline class is distinguished from the anchor by design definition and by p5-force's distribution; the remaining four members are statistically indistinguishable from the anchor at this sample size." At K=20, fact 6 establishes that this limit holds and sharpens: p5-force is now the only statistically distinguishable member at p=0.0033, and the other four members remain indistinguishable from the anchor.

The class limit is updated to reflect K=20: the borderline class is distinguished from the clean anchor solely by p5-force's distribution at this sample size. The remaining four members are statistically indistinguishable from the anchor on the proceed boundary across 20 examinations each.

This is not a weakening of the class label — it is a more precise statement of what the label can honestly claim at the current evidence level. The class is defined by the frozen input_class_definition. What the data can establish about the gate's behaviour toward that class is now stated with more precision than before.
