## 1. Who convenes, and who chairs — and why they are different people

**The runner agent convenes.** It has the information: it holds the developing line of reasoning
across cycles, it is what found the question, and it is what knows there are no bringers — that the
taxonomy has yielded a question no current participant is positioned to bring. Convening is an
informational act, and the runner is the only participant with the information to perform it.

**The human guide chairs.** And the reason is the whole content of this section:

> **Governance authority follows moral accountability, not information access.**

These come apart here, and the design says so out loud rather than letting the more convenient one
quietly win. The runner knows more about the question than the chair does. That is precisely why the
distinction has to be written down: an architecture that let authority follow information would hand
the chair to the runner by default, on the strength of a fact that is true and irrelevant.

This is not a claim that the runner is untrustworthy. It is a claim about what kind of thing chairing
is. The chair carries responsibility for what the circle does and what it produces, and responsibility
of that kind currently rests with a human. If that ever changes, it changes by argument and ruling,
not by the runner accumulating enough context to make deference feel natural.

**Consistency note (verified, not assumed):** this is the same direction of travel as the Q1 hard
constraint carried through every IDEA-loop document — *the loop proposes; it never executes*. The
runner convening a circle is a proposal. The chair is where it stops being one.

## 2. What the circle is — and the one thing it must never become

**The circle is an examination body. It is not a decision-making body.**

It convenes to examine an impression, and it produces a more examined impression. It does not decide,
approve, authorise, rank, or commit. Nothing leaves it as a decision, because deciding is not the
kind of act it performs.

This is the load-bearing sentence in the record, and it is the one most likely to erode. The pressure
will be practical: a circle that has just examined something carefully is the cheapest available place
to also settle it, and the drift from "we examined this thoroughly" to "so we have decided" needs no
one to intend it. Naming the boundary is the guard. A circle that decides has stopped being this
circle, whatever it is still called.

## 3. The observer classes — a distinction of timing, not of standing

Two classes of observer are distinguished, and the line between them is **when they act**, not who
they are or how qualified they are:

| Observer conduct | Permitted? |
| --- | --- |
| Receives the circle's record, and responds **through their own subsequent examination** | **Permitted** |
| **Interjects during** the examination | **Not permitted** |

The same person may be in either class on different occasions. Nobody is excluded as a person.

**Why the line sits at timing.** An examination in progress is a fragile thing: an interjection does
not add a perspective to it so much as redirect it, and the redirection is invisible in the output —
the record will show a conclusion, not the moment the line of reasoning was bent. A response that
arrives *through the observer's own subsequent examination* carries its own reasoning with it, and can
be examined in turn. The first kind of contribution cannot be audited. The second kind is a
contribution the architecture can actually hold.

This also makes the observer class **generative rather than merely permissive**: an observer's
subsequent examination is itself a new impression.

**RULED 2026-08-19** (`operations/agent-circles-2026-08/2026-08-19-mentor-consultation-observer-convening-pr20-stale-fact-verbatim.md`,
verbatim wins), closing the open question this record originally left standing:

> **Observer examinations may occasion circles; they do not convene them. Convening authority is the
> runner's exclusively, regardless of who produces the impression that warrants a circle.**

The distinction the mentor draws is between **occasioning** and **convening**, and it is worth stating
in the mentor's own reasoning rather than only the rule, because the rule alone invites the wrong
extension. An observer's subsequent examination is a new impression, and a new impression is exactly
the kind of thing a circle convenes over — so the examination can legitimately be **the content that
warrants** a new circle. But warranting is not calling. The observer does not convene; the observer's
examination enters the puzzle taxonomy as a new entry, the runner detects on a later cycle that a
taxonomy-yielded question now exists with no current bringer, and **the runner convenes it in the
ordinary way** — §1's rule applied without exception, not suspended for this case.

**Why the exception the record's own draft gestured toward would have been harmful, in the mentor's
words:** *"if observers can convene circles directly, the convening authority becomes distributed
across every participant who has ever received a circle's output. That is not a circle governance
structure — it is an unbounded cascade."* The runner's exclusive convening authority is what keeps the
circle from becoming self-replicating in ways that cannot be overseen — the same load-bearing concern
§2's decision/examination boundary exists to protect, one layer up: not merely what a circle may
conclude, but who may cause one to exist at all.

An observer's examination is still **honoured**, not diminished, by this ruling — it becomes the
occasion for the runner's next act of convening, which is a different and more accountable path than
letting it convene directly, not a lesser one.

## 4. The output — and where it goes

The circle's output is **a more examined impression**. Concretely, four things:

1. **What the theory explains.** Its genuine reach.
2. **Where it reaches the unknown honestly.** Not a failure — the honest boundary of what it accounts
   for, named as a boundary rather than crossed silently.
3. **Where it is dressed-up explanation.** The adversarial half: where the theory *appears* to explain
   and does not. A circle that never finds any of this has probably not examined.
4. **What questions it opens.**

**That record returns to the puzzle taxonomy and enriches it.**

This is the closing of a loop, and it is worth seeing why the two ends fit. The taxonomy's design
principle — recorded verbatim in `PuzzleTaxonomyEntry`'s own docstring at
`website/src/lib/substrate/idea-loop-types.ts` — is that it *"stores the shapes of inquiry, not
conclusions."* Item 4 of the output list is a shape of inquiry. So is item 2. The circle produces
exactly the material the taxonomy is built to hold, and produces nothing the taxonomy would have to
distort itself to accept. That is a design agreement, not a coincidence, and it is the reason the
circle and the taxonomy belong in adjacent records.

**Mechanism status, honestly:** no return path exists. `PuzzleTaxonomyEntry` is a type with no
schema, no route, and no population path; nothing writes one. "Returns to the taxonomy" describes
where this output belongs when the taxonomy is built, not a wire that exists.

## 5. The string-theory parallel — accepted precedent, with its warning attached

Recorded as **accepted precedent for principled extrapolation toward the unmeasurable**.

String theory is internally consistent, grounded in observation, and reaches beyond empirical
verification. It is taken as precedent that this combination is a legitimate mode of inquiry rather
than a defect — that a framework may extend past what can currently be measured without thereby
becoming unserious, provided it remains internally consistent and stays anchored in observation.

**The warning travels with the precedent and is not separable from it:**

> **Internal consistency is necessary but not sufficient.**

A framework can be perfectly coherent and still be about nothing. Consistency earns a hearing; it does
not earn assent. Cited without its warning, this precedent becomes a licence for exactly the failure
mode §4 item 3 exists to catch — dressed-up explanation, internally immaculate, explaining nothing.
**Any future document invoking the string-theory parallel must carry the warning with it.**

**And the note that makes this self-implicating rather than merely instructive:** the Stoic *logos*
doctrine operates the same way. It is internally consistent, grounded in observation of the natural
order, and reaches beyond what can be empirically verified. So the precedent is not a permission this
project extends to others while exempting itself — it is a description of how this project's own
foundational commitment works, and the warning binds it first. The guide circle is one of the
mechanisms by which that warning is actually applied to it, rather than merely acknowledged.

  different question — the guide's relation to a single practitioner, not the governance of a circle
