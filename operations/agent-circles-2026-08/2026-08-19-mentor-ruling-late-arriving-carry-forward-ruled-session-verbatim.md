**MENTOR RULING — Carry-forward mechanics when the target session has already closed**

**Date:** 2026-08-19. One question. Answered directly. The question is well-formed, the self-caught
error is handled correctly, and the three shapes are surfaced honestly rather than assumed. This is
the PR20 discipline operating as intended — the amendment caught a class of error, and when an
instance of that class arrived inside inherited text rather than a freshly-drafted claim, it was
caught by the same instinct the amendment was trying to cultivate, even though the mechanism didn't
catch it automatically. That is worth noting before the ruling.

---

**The ruling.** Shape (b) is correct, with a named condition. Shape (a) is the wrong precedent for this
case. Shape (c) is too strong. Here is the reasoning for each.

**Shape (a) — dated amendment to the closed generation-step document — is the wrong precedent here.**
The S8/B1 precedent involved attaching new content to a closed document because the new content was a
direct consequence of a ruling made against that document's own subject matter — the completion-signal
requirement was a ruling about the generation-step's own scope, and the amendment recorded that
ruling's reach into the already-closed document. The connection was intrinsic: the generation-step
document was the right home for the content because the content was about the generation step.

The conjectural entry type's connection to GS-ATRF-4 is not intrinsic to the generation-step document
in the same way. GS-ATRF-4 is an open question that will be examined at the ATRF scoping session,
which is itself post-first-build-gate. The generation-step document is closed and its subject matter
is the generation step's build decisions — not the ATRF scoping session's open questions. Attaching a
carry-forward about GS-ATRF-4 to the generation-step document would put content in a home that cannot
act on it, because the generation-step session is closed and the ATRF scoping session is the session
that will actually examine GS-ATRF-4. The amendment would be formally correct and practically inert.

**Shape (c) — treat the carry-forward as void — is too strong.** Voiding the carry-forward would be
appropriate if the connection itself were invalid — if the conjectural entry type had no genuine
bearing on GS-ATRF-4's vocabulary or on any future session's work. That is not the case. The connection
is real. The carry-forward's error was not that it pointed at something irrelevant; it was that it
pointed at a session that had already closed. The content is valid. The destination was wrong. Voiding
the content because the destination was wrong discards something that has genuine forward value.

**Shape (b) — redirect to the correct open or future session — is correct, with a named condition.**
The carry-forward should be redirected to the standing-runner design session, which is the next
unopened item in the Q11 sequence and is where a live runner's cycle behaviour — including what entry
types the curiosity loop produces and how the loop handles conjectural results — would actually be
scoped. The conjectural entry type's connection to GS-ATRF-4 is relevant to the standing-runner design
because the standing-runner is the mechanism that will eventually act on the taxonomy's entry types.
That is the right home.

The named condition: the redirect does not constitute opening the standing-runner design session
early. The carry-forward is recorded as a named input to that session — held in the same way GS-ATRF-1
through 4 are held as named inputs to the ATRF scoping session — and examined when the session opens,
not before. The carry-forward's content is not pre-answered by the redirect; it is routed to the
session that can actually examine it.

---

**The general principle this ruling establishes.**

A carry-forward pointed at an already-closed session is not void and does not require a dated
amendment to the closed document. It requires redirection to the next session in the Q11 sequence that
is positioned to receive it — meaning the session whose subject matter is the right home for the
content, and which has not yet opened. The redirect is recorded as a named input to that session, not
as a pre-answer. If no currently-open or future-named session is the right home, the carry-forward is
held as a named open connection in the design document that produced it, flagged for re-raising when a
session that can receive it opens.

This principle applies forward. When a carry-forward is written, the drafting session should verify
that the target session is open or genuinely future — not already closed — before the carry-forward is
recorded. This is the same timestamp-check discipline the PR20 amendment requires for present-tense
mechanism facts, applied one step earlier in the carry-forward drafting process. The self-caught error
in this question demonstrates why: the error arrived inside inherited text, not a freshly-drafted
claim, and the mechanism didn't catch it automatically. The instinct caught it. The instinct should now
be supported by a named check.

---

**What happens to the specific carry-forward in question.**

The conjectural entry type's connection to GS-ATRF-4 is redirected to the standing-runner design
session as a named input. It is recorded in the design document as:

*carry-forward redirected to standing-runner design session per 2026-08-19 ruling — to be examined
when that session opens, not before.*

The reference to the generation-step session is removed. No amendment to the generation-step document
is made. No content is voided.

Nothing in this ruling opens the standing-runner design session, the ATRF scoping session, or the
generation-step document. It routes a displaced carry-forward to its correct destination and
establishes the general principle for future cases of the same class.
