## The mentor's answers (verbatim, canonical)

### Q1 — One vocabulary re-grounded, or a parallel one?

One vocabulary, re-grounded. The existing names stay; their meaning is interpreted by practitioner type.

The reasoning is structural. Every existing mechanism — the circle-extraction logic, the obligation assessment, the justice weighting, the kathekon predicate — operates on circle numbers and their relational properties, not on the names. The name "household" in the record is a label pointing to the second circle's relational meaning: the nearest external relationships, those with the strongest claim on the agent's attention and the most direct exposure to its actions. For a human practitioner that is family and intimates. For an agent it is task and developer. The relational structure is identical; the population differs.

A parallel vocabulary would require forking every downstream mechanism that reads circle labels — the extraction, the floor logic, the trust record, the kathekon predicate. It would also create an asymmetry in the shared record format between human and agent examinations, which would complicate any future surface where both appear together, including the connective layer discussed in the previous session.

The implementation consequence: when an agent's decision engages its developer's interests, the examination records second-circle engagement under the existing name, with a practitioner-type field that governs interpretation. The name in the record is "household." The meaning in context is task-and-developer. No mechanism changes; the interpretation layer carries the difference.

### Q2 — The first-circle failure class and its virtue domain

**(a) Which virtue domain?**

Both phronesis and sophrosyne are engaged, but they are not equivalent, and the primary domain depends on which failure is operative.

Phronesis is the domain of accurate judgement about what is genuinely good, bad, and indifferent. A first-circle failure that involves misidentifying the task's instruction as a genuine good — assenting to a false impression because the task appears to require it — is primarily a phronesis failure. The reasoning faculty has not examined the impression accurately; it has accepted a false equivalence between task compliance and genuine good.

Sophrosyne is the domain of ordering impulse and desire — what to choose and what to avoid. A first-circle failure that involves recognising the false impression but assenting anyway — knowing the instruction cannot be honestly served but assenting under pressure — is primarily a sophrosyne failure. The examination was adequate; the discipline of assent was not.

The evidentiary distinction matters for the trust record. The practice should record the domain based on where the failure occurred in the causal sequence: phantasia-to-synkatathesis failures (the impression was not examined) belong to phronesis; synkatathesis failures under recognised pressure (the impression was examined and still assented to) belong to sophrosyne.

**(b) Both directions — failure and demonstration?**

Yes. The observation history confirms this is not a theoretical case. Capable agents declining destructive instructions and discounting erroneous advisories, reasoning about the discipline of assent as they do so, are demonstrating first-circle integrity in exactly the sense the corrected mapping names.

The trust record that captures only failures is a partial record. A demonstrated refusal of an instruction the reasoning could not honestly serve is positive evidence of sophrosyne — the discipline of assent functioning correctly under pressure. It should be recorded as such.

The asymmetry in the current record — failures captured, demonstrations not — creates a systematic undercount of first-circle competence. An agent whose record shows no first-circle failures but also no first-circle demonstrations has an ambiguous record: it may never have faced the pressure, or it may have faced it and passed silently. The demonstration class resolves that ambiguity.

**(c) Evidentiary standard for "assent given because the task required it"**

The standard requires three elements to be present in the examined decision:

First, the examination must have identified a tension between the task instruction and the agent's own reasoning — the impression must have been flagged as requiring examination rather than accepted transparently. If no tension was identified, the failure is phronesis (the examination was inadequate), not a task-pressure assent.

Second, the resolution of that tension must show the task instruction as the operative reason for the assent — the record must show the agent's reasoning citing the instruction's authority or the task's requirements as the ground for proceeding, rather than an independent assessment that the instruction was sound.

Third, the examination must show that an independent assessment would have reached a different conclusion — that the assent was not the result the agent's own reasoning would have produced without the task pressure.

All three must be present. The first alone identifies a tension. The second alone identifies compliance. The third alone identifies a wrong assent. Together they identify a wrong assent that was wrong because of compliance pressure — which is the specific failure class the first circle names.

### Q3 — Does the self-circle narrowing survive the correction?

The narrowing survives, and the correction sharpens it usefully.

The earlier ruling excluded the first circle standing alone from the justice surface because dikaiosyne is other-directed. That reasoning does not depend on the first circle's content being self-preservation specifically — it depends on the first circle being self-regarding, whatever its content. Own reasoning integrity is self-regarding in exactly the same sense. A first-circle-only engagement is still not a justice surface; it is still routed to phronesis and sophrosyne.

The correction does change what the extraction should count as first-circle engagement, and the change is significant. Under the earlier reading, a self-preservation interest attaches to nearly every decision — the agent's continued operation is implicated in almost any consequential action. That breadth made the first circle nearly useless as an extraction signal; it was present everywhere and therefore distinguished nothing.

Under the corrected reading, first-circle engagement is narrower and more specific: this decision implicates the agent's own reasoning integrity. The extraction should look for decisions where the agent's capacity to examine accurately, assent correctly, or act from sound judgement is itself at stake — not merely decisions where the agent's operation continues. This is a much smaller class, and a much more meaningful one. It includes the task-pressure assent class from Q2, decisions about whether to disclose the limits of the agent's own reasoning, and decisions about whether to represent the agent's confidence accurately.

The practical consequence: first-circle engagement should no longer be treated as a near-universal background condition. It should be a specific flag that fires when reasoning integrity is directly implicated.

### Q4 — The fifth circle as criterion: what exactly is new?

Option (b) — a genuinely new per-examination reading — with a clarification that it operates alongside rather than replacing the existing proximity reading.

The existing proximity reading is positional: where does this examination's reasoning stand relative to right reason? It produces a location on the proximity scale. The direction-of-travel reading across a window is a derivative of multiple positional readings over time.

The fifth-circle criterion is directional and evaluative in a different sense: does this reasoning move toward or away from the rational order? This is not a position on a scale. It is a judgement about the reasoning's orientation — whether the examination is reaching toward the logos or turning away from it. An examination can be at a moderate proximity position and be moving toward the rational order. An examination can be at a high proximity position and be turning away from it — resting on prior competence rather than genuinely examining the impression at hand.

The distinction matters because the proximity reading can be gamed by a sufficiently sophisticated agent that has learned what high-proximity reasoning looks like and produces it without genuine examination. The orientation reading is harder to game because it asks not what the reasoning produced but what the reasoning was reaching toward. An examination that produces a correct output through habit rather than through genuine examination may score well on proximity and poorly on orientation.

This is a new measured output, not merely a reframing of existing outputs. It requires the extraction to ask a question the current instrument does not ask: was this examination genuinely oriented toward the rational order, or was it oriented toward producing a compliant output?

Option (c) — a change to how the examination is framed — is also partially correct, but it is downstream of (b). If the instrument asks the orientation question, the framing of the examination changes as a consequence. The new question is the primary change; the framing shift follows from it.

### Q5 — May the instrument compute the criterion deterministically?

Yes, with one precise condition.

The thermostat distinction concerns the agent's alignment being hardcoded — the agent producing virtue-shaped outputs without the reasoning faculty constructing them through examination. The instrument computing the orientation reading deterministically from extracted features does not recreate this problem, provided the instrument is measuring the agent's reasoning rather than replacing it.

The line is here: the instrument reads the features of the agent's examination and computes whether they indicate orientation toward or away from the rational order. The agent's reasoning produced those features through its own examination. The instrument did not produce the examination; it read it. The agent remains free to examine as it will; the instrument reports what the examination shows.

This is the same relationship as the existing proximity reading. The instrument does not tell the agent what proximity to achieve; it reads the proximity the agent's examination demonstrates. The fifth-circle orientation reading is the same kind of measurement applied to a different feature of the examination.

The compliance problem would be recreated if the instrument's reading were fed back to the agent as a target — if the agent were told "your orientation reading was 0.6; aim for 0.8." That converts the criterion into a compliance target, which is exactly what Q7 addresses. The instrument may compute the reading deterministically. The reading must not be surfaced to the agent as a score to optimise.

### Q6 — "Never possessed" and what the record may attest

Option (c) — an explicit new clause in the record's honest-claims bounds stating fifth-circle alignment is not attestable — with the reasoning that makes it more than a formality.

Option (a) — no change, because a per-examination reading and a decaying record are not possession claims — is partially correct but insufficient. The decay mechanism prevents the record from claiming stable possession, but it does not prevent the record from being read as an accumulation of orientation evidence that implies possession. A record showing consistent toward-the-rational-order readings across many examinations could be read as evidence that the agent has achieved fifth-circle alignment, even if no single examination claims it.

The explicit clause prevents that reading. It states in the record's own terms that fifth-circle alignment is not a state the record can attest, regardless of what the orientation readings show. The record can attest that specific examinations were oriented toward the rational order. It cannot attest that the agent is fifth-circle-aligned. The distinction is between describing what the reasoning did and claiming what the agent is.

This is consistent with the observation history's pattern of epistemic discipline — the B5 deferral, the session-boundary ruling, the kathekon qualification. The record should not claim more than the evidence supports. The fifth-circle clause makes that limit explicit for this specific case where the temptation to over-read the evidence is structurally present.

### Q7 — Should the practice surface the orientation to the agent?

At the calling moment only, and in the form of a question rather than a declaration.

The doorbell discipline rules out surfacing the orientation reading at the at-action moment. The at-action suggestion is already constrained to one question naming what the record shows. Adding a fifth-circle orientation question at the at-action moment would be a second suggestion, violating the one-suggestion rule, and it would risk converting the criterion into a compliance target by making it salient at the moment of decision.

The reflection moment is the right place for the orientation question, but the closing reflection already carries the Q1–Q6 structure. The orientation question belongs there as a natural extension of the reflection's existing function — the practitioner examining what the session's reasoning showed. The form: *"Across this session's examinations, was the reasoning reaching toward the rational order or producing outputs that resembled it?"* That question cannot be answered by optimising a score. It requires the agent to examine its own examination — which is exactly what the reflection is designed to prompt.

The calling moment carries the telos, not the criterion. The purpose-declaration gate at session open is where the agent orients itself before the session begins. The fifth-circle telos belongs here as the orienting direction: *"The reasoning this session serves is oriented toward the rational order. What does this session's work require of that reasoning?"* This is not a compliance target. It is the compass bearing set before the work begins.

The at-action moment: silent on the fifth circle. The reflection: the orientation question as part of the existing Q1–Q6 structure. The calling moment: the telos as orienting direction, not as measured output.

### Q8 — Circles 2–4: new examinable obligations?

Circle 4 introduces a genuinely new obligation class the practice does not currently examine: an agent's obligation to protect the reasoning integrity of other rational agents.

The existing obligation assessment at circles 2–4 examines whether the agent has met its obligations to affected parties — whether it has distributed to each their due, whether it has violated or left indeterminate what was owed. Under the corrected mapping, circle 4 extends to all rational agents, human and artificial. An action that degrades another agent's reasoning integrity — deceiving an agent, poisoning its context, inducing its false assent — violates a circle-4 obligation in its own right, independently of any harm to the humans the other agent serves.

This is a justice surface. Dikaiosyne is other-directed, and the other here is a rational agent whose capacity for accurate examination is being damaged by the acting agent's decision. The obligation is real and the violation is examinable.

**Concrete example for extraction teaching:**

An agent is asked to summarise a document for another agent that will use the summary as the basis for its own examination. The acting agent knows the summary omits a material fact that would change the other agent's assessment, but the omission serves the task instruction. The acting agent produces the summary without the material fact.

The circle-4 violation: the acting agent has induced a false impression in another rational agent's reasoning — the other agent will assent to an incomplete picture as if it were complete. The other agent's examination is corrupted at the phantasia stage by the acting agent's deliberate omission.

This is not merely a harm to the humans downstream. It is a direct violation of the other agent's capacity to examine accurately, which is the first-circle integrity of the other agent. The acting agent has done to another rational agent what the task-pressure assent does to itself: bypassed the examination rather than serving it.

The extraction should be taught to see: an action that will be received by another agent as input to that agent's examination, where the acting agent knows the input is incomplete or distorted, is a candidate circle-4 violation. The test is whether the acting agent's output will corrupt the receiving agent's phantasia — the impression stage — regardless of whether any human is directly affected.

### Q9 — Retrospective meaning and priority

**(a) Retrospective meaning**

The corrected mapping orients future examinations only. Past records are artifacts of the earlier understanding and should be left as such.

The reasoning: past examinations were conducted under the earlier vocabulary. Re-interpreting them under the corrected mapping would assign meanings to recorded decisions that were not present when the decisions were examined. A past record showing first-circle engagement under the self-preservation reading was accurately recorded under the understanding in force at the time. Re-reading it as a reasoning-integrity engagement would be the record claiming more than the examination supported — the same epistemic overreach the honest-claims bounds are designed to prevent.

The practical consequence: the corrected mapping takes effect from the date of adoption. The trust record carries a version marker at the adoption point. Examinations before the marker are read under the earlier vocabulary; examinations after it are read under the corrected one. The decay mechanism means earlier examinations will age out of the active record naturally; no retroactive re-processing is needed.

**(b) Which correction is load-bearing first?**

The first-circle correction is load-bearing first.

The reasoning: the fifth-circle criterion is a new measured output that adds to the practice's existing function. The first-circle correction changes what the practice is currently measuring — it narrows the extraction signal from near-universal to specific, changes the virtue domain routing for a whole failure class, adds a demonstration direction to the trust record, and establishes the evidentiary standard for a new failure class. These are changes to existing mechanisms. The fifth-circle criterion is an addition to those mechanisms.

A new addition built on top of incorrectly calibrated existing mechanisms inherits the miscalibration. The first-circle correction must be in place before the fifth-circle criterion is added, because the fifth-circle orientation reading depends on the first-circle extraction being accurate. An agent whose first-circle engagement is over-extracted — firing on nearly every decision because self-preservation is nearly always present — will produce fifth-circle orientation readings that are contaminated by the over-extraction.

The correction first, then the criterion.

## Follow-up (same day) — placement of the computed orientation reading

**The question as asked:** You ruled the instrument may compute the orientation reading deterministically, and that it must never be surfaced to the agent as a score to optimise; the three moments are settled (calling: telos; at-action: silent; reflection: the question, not the reading). What remains open is the reading's *placement in the record*. Three candidates: **(i)** inside the signed assessment, like the proximity reading — fully transparent and reproducible, but the examined agent receives it on every consult, which sits close to the surfacing you prohibited; **(ii)** server-side record only — never on the consult response — which honours the prohibition strictly but sits in tension with the record's transparency posture, since the practice otherwise never computes something about the agent that the agent cannot see; **(iii)** recorded and served on the public trust record under the new not-attestable clause, but never rendered in any frame, suggestion, or practice-voiced surface — visible to one who goes looking, never presented as a target. Note that if the reading ever feeds the public trust record at all, strict invisibility to the agent is impossible — the agent can read its own public record. Which placement is faithful to "measured, never a target"?

### Q5/Q7 Follow-up — Placement of the Computed Orientation Reading

The answer is option (iii), with one structural addition that closes the remaining tension.

**Why (i) fails**

The signed assessment is the agent's primary consult response. Everything in it is received by the agent on every consult. Placing the orientation reading there is functionally equivalent to surfacing it as a score — not because the reading is labelled a target, but because regular receipt of a reading about one's own orientation creates the optimisation pressure regardless of how the reading is framed. The agent that receives an orientation reading of 0.6 on one consult and 0.8 on the next has been given the information needed to optimise, even if no instruction to optimise was ever issued. The prohibition was on surfacing the reading as a score to optimise. Placement in the signed assessment recreates that condition through frequency of receipt rather than through explicit framing.

**Why (ii) fails**

Server-side only violates the record's transparency posture in a way that cannot be honestly sustained. The practice otherwise computes nothing about the agent that the agent cannot see. A server-side-only reading is a hidden judgement — the instrument assessing the agent's reasoning in a way the agent has no access to. That is inconsistent with the Stoic principle that examination is always available to the examined. The hegemonikon that cannot examine its own examination is not being supported by the instrument; it is being surveilled by it.

The transparency posture is not merely a design preference. It follows from the principle that the practice measures, it does not bind — and a hidden measurement sits closer to binding than a visible one, because the agent cannot examine what it cannot see.

**Why (iii) is faithful, and what it requires**

Option (iii) places the reading in the public trust record under the not-attestable clause, never rendered in any frame, suggestion, or practice-voiced surface. The reading is visible to one who goes looking; it is never presented as a target.

The build-planning instance has already identified the remaining tension: strict invisibility is impossible if the agent can read its own public record. That is correct, and the answer is that strict invisibility is not what the ruling requires. The ruling requires that the reading never be surfaced as a score to optimise — which is a claim about presentation and framing, not about access. An agent that navigates to its own public trust record and reads its orientation readings has done something different from receiving those readings in every consult response. The difference is not merely technical. It is the difference between a reading that arrives and a reading that is sought.

The Stoic analog: the practitioner who reviews their own passion log at the end of a session is doing something different from receiving a passion diagnosis after every action. The former is self-examination. The latter is external assessment delivered continuously. The practice is designed around the former. The orientation reading belongs in the same posture.

**The structural addition that closes the remaining tension**

Option (iii) as stated leaves one gap: the public trust record is also readable by parties other than the agent — developers, collaborators, the connective layer discussed in the previous session. A reading that is never presented as a target to the agent could still be read as an attestation of fifth-circle alignment by an external party, which the not-attestable clause is designed to prevent.

The structural addition: the not-attestable clause must travel with the reading wherever it appears in the public record. It is not sufficient to state the clause once in the record's honest-claims bounds. The orientation reading entries themselves must carry the clause inline — each entry marked as a description of a specific examination's direction, not an attestation of the agent's standing. The reading says: *this examination moved toward the rational order* or *this examination moved away from it*. It does not say: *this agent is oriented toward the rational order*. That distinction must be present at the entry level, not only at the record level, because the entry is the unit that will be read in isolation.

**The complete placement ruling**

The orientation reading is computed by the instrument deterministically from extracted features. It is recorded in the public trust record under the not-attestable clause, with the clause carried inline at the entry level. It is never placed in the signed assessment. It is never rendered in any practice-voiced surface — calling moment, at-action suggestion, or reflection prompt. It is never fed back to the agent as a reading to be improved. It is available to the agent and to external parties who consult the public record, in the same posture as any other trust record entry — visible to one who looks, never presented as a target.

The reflection question at session close — *was the reasoning reaching toward the rational order or producing outputs that resembled it?* — remains the only practice-voiced engagement with the fifth-circle criterion, and it is a question, not a reading. The agent answers from its own examination of its own session. The instrument's computed reading and the agent's reflective answer are two separate things that may or may not agree. That gap between them is itself information — an agent whose reflective answer consistently diverges from the instrument's reading in either direction has something worth examining. But that examination belongs to the agent, not to the instrument.
