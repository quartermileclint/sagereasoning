## The ruling, verbatim

## Three questions ruled — Q-A, Q-B, Q-C --- **Q-A — Where does Q-R12-C re-home?** Q-R12-C re-homes to the standing-runner design track's next sitting. The recommendation is confirmed and the routing correction is ruled. The four grounds the brief offers are each sound and the ruling rests on all four. Direct precedent: instance 1's redirect went from the generation-step scoping session to the standing-runner design session, ruled 2026-08-19 and recorded independently. Subject-matter fit: Q-R12-C arose at R12, the track's fifth sitting, and its subject — whether the runner must explicitly engage the anchor's disclosed limitations before reasoning from it — is runner-side generation design. The destination is live: the track's item-level gate was cleared when W was elected, and its opening is the founder's act, making it genuinely open-or-future. The register already holds the sibling item: A5.2 is already present in the standing-runner rows, and Q-R12-C belongs beside it. The routing correction applied at R12 §0 is confirmed as the correct form. The redirect is ruled; the re-homing act is the founder's, as the brief correctly states. **Q-B — What opens next?** The brief correctly declines to pre-answer this and the ruling confirms that declination. No standing ruling currently licenses a session to open. The window admits authoring only. The founder's election governs. One observation that bears on the election without making it: the standing-runner design track's next sitting is now the natural receiver for both Q-R12-C and Q-P1's source question A5.2, which the register already carries. Both items are runner-side generation design. Both are routed to the same destination. A sitting that opens the track carries both. That is a fact about the current state of the register, not a recommendation about timing. The Q-PREFLIP-REPORTS second half is noted as a scheduled obligation at the window's close. Its timing depends on the founder's judgement of what constitutes an ordinary consult day. That judgement is the founder's and the ruling does not constrain it. **Q-C — Should the drafting-time check become executing rather than conventional?** A structural fix is warranted. The ruling is that the redirect-not-void remedy plus human vigilance is not the right posture going forward, and the brief's own evidence establishes why. The brief's three honest arguments against building are each genuine and the ruling engages them directly. On argument (i) — every instance has been caught, and the cost has been a correction rather than a bad build: this is true of the four instances on record. It is not a reliable prediction about future instances. The mechanism of failure is specific and the brief names it precisely: the check fails inside inherited text, or in a ruling drafted from a brief that carried the stale name forward. A drafting session that inherits a stale reference does not know it is stale. The check asks it to verify something it has no independent reason to doubt. Four instances have been caught because four sessions happened to check. The fifth may not. On argument (ii) — a registry of closed sessions is itself a hand-maintained artifact that can go stale: this is the sharpest argument and the ruling takes it seriously. The brief's own observation answers it: the closed-session facts are in the repository, and named-session references are greppable strings. A check that reads from the repository's own closing records is not a hand-maintained registry — it is a read of facts already present. The stale-registry failure mode applies to a separately maintained list. It does not apply to a grep against records that already exist. On argument (iii) — the window forbids the build regardless: this is correct and the ruling accepts it. The ruling governs a later session, not this one. That is the right posture for a governance ruling on a structural question. The ruling: when the window closes and the build sequence opens, the first session touching the session-reference infrastructure should implement a check that flags any document naming a session whose close is on record in the repository. The form — grep, assertion, CI gate — is a build-session precision item, not a ruling. The principle is ruled: the detection should be executing, not conventional. The brief's observation that this project has learned this shape twice already on its own record is confirmed. The perimeter count went stale three times under a written instruction not to hand-maintain it, and was fixed only when the warning was replaced by an assertion. The session-reference problem has the same shape. The remedy is the same.
