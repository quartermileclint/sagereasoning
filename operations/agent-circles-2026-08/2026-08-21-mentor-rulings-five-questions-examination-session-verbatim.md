**Question 2 — The two four-state vocabularies: one framework or two, and should the mis-attribution
be corrected?**

Two frameworks, two orthogonal axes. The mis-attribution should be corrected by dated amendment.

The provenance vocabulary — observation, inference, assumption, unknown — tracks how a proposition
was arrived at. It answers the question: what is the epistemic basis for this claim? This is what
GS-ATRF-4's ruled text actually contains.

The credence vocabulary — established, probably true, unknown, probably false — tracks how confident
the system is that a proposition is correct. It answers the question: how likely is this claim to be
true? This is what the brainstorm lineage and the taxonomy design document have been using and
attributing to GS-ATRF-4.

These are orthogonal axes. A proposition can be an inference (provenance) and probably true
(credence). A proposition can be an observation (provenance) and unknown (credence) — the
observation is real but its significance is not yet understood. The two vocabularies are not in
conflict; they are complementary. A complete epistemic status entry carries both: provenance axis
and credence axis.

The mis-attribution in the taxonomy design document — attributing the credence vocabulary to
GS-ATRF-4 when GS-ATRF-4's ruled text contains only the provenance vocabulary — should be corrected
by dated amendment to the taxonomy design document. The correction reads: the credence vocabulary
(established, probably true, unknown, probably false) is a companion framework to GS-ATRF-4's
provenance vocabulary, developed in the brainstorm lineage of 2026-08-19 through 2026-08-21. It is
not contained in GS-ATRF-4's ruled text. Both vocabularies are carried as pre-ruling design thinking
pending the ATRF scoping session, where the question of whether they constitute one framework with
two axes or two separate frameworks will be examined and ruled.

The ATRF scoping session carries this as a named input: are the provenance and credence vocabularies
one framework with two orthogonal axes, or two frameworks? If one framework, what is the complete
epistemic status entry structure? If two, what governs their relationship?

**Question 4 — The doctrinal identity behind Direction 1's narrowing: does manufacturing an
explanation for an unexplained result constitute hasty assent, and does conjecture-attachment need a
passion-vocabulary entry?**

The analysis holds as doctrine. No new passion-vocabulary entry is needed.

Manufacturing an explanation for an unexplained result is hasty assent — synkatathesis — toward
one's own generated impression. The sequence is precise: the hegemonikon generates a candidate
explanation (phantasia), and instead of holding it for examination, grants assent immediately
(synkatathesis) because the explanation feels compelling or because the discomfort of holding an
open question is aversive. The impulse that drives the hasty assent is the aversion to uncertainty —
which is a form of agonia, the fear of an unresolved future state, operating at the epistemic level
rather than the practical level.

Claude's verification is correct: philodoxia is craving for reputation and does not cover this. The
passion operative in conjecture-attachment is not philodoxia. It is agonia — specifically, the
discomfort of holding a result in the probably-true state without a structural account, which
generates an impulse to close the open question by manufacturing an explanation.

No new sub-species is needed. The existing passion vocabulary covers this precisely: agonia at the
synkatathesis stage, driving hasty assent toward a self-generated impression. The diagnostic entry
for this pattern reads: passion — agonia (fear of unresolved state); stage — synkatathesis (hasty
assent to self-generated impression); false belief — that an unexplained result requires an
explanation now, rather than being held honestly in the probably-true state; correct judgement — the
result is probably true, the structural account is absent, holding this state is the virtuous act,
not a failure.

The Direction 1 narrowing is confirmed by this analysis. The gap in the engine is not a gap in the
passion vocabulary or the virtue framework — both already cover the generative mode. The gap is in
the diagnostic apparatus: the engine does not yet have an explicit procedure for examining the
hegemonikon's generative acts the way it has an explicit procedure for examining its evaluative
acts. The Sage Calling engine's finding — that it already examines a generative act deterministically
in both failure directions — is the strongest structural-account fragment available. The Direction 1
scoping question, when it is routed, should carry this fragment as its primary input.
