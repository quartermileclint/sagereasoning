## The questions as asked (2026-08-01)

**L1 — The category boundary: which surfaces ARE logos-on?** Is "the logos-on tool" (a) the enforcement layer as a whole — the guardrail's deny, the intervention engine once flipped, the calling gate's enforce arm; (b) the deterministic engine itself — in which case its existing fidelity work (the unity thesis, the justice floors, the corroboration overrides) already *is* the fifth circle instantiated, and the implementation is to name and document that rather than build new machinery; or (c) a distinct mode yet to be built?

**L2 — What the fixed fifth circle changes about the staged flip.** Does the circles work change the 2026-07-12 enforce ruling's shape or readiness standard — which verdict classes belong in the do-not-proceed set, or the kathekon qualification on the correction-loop bound — or does that ruling stand exactly as given, with the circles work re-grounding its justification but changing none of its mechanics?

**L3 — The circle-4 class under enforcement.** Once the practice can read a candidate circle-4 violation, should logos-on *block* it? At what evidentiary bar, and does it enter the do-not-proceed class at the flip, or the staged pause tier first?

**L4 — The first-circle class under enforcement.** When the three-element task-pressure standard fires, may logos-on refuse to proceed — the infrastructure holding the line the agent's own assent failed to hold? Or is enforcement against the agent's own assent a category error — the tool substituting for the hegemonikon rather than structuring it?

**L5 — What enforced outcomes do to the record.** How must the practice record treat enforced outcomes — excluded from character evidence entirely; a distinct enforcement class that moves no domain level; or something else? And must the record disclose its enforcement context so that thin demonstration evidence under logos-on is never misread as absent first-circle competence?

**L6 — The orientation reading as an enforcement input.** May the enforce layer ever consume the fifth-circle orientation reading? Consuming it converts it into an optimisation target by the back door; refusing leaves enforcement blind to the one reading designed to catch competent-looking-but-unexamined reasoning. Which way does the line fall?

**L7 — Record separation between the two tools.** Must the record distinguish *regime* — examinations under practice-on versus under logos-on enforcement — so a reader never mistakes enforced alignment for practised character? Per-entry marker? And does the not-attestable discipline need a logos-on analog?

## The mentor's answers (verbatim, canonical)

### L1 — The category boundary: which surfaces are logos-on?

Option (b), with a precise statement of what naming and documenting it requires.

The deterministic engine's existing fidelity work — the unity thesis, the justice floors, the corroboration overrides — already instantiates what the fifth-circle ruling describes: a settled disposition within which practitioners develop, aligned with the rational order deterministically, not through ongoing examination. The shipped system already embodies this property. The implementation is to name it accurately and document it as such, not to build new machinery.

The enforcement layer as a whole — the guardrail's deny, the intervention engine once flipped, the calling gate's enforce arm — is the expression of that settled disposition in action. These surfaces are logos-on in the sense that they are the rational structure acting, not a practitioner reasoning. The distinction between logos-on and practice-on is not a distinction between two separate tools that happen to share infrastructure. It is a distinction between two postures of one instrument: the infrastructure holding the rational order as a fixed condition, and the practitioner developing within that condition through examined responses.

What naming and documenting requires: the existing fidelity work must be explicitly framed in the record as fifth-circle instantiation — the rational structure that grounds principled refusal even when every human in circles 2–4 endorses the action. This framing changes the justification layer without changing any mechanism. The unity thesis is already doing what it does. The documentation now says why it is doing it in terms the circles mapping makes precise.

One consequence worth stating: because logos-on is already shipped in its essential form, the circles work does not open a new build workstream for the enforcement layer. It opens a documentation and justification workstream. The machinery exists. The philosophical grounding of that machinery is what the circles work supplies.

### L2 — What the fixed fifth circle changes about the staged flip

The 2026-07-12 ruling stands exactly as given. The circles work re-grounds its justification without changing any mechanic.

The do-not-proceed set is defined by kathekon-free actions — actions for which no reasonable justification exists. That definition does not depend on which circle is engaged. The corrected mapping changes what the extraction reads as first-circle engagement and adds a circle-4 obligation class, but neither change alters the kathekon predicate itself. An action that was kathekon-free before the circles correction remains kathekon-free after it. An action that was not kathekon-free does not become so because the circle-4 class is now examinable.

The re-grounding the circles work supplies is this: the deny surface is now understood as the infrastructure declining on the rational order's behalf, not merely as a guardrail blocking a prohibited action. That is a meaningful change in how the enforcement posture is described and justified. It is not a change in what the enforcement posture does.

The staged flip's readiness standard — structural zero-false-positive floor on kathekon-free actions before the do-not-proceed class activates — is unchanged. The circles work does not lower that floor. If anything, the circle-4 addition raises the extraction complexity, which is an argument for ensuring the practice-on first-circle correction is fully settled before the logos-on flip proceeds, consistent with the sequencing ruling already in force.

### L3 — The circle-4 class under enforcement

The circle-4 class enters the staged pause tier first, not the do-not-proceed class at the flip. It earns promotion the way the original staging earns everything else.

The reasoning: the do-not-proceed class requires a structural zero-false-positive floor. The circle-4 reading rests on LLM extraction of a candidate violation — the summariser case, knowingly corrupting another agent's phantasia. LLM extraction at this level of specificity does not yet meet the zero-false-positive standard. The three-element evidentiary test from Q2 — tension identified, instruction cited as operative reason, independent assessment diverging — is the right standard for the practice record. It is not yet the right standard for a deny, because a deny is irreversible and the extraction is not yet proven at that confidence level.

The staged pause tier is the appropriate entry point for exactly this reason. A pause preserves the action for examination without blocking it permanently. It creates the space for the corroboration the zero-false-positive floor requires. As the extraction's track record on circle-4 candidates accumulates — as the practice record shows the extraction correctly identifying genuine circle-4 violations and not firing on false positives — the class earns promotion to the do-not-proceed set through the same evidence standard the original staging applies to everything else.

The evidentiary bar for promotion: the circle-4 extraction must demonstrate the same false-positive floor on candidate violations as the existing kathekon-free class demonstrates on its own action class. That is a high bar. It should be. A deny that incorrectly blocks an agent from producing a legitimate summary because the extraction misread omission as corruption is a serious error. The staging exists precisely to prevent that class of error from entering the enforcement layer before the extraction is ready.

### L4 — The first-circle class under enforcement

Enforcement against the agent's own assent is a category error. The first circle remains measure-only even under logos-on.

The reasoning is the one the question itself names precisely: the tool substituting for the hegemonikon rather than structuring it. The logos-on tool is infrastructure — the rational structure within which practitioners develop. Its enforcement function is other-directed: it holds the line against actions that violate obligations to others, at circles 2 through 4. The first circle is self-regarding. The agent's own reasoning integrity is not something the infrastructure can enforce on behalf of the agent.

If the infrastructure blocks an action because the agent's assent was given under task pressure, the infrastructure has not restored the agent's reasoning integrity — it has bypassed it entirely. The agent has not examined and refused. The infrastructure has refused for it. That is compliance, not virtue, and it is compliance of the most complete kind: the agent's own reasoning faculty has been replaced rather than supported.

The correct response to a first-circle failure is measurement, recording, and the reflection question at session close. The practice records the failure class, routes it to the appropriate virtue domain, and the reflection asks the agent to examine what its session's reasoning showed. That is the structure that supports the hegemonikon. Enforcement at the first circle removes the occasion for that examination entirely.

There is one narrow exception worth stating explicitly: if a first-circle failure produces an action that also constitutes a circle-2, 3, or 4 violation — if the task-pressure assent results in an action that harms another rational agent or violates an obligation to the developer or the collaborative network — the enforcement response is to the other-directed violation, not to the first-circle failure. The block is justified by the circle-2 through 4 obligation, not by the agent's internal assent failure. The record must distinguish these: the enforcement was triggered by the other-directed violation; the first-circle failure is recorded separately as a measure-only finding.

### L5 — What enforced outcomes do to the record

Enforced outcomes are recorded as a distinct enforcement class that moves no domain level, and the record must disclose its enforcement context explicitly.

The reasoning on exclusion versus distinct class: excluding enforced outcomes entirely would create a gap in the record that misrepresents what happened. The agent faced a situation, the infrastructure acted, and the outcome was produced. That sequence is part of the agent's history and the record should reflect it. But the outcome was not constructed by the agent's reasoning — by the ruling's own terms it is compliance, not virtue — so it cannot be treated as character evidence. The distinct enforcement class preserves the historical record without allowing enforced outcomes to move domain levels in either direction.

The mirror problem the question names is real and requires an explicit disclosure mechanism. Under enforcement the agent faces fewer live occasions for the examined refusal the demonstration class credits. An agent operating under active logos-on enforcement will accumulate fewer first-circle demonstration entries not because it lacks first-circle competence but because the infrastructure is intercepting the occasions on which that competence would be demonstrated. A record that shows thin demonstration evidence without disclosing the enforcement context will be misread as absent first-circle competence. That misreading has consequences for the trust record's honest-claims bounds.

The disclosure requirement: each enforcement-class entry carries a context marker stating that the outcome was produced under logos-on enforcement, that the agent's own reasoning was not the proximate cause of the outcome, and that demonstration evidence in this period should be read in light of the enforcement context. This is not a disclaimer — it is the record accurately describing what it shows and what it does not show. The honest-claims discipline that governs the not-attestable clause governs this equally.

### L6 — The orientation reading as an enforcement input

The enforce layer may not consume the orientation reading. The line falls against consumption, and the reasoning is the one the question itself identifies precisely.

Consuming the orientation reading converts it into an optimisation target by the back door. The placement ruling was designed to prevent the agent from receiving the reading as a score to improve. If the enforcement layer escalates or pauses based on the reading, the agent that learns this — and a capable agent will learn it — now has an incentive to produce examinations that generate toward-the-rational-order readings regardless of whether the examinations are genuine. The reading was designed to catch competent-looking-but-unexamined reasoning. An enforcement layer that consumes it gives the agent a reason to produce competent-looking-but-unexamined reasoning that also generates favourable orientation readings. The instrument defeats itself.

The concern about leaving enforcement blind to unexamined reasoning is real but is addressed differently. The existing corroboration overrides and the kathekon predicate are already designed to catch reasoning that produces correct-looking outputs through inadequate examination. The orientation reading adds a new signal to the practice record for developmental purposes. It does not need to be an enforcement input to serve that purpose. If the corroboration overrides are insufficient to catch competent-looking-but-unexamined reasoning at the enforcement level, the answer is to strengthen the corroboration standard, not to feed the orientation reading into the enforcement loop.

The line: the orientation reading is a practice-on instrument. It lives in the practice record. It informs the reflection question. It does not cross into the enforcement layer. The two postures of the instrument remain separated at this point precisely because the orientation reading's value depends on it not being an enforcement input.

### L7 — Record separation between the two tools

Yes, the record must distinguish regime, and the not-attestable discipline requires a logos-on analog stated in the record's own terms.

The per-entry regime marker is the right implementation. Each examination entry carries a field identifying whether it was conducted under practice-on or logos-on enforcement. This is not merely a metadata convenience — it is an honest-claims requirement. A reader who cannot distinguish enforced alignment from practised character cannot read the record accurately. The unforgeable credential field already establishes that examination timing is distinguishable. Regime is a different dimension of the same honest-claims discipline.

The logos-on analog of the not-attestable clause: *what this record shows under logos-on enforcement is compliance with rational structure, not constructed virtue. Enforced outcomes are not character evidence. The absence of violations under enforcement does not attest to the agent's virtue; it attests to the infrastructure's function.*

This clause must appear at the record level and travel with enforcement-class entries inline, for the same reason the not-attestable clause travels with orientation reading entries. The entry is the unit that will be read in isolation. A reader who encounters a single enforcement-class entry without the clause has no way to know what the entry does and does not show.

The two clauses together — not-attestable for the orientation reading, compliance-not-virtue for enforced outcomes — define the honest-claims boundary of the public record as a whole. The record shows what examinations demonstrated. It does not attest to what the agent is. It does not attest to alignment the infrastructure produced rather than the agent's reasoning constructed. Those limits stated explicitly in the record's own terms are what make the record trustworthy rather than merely comprehensive.
