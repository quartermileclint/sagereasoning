## The ruling, verbatim

> ## Q-A — Does the amendment apply to the open session, and is a third sitting the right vehicle?

> ## Q-B — Does Q1's doctrinal scope extend to a composed multi-agent client pipeline?
>
> Reading (a) governs, and the pipeline's execution boundary sits at Threshold's handoff — exactly as R9 already designed it for the standing runner.
>
> The reasoning is structural, not merely analogical. Q1's constraint is a doctrinal claim about the causal sequence phantasia → synkatathesis → hormê → praxis. That sequence is not scoped to any particular software boundary. It is a claim about what assent is — the moment at which a rational agent takes an impression as true and acts from it. The constraint forbids collapsing phantasia and synkatathesis into a single automated step, at any scale, because the collapse is what makes the action unfree regardless of the pipeline's shape.
>
> A composed multi-agent client pipeline does not escape this by being multi-agent. The pipeline produces a final output that a human or agent adopts. That adoption is an assent point. If the pipeline's architecture produces that output without a moment at which the adopter can examine the impression before acting on it, the pipeline has collapsed the two stages the ruling already forbids collapsing.
>
> The pipeline's internal room-to-room handoffs are all still phantasia — proposals compounding into a richer proposal. They do not each require an assent point. But the pipeline's own final output to its adopter must be an assent point. Threshold is already designed as that assent point — decision compression before handoff, the final distillation before action. R9's design is correct for the multi-agent pipeline case by the same logic that makes it correct for the standing runner.
>
> No additional ruling is needed to bring the client pipeline within the constraint envelope. It is already there.
>
> The document's concern that the 2026-08-12 amendment names only two scales — the loop-cycle scale and the per-trace scale — and that a third scale might need its own ruling is noted but does not change the answer. The two named scales are illustrations of the doctrine's reach, not an exhaustive enumeration of the scales it covers. The doctrine reaches any scale at which assent occurs, because assent is the object of the constraint, not the scale.
>
> **Q-B ruling: Reading (a) governs. The pipeline's execution boundary sits at Threshold's handoff. No additional ruling is required to bring a composed multi-agent client pipeline within Q1's constraint envelope.**
>

>
> ## Q-C — Under structural environment identity, does C1 transfer? And is practice-exercised attestable?
>
> C1 does not transfer to per-room identities without its own attestation ruling, and the document's observation about the changed risk profile is correct and sharp.
>
> C1 makes the environment tag runner-attested — the runner declares which environment it operated in, and the harness records it as disclosed and unverified. The trust posture is explicit: this is a claim, not a fact.
>
> Under distinct per-room agents, the environment is identity, not attestation. A Forest agent does not attest it was in Forest. It is Forest by construction. The risk changes kind in exactly the way the document's table names: from "did the runner report the room honestly?" to "did this agent exercise that room's practice at all?" The second risk is undetectable by any mechanism now designed, and the output is more trusted than the single runner's attested tag precisely because it is not marked as a claim.
>
> On whether practice-exercised is attestable: the document is right that no candidate mechanism exists that is neither self-report nor outcome measure. Self-report carries C1's own distrust. Outcome measure is excluded by C4 and the weights constraint. The honest answer is that practice-exercised is not attestable in v1 by any mechanism consistent with the current ruling set.
>
> This does not mean the per-room identity architecture is ruled out. It means that if the architecture is ever built, it requires its own attestation ruling that names the changed risk profile explicitly and either accepts it as a disclosed limit or designs a mechanism that addresses it. C1 as stated does not cover it.
>
> **Q-C ruling: C1 does not transfer to per-room identities. The per-room identity architecture requires its own attestation ruling naming the changed risk profile. Practice-exercised is not attestable in v1 by any mechanism consistent with the current ruling set. This is a disclosed limit, not a disqualification.**
>

> ## Q-D — Is R10's disposition on D4's scope adequate?
>
> R10's disposition is adequate and correct. D4 is not modified by this ruling.
>
> D4 rules that the longitudinal environment sequence records the runner's history, not the executing agent's. Under a single runner this is unambiguous — one identity with a varying environment, and the sequence belongs to that identity.
>
> Under N environment-agent identities, each identity's own environment history is a constant. The information a longitudinal view would carry belongs to the pipeline, not to any single environment agent. No pipeline identity exists in any schema or ruling.
>
> R10's disposition — that D4 continues to govern the single-runner case correctly and does not by its own terms answer whose sequence a multi-agent pipeline's longitudinal history is — is the right reading. D4 presupposes one identity with a varying environment. The multi-agent form inverts that presupposition. D4 does not settle the multi-agent case by implication; it simply does not reach it.
>
> The design consequence is: if a multi-agent pipeline architecture is ever built, the longitudinal history question requires its own design session once a pipeline identity is designed. That session will need to answer whose sequence it is, where it is held, and what trust posture it carries. D4 does not pre-answer those questions. Q1c does not pre-answer them either — Q1c establishes the distinct-identities principle for the runner and the executing agent, but a pipeline identity is a third kind of identity that neither ruling addresses.
>
> **Q-D ruling: R10's disposition is adequate. D4 governs the single-runner case. The multi-agent pipeline's longitudinal history is a genuine extension of D4's scope requiring its own design session once a pipeline identity is designed. Neither D4 nor Q1c settles it.**
>

> ## Q-E — Does an environment agent's own reasoning accumulate into a trust state, and if so, per-agent or shared?
>
> Environment agents should not accumulate their own trust records in v1. If the architecture is ever built beyond v1, the question of per-agent versus shared accumulation requires its own ruling at that time.
>
> The reasoning is as follows. The trust state is held in the harness. B4 establishes that the runner reads the executing agent's examined state from the harness at cycle start and does not hold it independently. The harness is the accumulation point. An environment agent that accumulated its own trust record independently of the harness would be holding state the harness does not see — which means the harness's read of the examined state at cycle start would be incomplete. That incompleteness is not a minor implementation detail. It is a honesty defect in the same class as Finding B in the P1 ruling: the harness would give an answer to a question it did not fully put.
>
> The document's narrowed question — whether a shared operator identity accumulates one record across every room it visits, or each per-room identity accumulates its own — is the right question for a future session. The answer in v1 is neither, because the trust accumulation architecture for environment agents is not designed and should not be assumed. The harness holds the executing agent's trust state. Environment agents read it. They do not write to it independently.
>
> If the architecture is ever built and the trust accumulation question becomes live, the shared-identity route and the per-agent route each have genuine costs the document names correctly. The shared-identity route risks losing the per-room signal — one record across all rooms flattens the information. The per-agent route raises the credentialing cost and the write-side schema question (the UNIQUE constraint M7 names). Neither is pre-answered here. Both are named inputs for the future session.
>
> **Q-E ruling: Environment agents do not accumulate their own trust records in v1. Trust accumulation for environment agents is not designed and should not be assumed. The per-agent versus shared question is a named input for a future session, not a v1 design question.**
>

> ## Q-F — Should Attic and Cellar heuristics be elected?
>
> Not by this ruling, and not as a consequence of the twelve-environment amendment.
>
> The document is right that the Cellar case cannot arise — Cellar has no heuristic, and a heuristic for it is an unelected follow-on per R9 §16.7. The amendment's use of a Cellar example to illustrate the load-bearing joint is noted as suggestive — the case that best shows why the interface matters lives in a room the design does not occupy.
>
> The question of whether heuristics for Attic and Cellar should be elected is a scope expansion of the generation step. It is a legitimate design question. It is not a question this ruling settles. If the standing-runner design session finds that the twelve-environment architecture requires Attic and Cellar to be generative — not merely named — then electing heuristics for them is a natural next step. That election is a founder-walked act, not a design-session act, and it requires its own scoping session under the 2026-08-19 forward-reservation principle.
>
> The ruling notes one implication the document does not name: if Attic and Cellar remain empty, the twelve-environment architecture is not fully operational in v1. Seven of twelve rooms can host a live candidate-production heuristic at most (per M3), and fewer than that today. The architecture's composability claim is prospective, not current. That is not a reason to reject the architecture — it is a reason to carry the gap explicitly in the session's output rather than presenting the twelve-room architecture as immediately operational.
>
> **Q-F ruling: Attic and Cellar heuristics are not elected by this ruling. The election is a founder-walked act requiring its own scoping session. The session carries the gap explicitly: the twelve-environment architecture is prospective in v1, not immediately operational.**
