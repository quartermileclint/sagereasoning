## 4. Q-C — SHOULD THE DRAFTING-TIME CHECK BECOME EXECUTING RATHER THAN CONVENTIONAL?

**This is the question this brief exists for.** §1 shows a rule that is correct, ruled, and
repeatedly ineffective.

**The mechanism of the failure is specific.** PR20's amendment asks a *drafting session* to verify a
target session's status. That is an instruction in prose, discharged by whoever remembers it. **It
has no executing form**, so it fails exactly where instances 2–4 failed: inside inherited text, or in
a ruling drafted from a brief that carried the stale name forward.

**This project has learned this shape twice already, on its own record**, and both times the lesson
was the same: *"a count written into prose is a count that will go stale, and an instruction not to
hand-maintain it does not prevent that"*, and *"where a count matters, prefer an executing check over
a written instruction."* The perimeter count carried its own emphatic warning not to hand-maintain
it, went stale three times anyway, **and was fixed only when the warning was replaced by an
assertion.**

**A mechanical check appears available**, stated as an observation, not a proposal to build: the
closed-session facts are in the repository (each session's closing ruling and decision-log entry), and
named-session references are greppable strings. A check could flag any document naming a session
whose close is on record.

**The question: is a structural fix warranted here, or is the ruled redirect-not-void remedy plus
human vigilance the right posture, accepting that instances will keep being caught late?**

**Three honest arguments against building anything**, offered so the question is not loaded: (i) every
instance *has* been caught, and the cost has been a correction rather than a bad build; (ii) a
registry of closed sessions is itself a hand-maintained artifact that can go stale — the very failure
mode it addresses, one level up; (iii) **the window forbids the build regardless**, so a ruling here
governs a later session, not this one.

**Nothing is proposed, designed or scoped by this brief.** Q-C is put as a question of governance
posture.
