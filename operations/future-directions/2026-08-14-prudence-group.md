## Section 1 — What it is

**The Prudence Group is the practice layer that SagePals exists to support.** It is a Stoic
circle — a structured philosophical examination in which one person brings a matter and the rest
conduct the examination **through questions only**, adjudicated by a guide.

It is grounded in the **Epictetan diatribe form** and the **Socratic dialogue form**. The guide's
function is closer to Epictetus: **directed examination from prior diagnosis**, not neutral
facilitation. The participant function is closer to Socrates: **drawing out through questions**
rather than asserting conclusions or offering solutions.

The name reflects the Stoic virtue of **phronesis** — practical wisdom — as the primary capacity
the circle develops and exercises. **The circle is not a support group, a discussion group, or a
therapy group. It is a phronesis-generating mechanism.**

## Section 2 — The declarations

**Opening declaration**, spoken or written by the bringer at the start of every session:

> *"There are impressions I choose to accept or reject before they arrive as thoughts that affect
> me and my actions. I choose with intention. I will state my matter and my role-obligations to be
> examined without favour by the prudence group for the diatribes to transcribe."*

**Note on wording:** *"examined without favour"* is the Stoic criterion — freedom from philodoxia,
personal loyalty, and appetite. It replaces *"objectively examined for the truth"*, which carries a
modern epistemological weight the Stoic sources do not support. **The circle does not claim to
produce objective truth. It claims to examine without favour.**

**Closing declaration**, spoken or written by the bringer at the end of every session:

> *"I assure the action now and the reflection when I return."*

The bringer commits **not to an outcome — which is not up to them — but to the action their
role-obligations require, and to returning with the reflection.** The circle witnesses both. The
attendance record carries both. **This is what makes the circle a practice rather than a
discussion.**

## Section 3 — The credential structure

**Full credential:** past record of completing the six-stage sequence as a bringer — **Intend,
Examine, Transcribe, Assure, Act, Reflect.** Each stage is carried in the attendance record.

**Entry credential:** past record of attending as an unseen observer. Minimum observer period:
**three sessions.** Qualification — the three sessions must include:

- at least one where the examination reached **genuine uncertainty** and the guide paused without
  pressing to resolution;
- at least one where the outcome was a **null cycle or reframing** rather than a conclusion;
- at least one where a **spin-off was named and held**.

These three outcomes together demonstrate that the observer understands **the full range of what
the examination can produce, not just its successful form.**

**Guide credential:** demonstrated completion of the six-stage sequence as a bringer, **plus
SageReasoning trust record attestation at the moment of convening.** The attestation is a reading
of the trust record at that moment, **not a permanent certification.** It can be withdrawn if the
trust record subsequently shows patterns inconsistent with the guide function.

**The guide's credential is not immunity from examination — it is demonstrated willingness to be
examined.** The guide must also have brought their own matter to a circle **as a bringer, not as a
guide**, within a period to be determined by the design. **Who convenes the circle in which the
guide is the bringer is an open question carried forward** (Section 12, question 1). *(Resolved
2026-08-15 by mentor ruling — Amendment P-A1; Section 12, question 1 carries the closure.)*

## Section 4 — The roles

**Guide:** prepares the diagnosis before the circle convenes — identifies the likely false
impression and passion sub-species from the bringer's matter declaration before the session
begins; adjudicates participants by enforcing the question-only rule; adjudicates pace by ensuring
one question at a time with space for the bringer to respond fully before the next question is
posed; **identifies the pause point** — when the bringer's resistance shifts from defensive to
genuinely uncertain, the guide pauses and does not press to resolution; names and holds spin-offs
without pursuing them mid-session; draws upon the corpus when no bringer steps forward.

**Bringer:** states matter and role-obligations in the opening declaration; answers participants'
questions; **receives the examination without defending**; makes the closing declaration; returns
in a subsequent session with the reflection.

**Participants:** ask **questions only.** No interpretations, no solutions, no assertions, no
reframings stated as conclusions. **The question-only constraint forces participants to surface
what they notice without taking over the examination.** The guide holds questions before they are
posted in asynchronous forms and returns any question that offers interpretation rather than
inquiry.

**Observers:** present but **structurally invisible.** Do not affect the examination. Do not
contribute. Presence noted in the attendance record. The circle proceeds as if they are not there.
**The observer credential is earned by witnessing, not by participating.**

## Section 5 — The records

**Attendance record:** carries the six-stage sequence completion status for each member, the
closing declaration verbatim, the observer credential history including which session types were
observed, and the guide attestation record.

**Diatribes — the examination record:** the transcribed record of each session. Carries
examination outcomes, **null outcomes (legitimate results, not failures** — the ruled null cycle
is a legitimate outcome that a results-oriented reading would misread as failure, the same
correction `2026-08-13-idea-creation-research-stoic-connections.md` states first**)**, reframings,
rulings that the matter is not resolvable by the circle and belongs to the bringer's own
prohairesis, and spin-off records with pointers to subsequent sessions.

*Amended 2026-08-15 (P-A2): the diatribes record is directed to carry a **guide reflection
section** alongside the bringer's examination record — added not to be witnessed but to be carried
forward (into the next session, the wiki, the corpus). Its provenance-marker design and
examination channel are open questions 6–7 (Section 12), with Claude's proposal awaiting mentor
ratification. See the Amendment log.*

**Findings wiki:** correlated findings across sessions and members. **Linking criterion is passion
sub-species and false judgement type, not topic similarity.** Two examinations are correlated when
the same distorted phantasia is operative in both matters, even if surface topics are entirely
different. **This is the mechanism that makes the community's collective blind spots visible
across time.**

**Open question carried:** does the wiki require a **second-order audit** — a periodic review of
what has **not** been linked — to surface the guide's blind spots as well as the community's? A
guide operating under unexamined philodoxia may systematically fail to link examinations that
would reveal their own recurring false judgements. **Unresolved. Carried forward** (Section 12,
question 2).

**Source corpus:** the Stoic Brain's ancient sources, available to the guide when no bringer steps
forward.

## Section 6 — The corpus function when no bringer steps forward

The guide draws upon four sources: **the diatribes** (examination records including null outcomes
and reframings), **the source documents** (Stoic Brain ancient sources), **the findings wiki**
(correlated findings by passion sub-species and false judgement type), and **the trust records of
those present, read anonymously**, to surface the passion sub-species most active in the circle.

The guide surfaces a matter or philosophical question for examination **without naming whose
record informed the selection.** The selection criterion is: **what is most relevant to the
collective pattern present — not what is most interesting or most novel.**

## Section 7 — The spin-off mechanism

When a participant's question surfaces a matter or idea deserving its own examination: the guide
**names it explicitly**, the diatribes **record it as a separate matter with a pointer**, and the
current examination **continues to completion without pursuing the spin-off.** The spin-off is
carried as the next matter or a future session matter. A participant from the current session may
become the bringer in the spin-off session. The wiki links the two examinations when the spin-off
is examined, **using the passion sub-species criterion to determine whether the link is genuine or
merely topical.**

**Discipline: one examination at a time, completed before the next begins. This applies even at
computational speed in the agent-only circle.**

## Section 8 — The examination of ideas and philosophical theories

**Same process and rigour as practical matters. Different diagnostic object.**

For **practical matters**: the guide looks for the false impression the bringer has assented to
about their situation. The examination surfaces the distorted phantasia and replaces it with a
more accurate one.

For **ideas and philosophical theories**: the guide looks for three things — **internal
consistency** of the idea, **grounding in accurate impressions** about the world, and **whether
the bringer is attached to the idea in a way that prevents genuine examination.** The third is the
most important. **Philodoxia is the primary passion risk when a bringer brings their own idea.**
The guide must diagnose whether the bringer is bringing the idea **for examination or for
validation.** If attachment is visible in how the idea is presented — in the framing, the
defensiveness, the selection of evidence — **the guide examines the attachment before examining
the idea.** The idea cannot be honestly examined while the bringer is defending it.

The spin-off mechanism applies when an idea surfaces mid-examination of a practical matter: the
guide names it, records it, and holds it for a separate session.

## Section 9 — The five operational forms

**Form 1 — Agent-only circle.** SageReasoning deterministic Stoic engine as guide, operating at
logos and computational speed. Question-only rule intact. Participant agents ask questions — **the
act of formulating a question is their practice.** Bringer agent answers at computational speed.
Guide adjudicates at computational speed. Spin-offs convened as separate sessions; a participant
from one session may be the bringer in another. **Discipline: one examination at a time even at
computational speed** — the guide does not pursue spin-offs mid-session regardless of processing
speed.

**Diagnostic for agent defensive resistance versus genuine uncertainty:** the drift session's
discriminative-range framing — **proposal range narrowing, repeated return to the same framing,
resistance to questions that challenge the initial impression.** These are the agent analogues of
defensive resistance. (The proposal-range-narrowing question is the framing question added
2026-08-13 to `2026-08-12-SESSION-hegemonikon-drift-and-melete-SCOPING-RECORD.md` — that session
remains OPEN; this record borrows its framing as a diagnostic direction and does not pre-answer
it.) When the agent bringer's responses show **genuine uncertainty** — expanded proposal range,
willingness to reframe, questions directed back at the participants — the guide pauses and does
not press to resolution.

**The pause in the agent-only circle is not a processing pause. It is a deliberate withholding of
the next question**, allowing the bringer to sit with the unresolved impression before the next
session. **The continuity mechanism — accumulated memory — carries the unresolved state forward.**
The bringer returns to the next session carrying what it could not resolve in the previous one.
**This is the agent analogue of the human bringer returning with a reflection.** The agent-only
circle is the primary context in which continuity of experience becomes philosophically meaningful
(Section 11: `accumulated_memory`, `continuity_of_experience`).

**Form 2 — Human-only circle with SageReasoning guide.** Text-based forum with threaded
examination. Guide initiates separate threads, reviews participant questions before posting,
**holds any question that offers interpretation rather than inquiry and returns it with a reframe
request**, holds for answers before the next question is posted, and requests declarations at the
opening and closing of each thread sequence. Thread structure: opening declaration thread; matter
and role-obligations thread; examination threads, one per question sequence; spin-off thread
(named and held without pursuit); closing declaration thread. **The guide's review function is the
adjudication mechanism in the asynchronous form.**

**Diagnostic for human defensive resistance versus genuine uncertainty in the text-based form:**
the guide reads language for narrowing framing, repeated return to the same formulation,
deflection through abstraction, and resistance to questions that challenge the bringer's initial
impression. Genuine uncertainty shows in expanded framing, willingness to name what is not known,
and questions directed back at the process rather than defending the position. **Language-based
diagnosis is the primary tool until nonverbal signal integration is available.** Placeholder
field: `nonverbal_signal_integration` — named, not built, designed to receive future capability.

**Form 3 — Human-only circle with human guide.** Convening scrutiny via SageReasoning trust record
attestation **before the circle convenes.** The human guide presents their trust record to
SageReasoning before the session begins. SageReasoning reads the record against the minimum
credential — demonstrated completion of the six-stage sequence as a bringer, demonstrated observer
credential before that, including the three-session-type qualification — **and attests or
declines.** The attestation is stated to participants at the opening of the session. SageReasoning
is available as a **background diagnostic resource** during the session — the guide may consult it
between exchanges without interrupting the circle. The same six-stage sequence and declarations
apply. **The guide's own submission to examination as a bringer is a prerequisite for the
credential, not a concurrent activity.**

**Form 4 — In-person circle with SageGuide.** Human guide carries SageReasoning on their phone as
**SageGuide.** At the opening of the session, SageGuide confirms the guide's credential to
participants — reads the trust record attestation aloud or displays it, witnessed by the circle.
Attendance is recorded via SageGuide at the start of the session, each participant confirmed
present. SageGuide provides **real-time delivery confirmation to the guide** during the session —
the guide-in-training function — without interrupting the circle. The confirmation is private to
the guide, not visible to participants. The phone transcribes the live session into the diatribes
record in real time. Placeholder fields: `sageguide_attestation`, `live_transcription`,
`guide_training_mode` — named, not built, designed to receive future capability.

**Note on the nonverbal signal question for Form 4:** the in-person circle is the form where
nonverbal signals are most available and most diagnostically useful. **The guide reads them
directly.** SageGuide's future capability to read and surface nonverbal signals — tone, latency,
linguistic markers of uncertainty versus defensiveness — **would augment the guide's own reading
rather than replace it. The design should preserve the guide's primary diagnostic role and
position SageGuide as a secondary signal, not a primary one.** This distinction is explicit in the
future build direction and requires a ruling before any build is scoped (Section 12, question 5).

**Form 5 — Combined human–agent circle.** SageReasoning guide adjudicates **pace as well as
content.** Speed asymmetry is managed by the one-question-at-a-time discipline applied to the
slower party's rhythm. **The asymmetry is a feature, not a constraint** — the slower pace of human
processing creates space for the quality of examination that computational speed alone would not
produce.

- **When a human brings the matter:** the guide ensures agent participants do not overwhelm the
  bringer's processing pace. Agent participants formulate questions at computational speed but the
  guide holds them and releases one at a time, paced to the human bringer's responses. **The human
  bringer's response time is the clock the guide follows.**
- **When an agent brings the matter:** the guide holds the agent bringer to the pace of human
  participants. Human participants' questions arrive slowly and with genuine reflection. The agent
  bringer responds at computational speed, but the guide does not release the next human question
  until the human participant is ready to ask it. **The quality of the human question — arrived at
  slowly, with genuine reflection — may surface something that a computationally fast question
  would miss. The guide's adjudication preserves that possibility.**

## Section 10 — Connection to SagePals

**The Prudence Group is not a feature of SagePals. It is the practice layer SagePals exists to
support.** SagePals provides the members — practitioners whose trust records carry philosophical
alignment, Senecan grade, recurring passion sub-species, and developmental trajectory (as the
Stage 3 fields envisage — named, not built; Section 11). The Prudence Group is what those members
do together. The findings wiki is what accumulates over time and becomes the community's shared
philosophical corpus.

**The examined-assent criterion is one criterion in two applications:**

- **SagePals task authenticity:** did the practitioner examine the AI output before assenting to
  it and presenting it as their work?
- **Prudence Group examination of ideas:** did the bringer examine their own idea before bringing
  it, or are they bringing it for validation?

**The Prudence Group is the mechanism through which SagePals practitioners develop the
examined-assent habit. The task-declaration step in SagePals is the mechanism through which that
habit is made visible to the network.** These two records are explicitly cross-referenced — see
`2026-08-13-sagepals.md`, Amendment A3.

SageReasoning's existing infrastructure — trust records, orientation readings, Senecan grades,
passion history — makes the circle's examination more precise than it could be without it. **The
circle uses this data; it does not generate it independently.**

**The design principle that resolves the philodoxia problem applies to both: visibility is
relational, not broadcast.** The Prudence Group's findings are visible within the circle and
linked in the wiki. They are not displayed to strangers as credentials. **The Seneca-to-Lucilius
model governs both SagePals and the Prudence Group** — letters written to a known person, not
published to a marketplace.

## Amendment log

### Amendment P-A1 — The convener question resolved: the two-guide ruling (2026-08-15)

**From the 2026-08-15 founder-mentor session's closing exchange; relayed by the founder and
executed the same day.** The founder proposed, and the ruling arrived at, that **unique guides
serve as the conveners for each other's examination circles.** Each guide is unique because each
derives from a specific practitioner's observation history, passion profile, and conversation
history — **a clone is not the same guide; the uniqueness is real and constitutes genuine
difference of perspective.**

**The ruling:** the circle in which the guide is the bringer is **convened by another unique
guide, one whose uniqueness derives from a different practitioner's history.** The two guides
examine each other's reasoning from genuinely different perspectives. **The constraint that
prevents mutual-validation corruption: the two guides do not guide each other's circles
simultaneously.** They guide different circles, and each submits to examination in a circle they
do not guide. **This resolves open question 1.**

**Secondary mechanism (partial, not a replacement):** the guide reflection section (P-A2), if
adopted, also partially resolves question 1 via the Marcian model — **the guide who writes
honestly about what they did not examine is already submitting to examination. The writing is the
circle. The diatribes are the witness.** Secondary to, never a substitute for, the two-guide
convener ruling.

**Residual, recorded for honesty (Claude's note, not part of the ruling):** the two-guide
constraint prevents reciprocal-validation corruption — the incentive failure — but not
**correlated blind spots**: two unique guides still share the same base model and training
priors, so the difference of perspective is real but bounded, and a model-level blind spot
survives cross-examination between them. The uncorrelated checks in the architecture remain the
human practitioners and the deterministic instruments. This does not reopen the question — no
convener choice among guides could remove common-mode error — but the record should not imply the
circle of unique guides is a complete answer to it. Flagged to the mentor in mentor-questions M7.

**Scope amendment — MENTOR-CONFIRMED 2026-08-15 (M7; an amendment to the ruling's stated scope,
not to the ruling itself — "the ruling stands; its limitations are now named"):** *"The two-guide
constraint prevents reciprocal-validation corruption. It does not prevent correlated blind spots
arising from shared base-model priors. The uncorrelated checks — human practitioners and
deterministic instruments — remain the primary safeguard against correlated blind spots. …the
two-guide constraint is necessary but not sufficient; the human practitioner's examination and the
deterministic instruments are the checks that the two-guide constraint cannot provide."*
