## §1 What the mentor said

The observation:

> Innovation in the animal kingdom originates from primal tension — hunger, competitive pressure,
> reproductive drive — that forces departure from fixed patterns. **Behavioural flexibility, the
> eleventh trait in the research, is not generated by contentment. It is generated by friction between
> what the organism wants and what the environment currently provides.**

The Stoic reconciliation:

> Chrysippus defined the impulse (**horme**) as the starting point of action. **The sage does not lack
> impulse — the sage has impulse that is fully examined and proportionate.** The difference between
> the sage and the person in passion is not that the sage feels no drive but that **the sage's drive
> is not excessive and is directed at genuine goods.**

The consequence for agents:

> an agent operating **only from virtue-aligned heuristics** — only from what is already known to be
> good — **has no mechanism for the kind of departure from fixed patterns that produces genuine
> novelty.** The IDEA loop's heuristic 7, friction detection, is the closest current mechanism …
> **That is the functional analogue of hunger driving tool use.**

And the resolution:

> The Stoic-compatible version of this is not greed or laziness — it is **the examined recognition of
> a gap between what is and what virtue requires. That gap is the engine of Stoic progress.** The
> **prokoptôn** — the one making progress — is not content with current virtue. They are **in
> productive tension with the distance between their current state and the sage ideal.**
>
> … the generation-step should include a mechanism that explicitly asks: **where does the current
> system fall short of what virtue requires? Not what is broken technically, but what is the gap
> between current capability and the examined ideal?**

## §2 Mechanism facts (PR20)

### §2.1 `horme` is committed, with the mentor's exact distinction already encoded

`stoic-brain.ts:584-589`, `CAUSAL_SEQUENCE` stage 3:

> `horme` / Impulse — *"If assent is given, an impulse toward or away from something arises."*
> **Failure mode:** *"Excessive impulse (pathos) — impulse that overshoots reason."*

The corpus already says what the mentor says: the defect is **excess**, not the impulse. The framing
document can cite this rather than argue it. Note also that `horme` sits *after* `synkatathesis` in
the sequence — impulse follows assent — which is why S1's "examination is only possible if the impulse
is present and visible" is structurally right: suppressing the impulse before assent removes the thing
assent is given or withheld *to*.

### §2.2 The prokoptôn structure is committed, and it is three-graded

`stoic-brain.ts:471-506`, `SENECAN_GRADES` — three grades of progress, **all short of the sage**,
each defined by what remains undone:

- Grade 3, *Beginning the Path* — *"Where most philosophical practitioners find themselves … Progress
  is real but uneven."*
- Grade 2, *Overcoming the Worst* — *"Major passions checked but minor ones still operative."*
- Grade 1, *Approaching Wisdom* — *"Most passions overcome but not all … Can still relapse under
  extreme testing."*

**Every grade is defined by a remaining gap.** The mentor's *"productive tension with the distance
between their current state and the sage ideal"* is not an addition to this structure — it is a
description of it. And `PROGRESS_DIMENSIONS` (`:514`) is the committed four-dimension measurement of
movement across it.

### §2.3 The mechanism the mentor describes already exists — as a shipped human tool

This is the most useful finding in this document.

**`/sage-compass`** (live since 2026-07-15) is the mentor's own #14 design: *the sage as a **compass
bearing**, not an aspiration.* Its shape, from the live route
(`website/src/app/api/mentor/sage-compass/route.ts`):

1. name the decision and the action being considered;
2. identify which virtue is **primarily** engaged;
3. write **what that virtue's complete expression would look like here** — the bearing;
4. name the **distance** between that expression and the action.

And its binding constraint, verbatim in the route's own comments:

> ***NOT A VERDICT (mentor #14, binding).*** This is CAPTURE: the practitioner chooses it for
> themselves. Nothing in this route computes, scores, ranks, grades, or classifies it.

**That is S2's "gap between what is and what virtue requires", already built, already live, already
ruled — for humans.** The mentor's proposed generation-step question — *"where does the current system
fall short of what virtue requires?"* — is the same question with the system in the practitioner's
place.

Two consequences for the framing document:

- **It should say so.** The coherence claim (a project whose agent mechanism descends from the same
  doctrine as its human tool) is exactly what `/logos` was built to make visible, and this is a live
  instance of it.
- **It should carry the constraint across.** `/sage-compass`'s binding rule is that the distance is a
  **developmental orientation, never a verdict**. If the agent-side mechanism (S3) computes a gap that
  then *scores* something, it violates the same constraint in a different medium. **Named here,
  enforced in S3 §5.**

### §2.4 Heuristic 7's role — stated precisely, because S6 depends on the same facts

`GenerationHeuristic` (`idea-loop-types.ts:81-88`) has exactly seven values;
`friction_detection` is one. Per the ruled generation design, friction detection *"identifies points
where the existing system resists or fails"* — the run's own definition being any routine-task step
that *"takes longer than expected, produces an unsatisfying result, or requires workaround
behaviour."*

**The mentor's own framing is careful and should not be flattened:** heuristic 7 is *"the closest
current mechanism"* to the functional analogue of hunger — **not** that it already is the mechanism.
The distinction matters because the gap the mentor actually wants is *"not what is broken technically,
but … the gap between current capability and the examined ideal."* Friction detection reads
**technical** resistance (slow steps, workarounds, unsatisfying results). The proposed mechanism reads
a **normative** gap (falling short of what virtue requires). **These are different questions**, and the
document must not let "friction detection is the closest thing" collapse into "friction detection is
it."

This is also the point where S2 and S6 must agree: **S6's hypothesis (friction as primary generator)
and S2's proposed mechanism (the virtue-gap question) are not the same proposal.** One promotes an
existing technical-friction channel; the other adds a normative-gap question. They could both be
adopted, or either alone. Conflating them would let evidence for one be read as evidence for the
other. **Cross-referenced in S6 §2.1 and stated here.**
